# Operational Concept for the Greenvale Metro Communications-Based Train Control System

## 1. Operational Context and Control Philosophy

Greenvale Metro is operated as a high-frequency urban railway with mixed peak, off-peak, and engineering-hour traffic patterns across underground and surface sections. The Communications-Based Train Control system is the primary means of supervising train separation, movement authority, and service regulation on the controlled portions of the railway. The operating environment assumes continuous interaction between onboard equipment, wayside control equipment, station interfaces, and the central control room. The principal service objective is to maintain safe train movements while achieving the advertised headway, predictable dwell performance, and rapid recovery from routine disturbances. The control philosophy assigns strategic regulation to the control room and tactical protection to safety-related train and wayside functions. The Line Traffic Controller is responsible for monitoring service performance, authorizing planned operational changes, coordinating incident response, and directing recovery actions when automatic regulation is insufficient. The Revenue Train Operator remains responsible for train readiness, cab procedures, passenger-facing actions, and compliance with instructions issued through approved operational channels. Metro Operations Control System is assigned the alias Lantern for operator shorthand in this document. Metro Operations Control System provides the control room with consolidated train positions, service regulation functions, alarms, and operator command interfaces. North District Zone Controller and South District Zone Controller calculate safe movement authority limits using train location reports, track occupancy data, and the state of controlled field equipment. East Junction Interlocking and West Junction Interlocking maintain vital control of points and signals at the principal junction areas and only accept commands that satisfy their configured safety rules. Atlas Onboard Controller supervises automatic train protection functions on each equipped train and uses data from Dual Channel Odometer to maintain a validated estimate of train position and speed. Train Radio Modem connects each train to the Wayside Radio Access Network, which provides the radio bearer for train-to-wayside control communications along the alignment. Safety Data Communication Network carries safety-related messages between approved control components and is engineered for deterministic performance, segregation, and monitored availability. Service Timetable Server supplies planned service data used by central regulation functions, while Passenger Information Gateway distributes approved service messages to customer information systems. Voice Dispatch Console provides the fallback voice coordination path used by the Line Traffic Controller, Revenue Train Operator, and field staff when procedural confirmation is required. Platform Screen Door Controller exchanges platform interface indications with the train control environment so that train movement and door operation remain coordinated at fitted stations. During normal operation, automatic supervision is expected to regulate headways, enforce safe separation, and present the Line Traffic Controller with exceptions rather than routine control steps. During degraded operation, the system is operated to a defined safe state, with reduced automation, additional human confirmation, and conservative movement arrangements applied according to the operating rulebook. Loss of train location confidence, loss of communications, or disagreement between vital functions results in protective restriction of movement until the condition is resolved or an approved degraded procedure is invoked. Signal Maintenance Engineer is assigned the alias Maple for maintenance shorthand in this document. Signal Maintenance Engineer uses Maintenance Diagnostic Server to review equipment health, correlate alarms, and support restoration without bypassing required operational authority. Cyber Security Officer oversees access controls, key-management processes supported by Cryptographic Key Management Appliance, and the response to security events that could affect train control availability or integrity. Metro Operations Control System sends the Crew Login Token Bundle as part of the controlled start-of-duty process.

## 2. Onboard CBTC Functions and Train Interfaces

After successful operator authentication, Atlas Onboard Controller assumes the active train control role when the train is energized and train integrity indications are valid. The Atlas Onboard Controller is referred to in some installation drawings as Kestrel, but this document uses Atlas Onboard Controller as the canonical name. Atlas Onboard Controller supervises movement authority processing, onboard speed enforcement, door enable logic, and train interface commands required for CBTC operation. The controller maintains a safety-critical state machine that separates initialization, protected manual operation, automatic train operation, fallback operation, and cutout conditions. Movement authority data received from North District Zone Controller and South District Zone Controller is checked for sequence, applicability, and consistency before it is applied onboard. Interlocking indications associated with East Junction Interlocking and West Junction Interlocking are treated as wayside state inputs conveyed through established safety channels. Dual Channel Odometer provides independent speed and distance measurements derived from axle rotation, inertial sensing, and configured calibration parameters. Atlas Onboard Controller compares the two odometry channels continuously and degrades the operating mode when the measured divergence exceeds the permitted threshold. When odometry confidence is reduced, the onboard functions increase position uncertainty and request operating actions that preserve separation from other trains. The trainborne interface to propulsion and braking equipment converts safety commands into vehicle-compatible requests while preserving electrical isolation from non-vital circuits. Brake assurance functions verify service and emergency brake availability by monitoring brake feedback, pneumatic pressure indications, and commanded deceleration response. During each enforcement cycle, Atlas Onboard Controller recalculates the braking demand needed to keep the train within the authorized movement limits. Atlas Onboard Controller produces the Brake Curve Checksum Log after validating the active enforcement data set. Train Radio Modem provides the onboard data radio function and maintains CBTC sessions over Wayside Radio Access Network coverage areas. The radio subsystem reports link quality, registration state, and session continuity information to Atlas Onboard Controller for use in mode management. Safety Data Communication Network provides the protected communications transport used by train control messages that require integrity, authentication, and bounded latency. Safety Data Communication Network sends the Clock Drift Sample Report at defined synchronization intervals. Cryptographic Key Management Appliance supports the security material used by onboard communications without changing the safety logic executed on the train. The operator interface presents movement authority limits, permitted speed, train location confidence, radio condition, and required operator actions to Revenue Train Operator. Acknowledgements entered by Revenue Train Operator are passed to Atlas Onboard Controller through a monitored input path that rejects stale or inconsistent commands. Voice Dispatch Console remains operationally separate from the vital CBTC data path, but it provides a means for voice coordination during degraded service. Platform Screen Door Controller exchanges door enable and closed-and-locked indications with the train interface when the train is correctly berthed at a platform. Passenger Information Gateway receives non-vital train position and service state data through an interface that prevents passenger systems from influencing safety decisions. Service Timetable Server provides schedule context to Metro Operations Control System and related non-vital functions, while onboard enforcement remains based on safety authority and train state. The Maintenance Diagnostic Server is referred to in some maintenance work instructions as Quarry, but this document uses Maintenance Diagnostic Server as the canonical name. Maintenance Diagnostic Server collects non-vital health indications from trainborne equipment so that Signal Maintenance Engineer can review faults without connecting directly to vital processing functions.

## 3. Wayside Communications and Zone Supervision

Wayside communications for Greenvale Metro are organized around continuous train radio coverage and a separated safety data backbone that carries vital movement authority traffic. The Wayside Radio Access Network is sometimes called Saffron in radio survey records, but this document uses Wayside Radio Access Network as the canonical name. The Wayside Radio Access Network provides overlapping cell coverage through stations, tunnels, crossovers, and depot approach tracks so that the Train Radio Modem can maintain a stable session during normal operation. Radio sites are engineered with sector overlap at station throats and interlocking approaches because these locations combine frequent braking, switching, and train-to-wayside message activity. The Train Radio Modem maintains the active bearer used by the Atlas Onboard Controller and reports radio quality changes before the session reaches the minimum design margin. Handover is planned as a make-before-break event where practical, with the old radio path retained until the new path has accepted the session and passed basic message checks. If radio quality degrades faster than predicted, the onboard equipment falls back to the last validated movement authority rules rather than accepting unverified wayside data. The Safety Data Communication Network carries vital messages between zone controllers, interlockings, and approved interfaces using deterministic routing and monitored latency limits. The Safety Data Communication Network is physically and logically separated from maintenance and passenger information traffic to reduce exposure to congestion and non-vital configuration changes. Cryptographic Key Management Appliance supports the authentication framework used on the safety data paths and is managed under controlled access by authorized technical staff. North District Zone Controller is sometimes called Marble in legacy boundary diagrams, but this document uses North District Zone Controller as the canonical name. South District Zone Controller is sometimes called Orchid in commissioning notes, but this document uses South District Zone Controller as the canonical name. North District Zone Controller supervises the northern main line, the northern turnback, and the approach to East Junction Interlocking within its configured limits. South District Zone Controller supervises the southern main line, the southern turnback, and the approach to West Junction Interlocking within its configured limits. The boundary between North District Zone Controller and South District Zone Controller is defined by track section ownership, radio coverage overlap, and the point at which a train authority must be reissued by the receiving zone. At a zone boundary, the Atlas Onboard Controller confirms train position from the Dual Channel Odometer before accepting the next authority source. The receiving zone validates route state, train identity, and the expected boundary track section before extending authority beyond the transfer point. East Junction Interlocking and West Junction Interlocking report switch state, track occupancy, and route locking status to the responsible zone controller through vital interfaces. Wayside supervision compares interlocking indications, train position reports, and radio session state to detect inconsistent conditions before they can affect service. When the comparison detects a mismatch, the affected authority is withheld or shortened until the condition is resolved under the applicable operating rule. Metro Operations Control System displays zone status, radio coverage alarms, and train supervision states so that Line Traffic Controller can manage service without direct intervention in vital logic. Maintenance Diagnostic Server receives non-vital summaries of radio and network health, which allows Signal Maintenance Engineer to investigate degraded coverage trends without connecting to the vital channels. Wayside Radio Access Network sends the Radio Handover Audit File after each configured reporting interval. Voice Dispatch Console remains available as an operational communication path, but it is not used as a substitute for safety data messages or zone controller validation. Platform Screen Door Controller exchanges only the permitted interface indications with the supervising wayside systems, and its state is not allowed to override movement authority logic. Passenger Information Gateway and Service Timetable Server may receive selected service information, but those non-vital outputs are isolated from the command paths used for train protection.

## 4. Interlocking Integration and Route Protection

The CBTC supervision boundary at junctions is defined by the commanded route state, detected point position, and confirmed track vacancy rather than by geographic chainage alone. East Junction Interlocking is also known locally as Falcon for maintenance callouts and legacy panel references. North District Zone Controller requests routes through East Junction Interlocking only when the requested path is consistent with the current movement authority and the train approach profile. South District Zone Controller applies the same route protection principles for interfaces with West Junction Interlocking, including checks on flank protection and conflicting movement locking. The interlocking retains final vital authority for point movement, point locking, route locking, and release of protected sections. CBTC supervision treats a route as available only after the relevant interlocking reports points detected, locked, and proved in the required position. A requested route is not incorporated into a movement authority until the associated overlap and flank conditions are confirmed as protected. If the interlocking reports loss of point correspondence after a route has been granted, the affected movement authority is shortened or withdrawn according to the safe braking envelope. Axle counter clear and occupied indications are used as independent wayside evidence for route availability and for the continued integrity of route locking. Where axle counter occupation conflicts with the train location estimated by the CBTC system, the more restrictive state is applied until the discrepancy is resolved. East Junction Interlocking sends the Axle Counter Reset Request under the configured degraded-mode reset procedure. Reset processing is inhibited while any associated route remains locked or while a train is approaching the affected detection section. Maple verifies local track clearance evidence and records the result before authorising restoration of normal route setting after a detection anomaly. West Junction Interlocking sends the Route Release Exception List when normal sectional release cannot be completed within the configured timing rules. The zone controller treats any release exception as a retained hazard until the affected path is excluded from new movement authorities. Platform approach protection is enforced by combining the active route state with train stopping accuracy supervision near the platform berth. The Platform Screen Door Controller provides door closed and locked indications to the supervising wayside systems, but those indications do not replace interlocking route proof. If platform door readiness is absent, the approaching train may be held outside the platform stopping window even when the junction route is otherwise proved. The Atlas Onboard Controller uses the token bundle to validate that onboard operating context is current before accepting supervised route-related data. Train Radio Modem messages carrying route status are exchanged through Wayside Radio Access Network paths with sequence monitoring and timeout handling. Safety Data Communication Network paths between the zone controllers and the interlockings are configured for deterministic delivery of vital state changes and command acknowledgements. Metro Operations Control System displays the commanded route, locked route, point state, and track occupancy state as separate indications so that control staff can identify mismatches. A Line Traffic Controller may request a regulating action, but the request is executed only if the interlocking and CBTC protection rules agree that the route remains safe. The Maintenance Diagnostic Server records route command outcomes, point operation timing, and detection transitions for later engineering review without participating in vital decisions.

## 5. Operations Control Centre Workflows

The Operations Control Centre workflow begins with the Line Traffic Controller maintaining a live view of train positions, route status, alarms, and service performance across the Greenvale Metro line. The Metro Operations Control System presents regulating options based on timetable variance, platform dwell time, headway spacing, and the current availability of routes. The Line Traffic Controller selects holding, skip-stop, turnback, or run-through measures only after checking that passenger impact and downstream recovery have been assessed. Lantern displays the proposed regulating action, affected train identities, and predicted recovery time before the Line Traffic Controller confirms the command. The North District Zone Controller applies movement authority constraints according to the confirmed operating state and reports any conflict or unavailable route condition for controller review. The South District Zone Controller applies the same operating principles for the southern section while preserving separation margins and route locking conditions. East Junction Interlocking holds the route locked until track section detection, point correspondence, and opposing route conditions meet the configured release criteria. West Junction Interlocking provides route state indications that allow the Operations Control Centre to distinguish an operational delay from a signalling equipment fault. When an engineering possession or field report requires a temporary operating constraint, the Line Traffic Controller produces the Temporary Speed Restriction Notice after confirming the limits, speed value, start time, and expected removal condition. After a temporary operating constraint is active, the Line Traffic Controller monitors actual train performance against the expected delay allowance and adjusts regulation plans when recovery margins are exceeded. The Atlas Onboard Controller enforces the active movement profile on each equipped train and presents any onboard rejection or degraded mode indication for operational assessment. The Dual Channel Odometer supports train position confidence by providing independent distance measurement inputs that are considered during degraded operation review. The Train Radio Modem supports continuous operational messaging, and loss of radio availability is treated as a service risk that may require holding trains before constrained areas. The Wayside Radio Access Network is monitored for coverage alarms, handover irregularities, and capacity conditions that could affect the stability of train control communications. The Safety Data Communication Network is monitored as a safety-related bearer, and persistent communication faults are escalated before additional regulating actions are attempted in the affected area. For incident management, the Line Traffic Controller records the initial event time, train identifier, location, passenger impact, and the first safe operating instruction issued. The Voice Dispatch Console is used when direct verbal instruction is required, and the instruction is repeated back by the Revenue Train Operator before the action is treated as accepted. The Revenue Train Operator reports train condition, cab indications, passenger issues, and any local hazard that cannot be confirmed from the control room display alone. The Maintenance Diagnostic Server provides non-vital trend data that helps the Signal Maintenance Engineer determine whether an incident has an equipment cause or an operational cause. The Signal Maintenance Engineer advises the Line Traffic Controller on equipment recovery options, but the Line Traffic Controller remains responsible for service regulation decisions during the event. The Service Timetable Server is updated only through an approved timetable change workflow, and the Line Traffic Controller verifies that the revised plan is visible before relying on it for regulation. The Passenger Information Gateway receives the operational service state needed to align platform messages and train destination information with the current regulating plan. The Cryptographic Key Management Appliance uses the sample report to verify that certificate validity windows and controller clocks remain within the permitted tolerance before operational use. The Cryptographic Key Management Appliance produces the Encryption Certificate Renewal Pack under an approved maintenance window and records the change reference for later security audit. The Cyber Security Officer reviews certificate change records, access events, and unusual authentication failures before authorising normal operational reliance on the renewed security configuration. Authority review is performed after major incidents, degraded operations, and timetable interventions to confirm that each commanded movement remained consistent with interlocking state, controller intent, and recorded train response.

## 6. Station, Platform, and Passenger-Facing Interfaces

This section describes the operational interfaces between the Greenvale Metro Communications-Based Train Control System and station equipment that affects departures, passenger information, and dispatcher awareness. Platform equipment is treated as part of the movement-authority environment when its state can prevent a train from arriving, dwelling, or departing safely. Platform Screen Door Controller produces the Platform Door Status Snapshot at each monitored platform face for operational use by the CBTC system. The door interface reports closed, locked, bypassed, inhibited, and faulted conditions using coded states that are interpreted consistently across the line. A departure command is not considered available to the Revenue Train Operator until the relevant platform door state is compatible with the requested direction of travel. North District Zone Controller suppresses platform departure authority when the associated berth reports an unsafe door interface state. Station status indications presented to local station staff are derived from validated CBTC states rather than from unverified field contacts alone. These indications distinguish normal service, held train, door interlock fault, radio degraded, emergency stop active, and evacuation support conditions. The Metro Operations Control System displays station and platform states with the same identifiers used in controller workstations to reduce interpretation errors during incidents. Passenger Information Gateway receives train prediction data, stopping pattern changes, and platform allocation updates after the CBTC system has reconciled them with the active timetable. The Passenger Information Gateway shall not publish a revised destination or skip-stop message until the underlying train identity and route have been confirmed by the CBTC system. When a platform is taken out of service, the passenger information interface marks the affected train movements as unavailable for boarding while retaining their operational tracking in the control center. Service Timetable Server provides planned service context used to compare predicted arrivals against scheduled platform usage. Metro Operations Control System coordinates timetable deviation, platform closure, and station crowding information before it is released to passenger-facing systems. Voice Dispatch Console displays train calling identity, current platform association, and the latest operational hold reason so that spoken instructions match automated indications. The Line Traffic Controller may apply a station hold through dispatch controls, and the hold is reflected in CBTC departure logic without changing the underlying route locking rules. Dispatch-related controls use positive confirmation and cancellation states so that a cancelled hold is not confused with a timeout, failed message, or rejected command. Kestrel shall report train door enable side, berth identity, and departure readiness to support platform interface checks during dwell and dispatch. Saffron shall maintain message delivery paths for station interface traffic during normal radio coverage transitions and degraded coverage recovery. Train Radio Modem uses the Encryption Certificate Renewal Pack before accepting renewed secure sessions for passenger-facing and station-interface data paths. Safety Data Communication Network carries safety-relevant station interface messages separately from nonvital passenger information messages. Maintenance Diagnostic Server records station interface faults, rejected commands, and inconsistent indications for later investigation by the Signal Maintenance Engineer. Cyber Security Officer reviews access rights for passenger information and dispatch integration workstations when station-facing interfaces are commissioned or materially changed. During degraded operation, the CBTC system favours restrictive platform states and requires explicit controller action before restoring automatic departure support at the affected station.

## 7. Diagnostics, Maintenance, and Asset Monitoring

Diagnostics and maintenance for the Greenvale Metro communications-based train control system are organised around prompt event collection, controlled remote access, and traceable engineering decisions. Each safety-related part records time-stamped events for state changes, command acceptance, rejected commands, communication loss, and local protective actions. The Maintenance Diagnostic Server receives event streams from fixed and onboard equipment through monitored interfaces that do not allow maintenance traffic to alter train movement authority. The Maintenance Diagnostic Server uses the checksum log to compare stored braking data identifiers against the expected maintenance baseline for the affected train. Remote diagnostic sessions are initiated by a Signal Maintenance Engineer and are limited to read-only observation unless an approved maintenance window authorises a specific intervention. The Cyber Security Officer reviews privileged diagnostic access logs and confirms that remote connections use current credentials and approved encryption settings. The Line Traffic Controller receives the Route Release Exception List during incident triage and uses it to check whether a release anomaly could affect service recovery decisions. Marble records controller health, interface state, and fallback mode transitions in a form that can be correlated with adjacent wayside and operations records. The South District Zone Controller maintains comparable diagnostic coverage so that boundary events can be reviewed without relying on a single source of evidence. The East Junction Interlocking and West Junction Interlocking report route command histories, point detection changes, and protective lock status to support failure localisation. The Atlas Onboard Controller stores onboard fault codes, movement authority acceptance records, propulsion cutback requests, and train interface exceptions for later retrieval. The Dual Channel Odometer provides distance and speed plausibility information that is retained with trainborne diagnostic records after any odometry disagreement. The Train Radio Modem records bearer loss, reconnection timing, signal quality indicators, and message retry counts needed to distinguish radio coverage issues from equipment faults. The Wayside Radio Access Network and Safety Data Communication Network provide communication performance records that are compared with controller logs during network-related investigations. The Metro Operations Control System displays selected fault summaries to the Line Traffic Controller but does not expose detailed maintenance commands at the traffic control workstation. A Revenue Train Operator reports observed cab indications, train handling effects, and passenger service impacts using the operational reporting channel defined for degraded service. Failure triage first establishes whether the condition is safety critical, service affecting, cyber security related, or a maintenance-only defect. Safety critical failures remain under traffic control coordination until the Line Traffic Controller confirms the operating restriction and a Signal Maintenance Engineer confirms the engineering status. The Maintenance Diagnostic Server preserves raw events, normalised event records, diagnostic session notes, and file integrity values for post-incident analysis. Configuration records include software build identifiers, parameter set identifiers, radio configuration versions, interlocking data versions, and approved change references. The Cryptographic Key Management Appliance maintains certificate and key status records used to verify whether communications faults could be related to authentication or expiry conditions. The Service Timetable Server and Passenger Information Gateway are checked when reported symptoms involve timetable-driven routing assumptions or passenger-facing messages rather than train control protection. The Voice Dispatch Console is included in the evidence scope when verbal instructions, emergency calls, or manual coordination affected the response to an event. After a reportable incident, retained evidence is frozen under maintenance control, copied to a protected repository, and released only through the agreed engineering investigation process.

## 8. Degraded Modes and Manual Recovery

During degraded operation, Greenvale Metro keeps trains stationary or moving at controlled speed until separation assurance is restored. A degraded mode is declared by the Line Traffic Controller when automatic movement authority, train localization, point detection, platform interface, or radio continuity cannot be trusted. The Metro Operations Control System shall present the affected limits, the selected fallback mode, and any pending operator actions without obscuring safety alarms. The Atlas Onboard Controller transitions to restricted manual operation when valid movement authority is unavailable or when onboard location confidence falls below the configured threshold. The Revenue Train Operator remains responsible for speed control, stopping accuracy, door release discipline, and compliance with verbal movement limits during restricted manual operation. The Dual Channel Odometer shall cause the train location to be treated as uncertain when its channels disagree beyond the permitted tolerance. The Train Radio Modem shall report loss of safe data connectivity to the Atlas Onboard Controller and shall not mask intermittent communication failures from the cab display. A failure in the Wayside Radio Access Network is handled by reducing train throughput before any extension of manual working is considered. The North District Zone Controller applies the Temporary Speed Restriction Notice only after validating the affected track limits against its degraded-mode map. Orchid shall inhibit automatic route setting within any block whose occupancy state is under manual confirmation. The East Junction Interlocking shall hold commanded points locked until field confirmation or a verified diagnostic indication supports a safe release. The West Junction Interlocking shall reject a route command that conflicts with a manually protected train movement. Manual route recovery begins when the Line Traffic Controller confirms train positions through independent indications and direct verbal reports. Before a manual route is issued, the Signal Maintenance Engineer verifies point condition, track vacancy, and local protection using approved field procedures. The Signal Maintenance Engineer uses the Axle Counter Reset Request as the controlling authority for restoring a failed section after local occupancy checks are complete. Restricted manual movements shall be authorized through the Voice Dispatch Console unless the contingency communications procedure has been formally invoked. The Revenue Train Operator shall repeat back the movement limit, maximum permitted speed, stopping point, and any instruction to remain clear of a platform interface. The Platform Screen Door Controller remains in a protected local state when train stopping accuracy cannot be verified by the normal automatic interface. Reset authorizations require concurrence between the Line Traffic Controller and the Signal Maintenance Engineer when the reset can change route availability or occupancy status. Quarry shall mark each degraded-mode diagnostic session with the incident identifier before any component restart is attempted. The Cyber Security Officer shall review any degraded condition that involves authentication failure, unexpected lockout, or abnormal network access behavior. The Cryptographic Key Management Appliance shall not be bypassed to accelerate service recovery unless the approved emergency security procedure is active. The Revenue Train Operator sends the Test Train Clearance Memo after completing the prescribed proving run under restricted manual control. The Passenger Information Gateway shall issue disruption messages that are consistent with the operating limits accepted by the Line Traffic Controller. Return to automatic operation requires stable communications, verified route state, confirmed train position, and a recorded handback from maintenance control to operations control.

## 9. Cybersecurity, Key Management, and Audit Obligations

Cybersecurity controls for the Greenvale Metro Communications-Based Train Control System shall be treated as operating prerequisites for safe automatic train movement after any maintenance handback. The safety case assumes that loss of trust in communications can be as significant as loss of communications, and the operating response shall reflect that assumption. The Cyber Security Officer shall maintain continuous monitoring of security events from the Safety Data Communication Network, the Wayside Radio Access Network, and the Metro Operations Control System. Security monitoring shall correlate authentication failures, session resets, rejected messages, configuration changes, and abnormal timing patterns without altering any safety command path. Alerts affecting train control availability shall be classified using operational impact, affected territory, and whether the event could change train movement authority. The Line Traffic Controller shall be notified when a cybersecurity event requires a restriction on automatic operation or a change in communications mode. The Cyber Security Officer uses the audit file to investigate radio session continuity and to confirm that security monitoring records are complete. The Cryptographic Key Management Appliance shall be the controlled source for operational certificates used by trainborne, wayside, control center, and maintenance components. Certificate issuance shall require approved asset identity, recorded software baseline, assigned operating domain, and confirmation that the requesting component is authorized for service. Certificate renewal shall be planned before expiry so that the Atlas Onboard Controller, Train Radio Modem, North District Zone Controller, South District Zone Controller, and West Junction Interlocking do not enter service with degraded trust. Falcon shall reject any control message with an invalid signature, an expired certificate, or a sequence value outside the accepted window. Revocation shall be available for a compromised device, a retired device, or a device whose maintenance history can no longer be reconciled with approved configuration records. The Maintenance Diagnostic Server shall not be used to bypass certificate checks or to create alternate trust paths into safety-related communications. Administrative access to the Cryptographic Key Management Appliance shall require named accounts, multifactor authentication, separation of duties, and recorded approval for privileged changes. The Signal Maintenance Engineer may request access needed for fault isolation, but the Cyber Security Officer shall approve security-sensitive permissions before they are enabled. The Revenue Train Operator shall not be assigned cybersecurity administration duties during passenger service, although security warnings presented in the cab shall remain part of normal operating awareness. The Metro Operations Control System uses the Test Train Clearance Memo as a controlled prerequisite before accepting security exceptions for non-revenue movement in protected territory. Audit evidence shall include time-synchronized event records, access approvals, certificate actions, configuration changes, alarm acknowledgements, and operator instructions related to communications security. Audit records shall be protected from local alteration by the component that generates them and shall be retained for the period defined by Greenvale Metro governance. The Service Timetable Server and Passenger Information Gateway shall be monitored for compromise because incorrect operational context can influence control room decisions even when safety commands remain valid. The Voice Dispatch Console shall be included in security incident procedures when a cyber event changes the normal method for issuing operational instructions. The Platform Screen Door Controller shall accept only authenticated interfaces approved for passenger service coordination and maintenance testing. The Dual Channel Odometer and Atlas Onboard Controller shall report security-relevant diagnostic conditions in a manner that supports investigation without exposing cryptographic material. Security responsibilities shall be reviewed after major timetable changes, radio coverage modifications, control center upgrades, or any incident that questions the integrity of safety-related communications.

## 10. Commissioning, Migration, and Readiness for Revenue Service

Commissioning shall begin only after the installed equipment baseline for the Atlas Onboard Controller, Dual Channel Odometer, Train Radio Modem, and wayside control equipment has been configuration checked against the approved release record. The test-train phase shall use non-passenger movements to prove train localization, movement authority processing, braking response, radio continuity, and operator displays under controlled operating conditions. Each test train shall be crewed by a Revenue Train Operator qualified for commissioning duties and supported by a Line Traffic Controller with authority to stop the trial when observed conditions depart from the approved script. Initial movements shall be conducted at restricted commissioning speeds and shall progress to representative service speeds only after the Atlas Onboard Controller and North District Zone Controller demonstrate stable supervision. The South District Zone Controller shall be commissioned under the same test regime so that boundary behavior is verified before whole-line operation is attempted. East Junction Interlocking and West Junction Interlocking shall remain under enhanced monitoring during migration because route setting, point detection, and authority release behavior are critical to mixed-mode operation. The staged migration from legacy signalling shall divide the railway into controlled areas that can be commissioned, operated, and, if necessary, returned to legacy control without affecting uncommissioned areas. Cutover planning shall define the exact control boundaries, permissible train movements, staffing levels, and rollback conditions for each migration stage. Before any cutover stage, the Safety Data Communication Network and Wayside Radio Access Network shall be verified for coverage, latency, redundancy, and alarm presentation at the control center. Metro Operations Control System uses the status snapshot to confirm that platform interface conditions are suitable before authorizing the associated commissioning scenario. The Platform Screen Door Controller shall be included in end-to-end tests that confirm safe coordination between platform equipment, train stopping position, and supervised door enablement. The Service Timetable Server shall provide trial schedules that exercise normal headways, recovery margins, terminal reversals, and junction conflicts without carrying passengers. The Passenger Information Gateway shall be observed during operational rehearsal to confirm that public-facing messages remain consistent with the planned commissioning condition. Voice Dispatch Console procedures shall be rehearsed for test-train dispatch, abnormal stop instructions, evacuation coordination, and return to depot instructions. The Maintenance Diagnostic Server shall collect commissioning diagnostics so that intermittent radio, odometry, controller, and interlocking events can be reviewed before acceptance. The Cryptographic Key Management Appliance shall be confirmed as available, synchronized with the approved security baseline, and capable of supporting commissioning operations without manual workarounds. Readiness reviews shall be held at the completion of each stage and shall include representatives from operations, signalling maintenance, rolling stock, communications, safety assurance, and cyber security. A Signal Maintenance Engineer shall verify that field defects raised during test running have been closed, deferred with approved controls, or assigned to a post-commissioning monitoring plan. A Cyber Security Officer shall confirm that access controls, event monitoring, operator accounts, and security incident escalation paths are effective before revenue operation is recommended. Operational rehearsal shall use realistic degraded scenarios including radio coverage loss, failed train initialization, controller switchover, interlocking unavailability, and control center workstation failure. The rehearsal shall demonstrate that the Line Traffic Controller can maintain safe service regulation while the Revenue Train Operator follows onboard indications and rulebook instructions. Acceptance for revenue service shall require completion of planned test cases, closure of safety-critical defects, successful rollback demonstration, and approval of the operations readiness record. No open defect shall be accepted for revenue service unless its operational restriction, maintenance response, and passenger impact have been assessed and approved by the readiness review authority. Final entry into revenue service shall occur only after management confirms that training, documentation, staffing, spare equipment, monitoring arrangements, and emergency procedures are in place for sustained operation.
