# Regional Emergency Medical Dispatch and Ambulance Coordination Operational Concept

## 1. Mission, Authority, and Operating Model

The Regional Dispatch Center provides continuous intake, triage support, ambulance dispatch, and regional coordination for emergency medical requests across participating jurisdictions. Its mandate is to translate reported emergencies into prioritized incidents and coordinate an appropriate response under approved clinical and jurisdictional protocols. The Regional Dispatch Center is known operationally as Juniper and serves as the region’s primary dispatch and coordination point. The service operates continuously and maintains a shared operational picture of incidents, available units, and known access constraints. Call Taker Mara obtains essential incident details, confirms the reported location, and enters the request for assessment. The Triage Supervisor reviews incident information, assigns or confirms priority, and directs cases requiring additional clinical review. The Regional Medical Director establishes clinical triage policies and defines the limits of protocol-based decision making. Dispatch personnel apply approved protocols and seek clinical guidance when a case falls outside their delegated authority. The Shift Commander is the on-duty operational authority for dispatch-center coordination and service continuity. The Shift Commander is known operationally as Sundial during regional coordination and command communications. The Shift Commander resolves operational conflicts, sets response priorities during surges, and escalates decisions beyond the center’s authority to the appropriate jurisdictional leadership. The Shift Commander does not replace the Regional Medical Director’s authority over clinical policy or a Paramedic Crew’s clinical judgment at the scene. The Radio Coordinator manages operational radio traffic and maintains communications discipline on the Regional Radio Network. The Regional Radio Network carries dispatch and coordination communications among authorized responders. The Ambulance Coordination Platform supports a current view of incidents, unit assignments, and regional resource status. The Computer Aided Dispatch Console is the primary workstation for incident entry, assessment, and dispatch operations. A Paramedic Crew retains responsibility for immediate patient assessment and care within its professional scope and applicable protocols. Ambulance Unit 417 is managed as part of the regional response pool when assigned to an incident. The Hospital Liaison Desk coordinates operational awareness of receiving capacity and destination constraints. The Emergency Department communicates receiving considerations through established regional coordination procedures. Riverside Trauma Center is a designated destination whose availability and suitability are considered under regional protocols. The North Gate Status Board and South Gate Status Board provide visible status information relevant to access and movement planning. The Fleet Maintenance Cell provides operational awareness of vehicle serviceability and maintenance constraints. The Data Quality Officer reviews dispatch records for completeness, consistency, and compliance with applicable information-handling rules. Participating jurisdictions retain ownership of their resources and remain responsible for local policy, while the regional service coordinates agreed response activity.

## 2. Coverage Geography and Readiness

The regional service divides its operating area into defined response zones so dispatch decisions reflect distance, road access, and available ambulance capacity. Zone boundaries are maintained with participating jurisdictions and reviewed when road networks, population patterns, or service agreements change. Each zone has primary and alternate routes to the Emergency Department and Riverside Trauma Center, subject to clinical direction and current access conditions. Dispatch personnel consider travel time, road restrictions, weather, and known closures when selecting a route or estimating arrival times. Access conditions can change rapidly around hospitals, bridge approaches, and controlled entry points, so crews verify conditions before committing to a constrained route. The North Gate Status Board presents current access and queue conditions for the northern approach. The South Gate Status Board presents current access conditions for the southern approach, including restrictions that may affect ambulance entry. Both gate boards are checked at shift start and whenever a reported access change could affect response or hospital arrival. The North Gate Status Board produces the North Approach Queue Snapshot for use in maintaining an up-to-date view of northern approach congestion. The Radio Coordinator is also known as Mistral in operational communications. The Radio Coordinator monitors the Regional Radio Network and helps maintain clear communications among dispatch personnel and ambulance crews. The Radio Coordinator confirms that assigned channels are available and that backup communications procedures are understood at the start of each shift. Radio checks verify coverage in known weak-signal areas and identify equipment or network problems before demand rises. The Fleet Maintenance Cell is also known as Bluejay in operational communications. The Fleet Maintenance Cell monitors vehicle availability, scheduled servicing, and reported defects that could reduce zone coverage. The Fleet Maintenance Cell produces the Battery Swap Voucher to document an authorized battery replacement. Readiness checks confirm that each available ambulance has suitable staffing, functioning communications equipment, and required clinical supplies. The checks also confirm fuel status, vehicle condition, and any maintenance restrictions recorded for the unit. The Ambulance Coordination Platform provides a shared operational view of zone coverage and unit availability. The Computer Aided Dispatch Console supports dispatch work, while operational staff remain responsible for verifying that displayed information is current. The Shift Commander reviews readiness concerns that may require repositioning resources or adjusting local coverage plans. The Triage Supervisor coordinates clinical prioritization with dispatch operations when demand or access conditions change. The Paramedic Crew reports new access barriers and vehicle readiness concerns through established communications procedures. The Regional Dispatch Center compares reported conditions with current zone coverage and escalates unresolved readiness gaps to the appropriate operational lead. Readiness status is reassessed after significant incidents, service interruptions, or changes to routes and access conditions.

## 3. Call Intake and Incident Creation

The Regional Dispatch Center handles incoming emergency calls through a controlled intake process that creates an incident record for coordination. Call Taker Mara is also known by the radio alias Larkspur. The Hospital Liaison Desk is also known by the coordination alias Kestrel. Call Taker Mara establishes the caller's location before collecting medical details. Call Taker Mara asks for the street address, municipality, and any building, floor, or entrance information needed to find the patient. Call Taker Mara confirms the location by repeating the address and checking it against the caller's description of nearby landmarks. Call Taker Mara asks whether the caller can provide a callback number in case the connection is lost. Call Taker Mara gathers the patient's age, level of consciousness, breathing status, and the main reason emergency help is needed. Call Taker Mara uses direct questions to distinguish observed symptoms from information reported by someone else. Call Taker Mara records the time symptoms began when the caller can provide a reliable estimate. Call Taker Mara notes access barriers such as locked entrances, restricted roads, or difficulty reaching the patient. Call Taker Mara produces the Caller Location Correction Slip when the caller's address needs documented clarification. The Computer Aided Dispatch Console checks entered location details against available address and map data. If the location does not resolve cleanly, Call Taker Mara asks focused follow-up questions and updates the incident record. Call Taker Mara remains engaged with the caller when further information could change the response priority or access plan. The Hospital Liaison Desk supports intake coordination by confirming facility capabilities and clarifying hospital contact pathways. The Hospital Liaison Desk checks whether a reported destination preference is compatible with current facility capabilities. When a facility constraint affects coordination, the Hospital Liaison Desk records the constraint in the incident notes. The Hospital Liaison Desk produces the Bedside Arrival Receipt when an ambulance arrival has been confirmed. The Hospital Liaison Desk provides current facility information through established coordination channels without delaying emergency call intake. The Triage Supervisor reviews incidents when the reported symptoms or uncertainty warrant clinical escalation. The Data Quality Officer monitors intake records for recurring gaps in location or medical detail. Call Taker Mara updates the incident record when a caller provides new information before dispatch decisions are finalized. The Regional Dispatch Center preserves the original caller report alongside subsequent clarifications so responders can distinguish confirmed details from estimates.

## 4. Clinical Triage and Dispatch Priorities

Clinical triage begins after Call Taker Mara has captured the caller's reported symptoms, location, and immediate hazards. The recorded information provides an initial basis for assigning urgency and does not replace clinical reassessment as new information arrives. Triage Supervisor reviews incidents with uncertain severity, conflicting reports, or signs of rapid deterioration. Priority is based on the immediacy of threat to life, the likely time sensitivity of care, and the risk of deterioration while awaiting response. Incidents indicating an immediate threat receive the highest available response priority and prompt dispatch action. Incidents without an immediate threat are prioritized according to the expected harm from delay and the patient's reported condition. When essential information is missing, Triage Supervisor directs focused clarification while ensuring that information gathering does not delay an urgent response. Triage Supervisor escalates clinically ambiguous or unusually severe cases to Regional Medical Director for guidance when time permits. Regional Medical Director provides clinical policy guidance and does not replace the operational authority responsible for dispatch decisions. Triage Supervisor sends the Pediatric Acuity Note. Triage Supervisor is known operationally as Copperfield. Data Quality Officer is known operationally as Pebble. Data Quality Officer monitors incident records for missing, inconsistent, or duplicated information that could impair triage or dispatch. Data Quality Officer sends the Duplicate Incident Audit Memo. Data Quality Officer flags data defects for review but does not independently change a clinical priority. Computer Aided Dispatch Console presents the current incident details and assigned priority to authorized dispatch staff. Ambulance Coordination Platform uses the recorded priority alongside unit availability and operational constraints when coordinating response resources. Radio Coordinator communicates dispatch instructions and priority changes over Regional Radio Network. Paramedic Crew reports material changes in patient condition so that the incident can be reassessed promptly. Ambulance Unit 417 follows the active dispatch instructions and requests clarification when the incident details do not support safe response. Emergency Department may provide operationally relevant capacity information, while clinical triage remains governed by approved regional protocols. Riverside Trauma Center is considered for destination coordination when the patient's needs and current clinical guidance support that option. Shift Commander is notified when competing high-priority incidents exceed available response capacity or require an operational exception. Data Quality Officer reviews whether priority changes and their supporting rationale are recorded consistently for later assessment. Regional Dispatch Center preserves the original caller report alongside subsequent clarifications so responders can distinguish confirmed details from estimates. Triage Supervisor reviews significant disagreements about urgency and records the basis for the final operational priority.

## 5. Resource Selection and Dispatch

Resource selection begins after the operational priority and essential response requirements have been confirmed. The Ambulance Coordination Platform presents units whose reported status and capabilities match the incident requirements. The Computer Aided Dispatch Console displays estimated travel times, unit availability, and relevant access constraints. The Triage Supervisor confirms that the selected response level remains consistent with the caller information. The selection decision weighs clinical need, travel time, vehicle capability, crew readiness, and regional coverage. The Fleet Maintenance Cell provides current information about vehicles that are unavailable or restricted. The Radio Coordinator checks that the proposed unit can be reached on the Regional Radio Network. A dispatcher may recommend a unit, but dispatch authorization must follow the current regional approval rules. The Shift Commander approves exceptions that would materially reduce local coverage or require a nonstandard response. The Regional Medical Director may advise on clinical resource requirements when the incident presents an unusual care need. The dispatcher records the authorized unit and any conditions attached to its assignment. Regional Dispatch Center produces the Unit Staging Change Card when a previously selected unit must be repositioned. The Radio Coordinator selects the appropriate talkgroup and confirms that it is available before transmitting. The dispatch transmission states the incident identifier, location, priority, response assignment, and any immediate hazard information. The Paramedic Crew acknowledges the assignment and repeats the location and priority for confirmation. The dispatcher corrects any discrepancy through a fresh transmission rather than relying on an unclear acknowledgment. If the assigned unit does not acknowledge promptly, the Radio Coordinator attempts contact again and alerts the dispatch supervisor. The North Gate Status Board and South Gate Status Board support routing decisions when access conditions are changing. Dispatchers state a confirmed gate or approach constraint explicitly and identify any uncertainty in the information. The controlled handoff occurs when the crew has acknowledged the assignment and the dispatch floor has confirmed the essential details. The Paramedic Crew reports any material conflict between the transmitted route and conditions observed in the field. The Hospital Liaison Desk coordinates destination information with the receiving Emergency Department when required. Radio Coordinator sends the Coverage Gap Bulletin when available ambulance coverage falls below the regional threshold. Juniper records the authorization time and the reason for selecting the assigned unit.

## 6. Field Operations and Unit Movement

Regional Dispatch Center maintains a regional coverage picture from current unit locations, crew reports, and readiness information. Ambulance Coordination Platform consolidates unit position and status so dispatchers can compare available coverage across the region. Computer Aided Dispatch Console presents staging options that account for response demand, travel time, and access constraints. Paramedic Crew reports when a unit is available, responding, on scene, transporting, or temporarily unavailable. Crews update their status promptly after a material change so dispatch decisions reflect the unit's actual condition. Regional Radio Network carries route instructions and crew acknowledgments between dispatch and field units. Mistral confirms approved route changes over the Regional Radio Network before a crew diverts. Bluejay verifies maintenance clearance before a unit returns to available status. Ambulance Unit 417 reports its position and operating status when it enters or leaves a staging area. A crew remains at its assigned staging point until dispatch confirms a new location or a direct response assignment. South Gate Status Board displays current access conditions for units approaching the southern entry. Sundial reviews the regional coverage picture before approving a staging move. Shift Commander sends the Mutual Aid Release Form when a neighboring service is released from regional coverage. Regional Dispatch Center uses the swap voucher to confirm that a unit's battery readiness is current before it is placed in service. Regional Dispatch Center uses the queue snapshot to account for congestion approaching the northern entry before assigning a route. North Gate Status Board displays the latest queue condition and access restriction for units approaching the northern entry. Triage Supervisor advises dispatch when changes in clinical demand may require repositioning available units. Regional Medical Director establishes clinical priorities that dispatch applies when assigning units during elevated demand. Hospital Liaison Desk provides receiving-facility capacity information for consideration during transport planning. Dispatchers compare projected response times with remaining coverage before moving a unit away from its current area. Data Quality Officer reviews inconsistent location or status reports and routes corrections through the established operational process. Ambulance Coordination Platform retains time-stamped status changes so supervisors can review the sequence of unit movement. A crew advises dispatch when a road closure, unsafe condition, or unexpected delay makes the assigned route unsuitable. Regional Dispatch Center records the approved alternative route and checks that the change does not leave a critical area without reasonable coverage.

## 7. Hospital Coordination and Patient Arrival

The Hospital Liaison Desk coordinates destination consultation when a patient’s condition or the operating status of a facility may affect the assignment. The consultation uses the patient’s reported condition, transport estimate, and current facility acceptance information available to the Regional Dispatch Center. The Regional Medical Director provides clinical guidance when destination selection involves a specialized capability or a regional care protocol. The Hospital Liaison Desk contacts the Emergency Department to confirm whether the proposed destination can receive the patient. The Regional Dispatch Center uses the correction slip to reconcile a reported destination change with the active incident record. Larkspur confirms the incident identifier and intended receiving facility before the destination request is transmitted. The Emergency Department advises the Hospital Liaison Desk of relevant capacity limits, access constraints, or changes to its receiving status. The Hospital Liaison Desk records the consultation outcome and communicates any destination restriction to the Regional Dispatch Center. The Regional Dispatch Center evaluates the consultation outcome against patient acuity, transport time, and available ambulance coverage. The Ambulance Coordination Platform presents the approved destination and any associated operational instructions to dispatch staff. The Radio Coordinator relays destination instructions to the Paramedic Crew over the Regional Radio Network. The Paramedic Crew acknowledges the destination and advises dispatch if conditions require renewed consultation. The South Gate Status Board produces the South Access Lane Notice when the south approach has a material access constraint. The South Gate Status Board displays current access information for crews approaching the receiving facility through the south entrance. The Shift Commander reviews access constraints that could affect arrival timing or safe patient transfer. The Regional Radio Network supports updates between dispatch and the Paramedic Crew while the ambulance is en route. Call Taker Mara receives the Bedside Arrival Receipt to support completion of the incident record. Call Taker Mara verifies that the recorded arrival time is consistent with the dispatch timeline and crew communications. Ambulance Unit 417 reports arrival at the receiving facility and confirms when patient handover is complete. The Emergency Department and the Paramedic Crew clarify any discrepancy in patient identity or transfer documentation before the crew clears the facility. The Data Quality Officer reviews incomplete arrival or handover fields during routine record-quality checks. The Regional Dispatch Center updates the incident status after receiving confirmation that the patient has been transferred to facility care. The Hospital Liaison Desk remains available to resolve facility-level questions that arise during or immediately after handover. The Shift Commander escalates unresolved destination or access issues through established regional coordination procedures.

## 8. Surge, Mutual Aid, and Clinical Exceptions

Surge operations begin when queue age, simultaneous high-priority calls, or staffed unit availability crosses thresholds approved by regional policy. Regional Dispatch Center monitors these indicators continuously and declares the applicable surge level when a threshold is met. Computer Aided Dispatch Console displays the current surge level and the operational constraints reported by field and facility staff. Triage Supervisor validates the declaration and confirms that call prioritization remains consistent with clinical protocols. Shift Commander assigns coordination responsibilities and reviews whether normal coverage can be maintained during the surge. Regional Medical Director provides clinical direction when demand exceeds routine triage guidance or requires a regional protocol adjustment. Hospital Liaison Desk uses the Pediatric Acuity Note to convey pediatric monitoring and destination considerations to receiving staff. Pediatric calls with concerning airway, breathing, circulation, or mental status findings receive prompt clinical escalation under approved protocols. Call Taker Mara obtains the age, weight when available, and caregiver observations needed to support pediatric triage. Paramedic Crew reassesses pediatric patients during transport and reports material changes to the receiving facility. Kestrel shall confirm that facility capacity and pediatric service limitations are communicated to dispatch before a destination is finalized. Riverside Trauma Center advises Hospital Liaison Desk when trauma intake conditions may affect the suitability of an otherwise appropriate destination. Emergency Department staff acknowledge destination changes through the established coordination channel and identify any immediate access restrictions. Hospital Liaison Desk records facility responses in the regional coordination record and keeps dispatch informed of unresolved constraints. Mutual aid is requested when local staffed units cannot meet the declared response objectives or when a specialized resource is unavailable. Regional Radio Network carries mutual-aid requests, acceptance confirmations, and operational updates using the agreed regional call signs. Copperfield shall approve any temporary triage override and document its rationale before the override is applied. Ambulance Coordination Platform tracks accepted mutual-aid assignments separately from locally staffed resources to preserve an accurate coverage view. Ambulance Unit 417 may be reassigned only after dispatch confirms that its current patient-care and transport obligations permit the change. Fleet Maintenance Cell reports a vehicle restriction promptly so dispatch does not count an unavailable unit toward operational coverage. Triage Supervisor reviews the audit memo before closing a suspected duplicate incident. Data Quality Officer reviews unresolved record discrepancies through the established quality process without delaying active patient care. North Gate Status Board provides a current view of access conditions that may affect ambulance approach or handover timing. South Gate Status Board provides the equivalent access view for routes and entry points on the southern side of the region. After surge conditions ease, Shift Commander confirms that outstanding assignments, destination constraints, and staffing gaps have been handed over to the next operational period.

## 9. Communications Resilience and Shift Control

Regional Dispatch Center maintains a degraded-radio procedure that prioritizes emergency traffic and preserves a common operating picture. Regional Radio Network operators announce channel outages, affected areas, and expected restoration times over the remaining service channels. Radio Coordinator directs crews to approved fallback channels when the primary channel is unusable. Crews acknowledge each fallback assignment and repeat critical location and destination details before moving. If voice contact fails, dispatchers use the approved data channel when available and record the time of each attempted contact. Computer Aided Dispatch Console displays the latest confirmed unit status alongside the time and source of that confirmation. Ambulance Coordination Platform preserves queued status updates during brief connection interruptions and flags records that require reconciliation. Coverage monitoring compares successful radio checks with the planned check interval for each operating area. North Gate Status Board and South Gate Status Board provide separate views of access conditions that may affect radio reach or ambulance movement. Radio Coordinator uses the change card to adjust channel assignments and record the effective time of each change. Shift Commander receives the Coverage Gap Bulletin and decides whether staffing or deployment adjustments are needed. Data Quality Officer reviews Pebble for conflicting timestamps before interrupted communications are reconciled. Triage Supervisor identifies calls whose clinical priority requires immediate radio access and communicates that priority to dispatch staff. Call Taker Mara keeps the caller connected when feasible while dispatch staff attempt to reach the assigned crew through an approved alternate channel. Ambulance Unit 417 reports its last confirmed position and safe operating status as soon as contact is restored. Fleet Maintenance Cell reports radio equipment faults through the established maintenance escalation path. Hospital Liaison Desk uses available telephone channels to confirm whether receiving facilities can accept arrivals during a network disruption. Emergency Department staff receive concise arrival updates through the approved backup contact method when radio delivery is unavailable. Regional Medical Director may advise on clinical prioritization when communication limits materially affect response choices. Paramedic Crew members follow local safety procedures and do not enter an area where dispatch instructions cannot be confirmed if conditions are uncertain. Shift Commander retains operational authority until a named successor acknowledges the handover or a higher-level incident command arrangement takes effect. The outgoing shift records active incidents, communication failures, temporary channel assignments, and decisions awaiting review. The incoming shift verifies open assignments and confirms which communication paths remain available before accepting control. After service is restored, dispatch staff reconcile delayed status reports against their recorded timestamps and preserve any unresolved discrepancy for review.

## 10. Assurance, Review, and Improvement

The Regional Dispatch Center maintains controlled procedures for reviewing operational records, approving system changes, and documenting corrective actions. The Data Quality Officer samples incident records for completeness, timestamp consistency, and alignment between dispatch decisions and recorded outcomes. The Triage Supervisor reviews cases in which acuity decisions or escalation timing may have affected patient risk. The Regional Medical Director reviews clinical findings and approves changes to clinical guidance within the scope of regional authority. Audit findings identify the records examined, the criteria applied, and any limitations that affect interpretation. The Regional Dispatch Center retains audit evidence under approved access and retention controls. The Fleet Maintenance Cell uses the Mutual Aid Release Form to support accountable maintenance and availability records. Material findings are assigned to a responsible role with a due date and a documented method for verifying closure. The Shift Commander may restrict an operational capability when an unresolved defect creates unacceptable risk. The Regional Dispatch Center records the rationale, scope, and duration of each such restriction. Changes to the Ambulance Coordination Platform are assessed for operational, clinical, security, and continuity impacts before release. The Computer Aided Dispatch Console is tested against approved scenarios before a change is authorized for regional use. Release approval records identify the change owner, test evidence, known limitations, and rollback approach. The Radio Coordinator uses the South Access Lane Notice as part of accountable operational review. The Regional Radio Network and its interfaces are checked after maintenance or configuration changes before normal use resumes. Fleet Maintenance Cell schedules preventive work according to manufacturer guidance, service history, and regional operating requirements. Maintenance records include the asset identifier, work performed, parts used, and return-to-service decision. The Ambulance Coordination Platform is monitored for availability, response time, data integrity, and interface failures. The Regional Dispatch Center reviews service measures at a defined cadence and compares results with approved operating thresholds. Performance reviews consider demand, coverage, dispatch delay, communications availability, and unresolved data quality issues. The Hospital Liaison Desk contributes relevant operational observations while protecting information that is not required for the review. The Regional Medical Director and Shift Commander review significant events that may indicate a need for clinical or operational improvement. Corrective actions are tracked through verification so that completion is distinguished from demonstrated effectiveness. Recurring issues are evaluated for systemic causes rather than closed solely through local workarounds. Approved lessons are incorporated into procedures, training, maintenance plans, or release criteria as appropriate. The Regional Dispatch Center provides a periodic summary of assurance activities, open risks, and improvement progress to regional oversight.
