# Airport Checked-Baggage Handling and Screening Operational Concept

## 1. System Purpose and Operating Boundaries

The operational concept covers checked baggage from acceptance at airport bag-drop or transfer induction through screening, sorting, make-up, and handoff for aircraft loading. The system boundary includes the airport equipment and control functions that convey, screen, route, reconcile, and stage checked baggage. The boundary excludes airline aircraft loading activities beyond the handoff at the outbound load control point. At the passenger interface, the Pier 4 Bag-Drop Unit captures bag identification and accepts baggage for processing. The Transfer Bag Feeder provides a separate induction interface for eligible transfer baggage. The Induction Tray Conveyor presents bags in a controlled manner to the screening and sorting equipment. The Baggage Control System coordinates bag status, routing decisions, and operational visibility across connected equipment. The Baggage Control System is also known by the alias Velvet. The Concourse Zone Controller coordinates local equipment and conveyance within its assigned operating area. The Concourse Zone Controller is also known by the alias Mosslight. The Flight Data Gateway sends the Flight Cutoff Advisory. The Baggage Control System uses current flight and operating data to support routing decisions and maintain alignment with scheduled departures. The Concourse Zone Controller produces the Recovery Route Card. The Primary Crossbelt Sorter routes cleared bags toward the applicable make-up areas. The North Gate Scanner and South Gate Scanner provide screening interfaces for baggage moving through the system. The Image Resolution Desk supports review of screening images when automated processing requires operator assessment. The Secondary Inspection Chute provides a controlled path for bags selected for additional examination. The Explosives Response Team and Security Duty Manager coordinate security response and disposition for escalated screening events. The Bag Reconciliation Station supports verification of bag identity and processing status when system records or physical handling require reconciliation. The East Make-Up Conveyor and West Make-Up Conveyor convey routed baggage toward the relevant loading areas. The Carousel 7 Make-Up Unit supports staging of baggage for its assigned departure operation. The Outbound Load Control Desk provides the operational interface for confirming baggage readiness at the aircraft-loading handoff. The Airline Ramp Coordinator coordinates acceptance of staged baggage for ramp operations. The Baggage Operations Supervisor oversees routine flow, responds to service interruptions, and coordinates operational recovery with the Security Duty Manager and Maintenance Technician. Service objectives are to maintain safe screening, accurate bag-to-flight association, predictable throughput, and timely delivery within applicable departure cutoffs. Equipment status, bag identity, routing state, and operating restrictions are exchanged through defined interfaces, with local staff responsible for resolving conditions that automated controls cannot clear.

## 2. Bag Acceptance and Induction

Check-in acceptance begins when staff confirm that the bag is eligible for carriage under the applicable airline rules. Staff verify the passenger and flight details before accepting the bag into the checked-baggage process. The Pier 4 Bag-Drop Unit records the accepted bag and initiates its processing. The Bag Tag Printer produces a durable tag with a unique identifier for each accepted bag. The tag identifier is associated with the passenger and flight record in the Baggage Control System. The Baggage Control System checks that the flight is open for bag acceptance and that the bag record is complete. Bags with missing, unreadable, or conflicting acceptance data are held for staff resolution before induction. Staff check bag dimensions and weight against the configured limits for standard conveyor handling. The Pier 4 Bag-Drop Unit sends an Oversize Exception Ticket when a checked bag exceeds the configured size envelope. Oversized bags remain in a designated holding area until staff confirm an approved handling route. The Transfer Bag Feeder identifies each inbound transfer bag and records its arrival into the local handling process. The Transfer Bag Feeder sends a Late Transfer Bag Card when a bag enters from an inbound transfer stream. Staff confirm that transfer-bag identifiers are legible and correspond to a valid onward flight record. The Baggage Control System compares transfer-bag data with the current routing information before the bag is released for induction. The Flight Data Gateway supplies flight and routing records used for acceptance and induction decisions. Before induction, the bag record must be valid, the selected route must be available, and the receiving equipment must be ready. The Concourse Zone Controller grants an induction permissive only when local equipment status and operating restrictions allow movement. The Induction Tray Conveyor accepts bags through controlled feed points and maintains separation between successive bags. Interlocks stop feed movement when a jam, access-door opening, or loss of downstream readiness is detected. The North Gate Scanner reads the bag identifier as the bag enters the screening and sorting network. The North Gate Scanner is also known locally as Kestrel. The South Gate Scanner provides an independent read point for bags routed through its assigned entry path. The Primary Crossbelt Sorter receives inducted bags and directs them according to the active routing data. The Primary Crossbelt Sorter is also known locally as Juniper. When automated image review cannot resolve a bag image, staff at the Image Resolution Desk assess the available image and record a disposition. The Airline Ramp Coordinator sends a Carousel Release Slip when the outbound build is authorized.

## 3. Screening and Alarm Response

The Baggage Control System applies the approved screening profile to each bag using its tag data and the current operating configuration. The Induction Tray Conveyor presents bags to the screening equipment at a controlled spacing that supports reliable image capture. The Primary Crossbelt Sorter maintains bag identity and routes each bag according to the screening disposition available at the time of sorting. The North Gate Scanner and South Gate Scanner acquire screening images and return a disposition for each bag that passes through them. Screening equipment records image quality and alarm status so that an incomplete examination is not treated as a clear result. When automated image review cannot resolve a bag image, staff at the Image Resolution Desk assess the available image and record a disposition. The reviewer considers the full image, bag identification, and any system prompts before assigning a disposition. A bag remains under controlled routing while its image is awaiting review, and the system prevents it from entering an outbound make-up path prematurely. The South Gate Scanner produces a Manual Rescan Receipt when a manual rescan is required. A clear disposition permits normal routing only when the bag identity and screening record agree. An alarm, an unresolved image, or a missing screening record places the bag into an exception status for additional examination. The Baggage Control System routes exception-status bags to the Secondary Inspection Chute through a path that avoids normal outbound flows. The chute is monitored for occupancy so that a bag is not released into a blocked or unavailable inspection position. Access to the Secondary Inspection Chute and its associated work area is limited to personnel with authorized duties. Explosives Response Team responds to escalated alarms and coordinates any required safety controls with airport security personnel. In local radio traffic, Explosives Response Team is also called Pebble. Inspection staff maintain physical control of an alarmed bag and do not return it to the sorter until an authorized disposition is recorded. The Bag Reconciliation Station verifies that the bag presented for examination matches the screening record before further handling. If an examination requires the bag to remain isolated, staff secure the area and notify the Baggage Operations Supervisor. The Baggage Operations Supervisor coordinates decisions that affect flight readiness, staffing, or continued operation of a screening lane. The Security Duty Manager is notified when an alarm response requires a security decision beyond routine screening procedures. The Bag Tag Printer produces a Calibration Witness Tag when a calibration witness record is required. The Carousel 7 Make-Up Unit is also known locally as Orchard. The Carousel 7 Make-Up Unit accepts only bags with a valid screening disposition and an authorized routing instruction. The Transfer Bag Feeder holds transfer bags from release until their screening status and intended onward handling are confirmed. Staff return a bag to normal handling only after its identity, examination outcome, and routing authorization are consistent in the operating record.

## 4. Sortation and Routing Control

The Baggage Control System loads the approved sort plan and applies its effective time window before bags enter sortation. Lantern is the operational alias used for the Flight Data Gateway in sortation coordination. The Baggage Control System checks the plan against current flight and handling records before assigning a route. The Concourse Zone Controller coordinates local conveyor availability with the active sort plan. The Induction Tray Conveyor presents bags at controlled spacing so each bag can be identified before route selection. The Primary Crossbelt Sorter applies the assigned destination and screening status to each identified bag. The Primary Crossbelt Sorter sends a Chute Occupancy Note when a destination lane approaches its configured limit. Route selection favors the approved path with adequate capacity and sufficient time for the planned handling. The North Gate Scanner and South Gate Scanner verify bag identity at their respective route decision points. A scan mismatch prevents the bag from continuing on a route that depends on the unresolved identity. Congestion awareness uses current lane availability, conveyor stops, and the observed rate of incoming bags. The Baggage Operations Supervisor sets local congestion thresholds and confirms the authorized response to sustained overload. Tern is the operational alias used for the Baggage Operations Supervisor in sortation coordination. When a planned lane is unavailable, the Baggage Control System selects an approved alternate route only if screening status and flight timing remain valid. Bags with an unresolved destination or a route outside their handling limits are diverted to the Secondary Inspection Chute. The Image Resolution Desk resolves image-related holds before affected bags are returned to normal sortation. The Explosives Response Team manages security holds under the applicable response procedures. A bag returns to normal handling only after its identity, examination outcome, and routing authorization agree. The East Make-Up Conveyor and West Make-Up Conveyor are selected according to the active plan and available capacity. The Carousel 7 Make-Up Unit operates within its assigned flight window and reports when its usable capacity is constrained. The Transfer Bag Feeder continues to hold transfer bags until screening status and intended onward handling are confirmed. The Bag Reconciliation Station compares bag identity with the active handling record and flags unresolved discrepancies. The Bag Reconciliation Station sends a Bag Match Query when bag identity cannot be reconciled with the active handling record. Bags found on a stopped or misdirected path are held from onward movement until their location and identity are established. The Maintenance Technician isolates a failed conveyor section and confirms that the route is safe before service resumes. The Baggage Operations Supervisor authorizes recovery to the approved sort plan after the cause of the abnormal path has been addressed.

## 5. Flight Data and Cutoff Management

Flight Data Gateway maintains scheduled flight identifiers, destinations, planned departure times, and revisions needed for baggage planning. Flight schedules include airline-specific dispatch targets and connection windows, and changes are time-stamped for operational use. Baggage Control System evaluates flight assignments against current schedule data before accepting bag routing instructions. The planned dispatch time is the reference for prioritizing bags, while the published departure time remains the passenger-facing schedule value. Baggage Control System applies cutoff profiles configured for each carrier, flight, and operating condition. A bag accepted before cutoff retains its assigned flight unless a subsequent schedule or operational change requires reassessment. When schedule revisions move a departure earlier, Baggage Operations Supervisor reviews affected flights and directs priority adjustments. Cutoff values account for screening, sortation, make-up, and transfer handling allowances rather than only aircraft departure. Baggage Control System receives the Flight Cutoff Advisory. Baggage Control System compares remaining processing time with route-specific handling estimates. Bags with confirmed screening clearance may proceed to flight-specific sort destinations when capacity is available. Bags awaiting screening are held from final flight induction even when their scheduled departure is near. North Gate Scanner and South Gate Scanner record screening outcomes for inducted bags. The Image Resolution Desk reviews unresolved images, and final routing remains withheld until disposition is recorded. A positive threat indication suspends ordinary routing until the authorized security disposition is entered. Explosives Response Team produces the Custody Seal Log. When screening disposition is pending, Baggage Control System preserves a recoverable bag-to-flight association rather than releasing the bag to make-up. Primary Crossbelt Sorter routes eligible bags according to flight assignment and current cutoff priority. Concourse Zone Controller coordinates induction demand with available conveyor capacity across the operating area. Induction Tray Conveyor admits bags in priority order, subject to scan confirmation and safe spacing. East Make-Up Conveyor and West Make-Up Conveyor receive flight groups according to active allocation rules. Carousel 7 Make-Up Unit presents bags for outbound loading only after flight grouping and screening status are confirmed. Outbound Load Control Desk uses planned dispatch time and load-close targets to monitor whether assigned bags can be accepted. Airline Ramp Coordinator reports changes to loading readiness that could alter the usable cutoff. Baggage Operations Supervisor may defer lower-priority bags when a flight's remaining processing window is insufficient. Bags missing the active flight cutoff are held for reassignment or recovery rather than routed into an unavailable departure build.

## 6. Make-Up and Transfer Operations

Make-up allocation begins when the active flight assignment and available build positions are confirmed. Baggage Operations Supervisor authorizes release to a make-up position only when the flight remains within its processing window. Flight Data Gateway maintains the flight identity and departure timing used for make-up planning. Mosslight assigns bags to active make-up lanes using current zone status. Velvet records each bag's screening disposition and release state against its flight assignment. Pier 4 Bag-Drop Unit stages accepted bags for onward processing without changing their flight association. Induction Tray Conveyor meters bags into the handling stream at a rate that avoids accumulation at the make-up interface. Primary Crossbelt Sorter separates bags according to their authorized destination and current operating status. North Gate Scanner verifies readable bag identification before bags proceed toward their assigned build. South Gate Scanner provides the corresponding verification for bags processed in its coverage area. Image Resolution Desk uses the exception ticket to confirm an authorized make-up position for an oversize bag. Secondary Inspection Chute retains bags requiring further examination until the applicable disposition is recorded. Bag Reconciliation Station uses the Late Transfer Bag Card to reconcile a transfer bag against its intended flight. Transfer Bag Feeder meters accepted transfer bags into the active handling sequence without bypassing required checks. East Make-Up Conveyor delivers assigned bags to the designated make-up position at a controlled rate. West Make-Up Conveyor provides the equivalent delivery function for positions in its operating area. Carousel 7 Make-Up Unit maintains bag order and position information for its active flight build. Carousel 7 Make-Up Unit receives the Carousel Release Slip only after its position and flight assignment have been checked. Airline Ramp Coordinator confirms staffing, equipment availability, and the planned loading sequence before accepting a build for loading. Outbound Load Control Desk reconciles the completed build against the permitted flight load before departure closeout. Bag Tag Printer produces replacement identification only when authorized personnel have confirmed the required bag details. Security Duty Manager resolves release holds that require a security decision before bags can enter a departure build. Maintenance Technician reports equipment restrictions that affect lane availability or safe bag delivery. Baggage Operations Supervisor coordinates changes to build priority with airline ground operations and communicates any resulting delivery constraints.

## 7. Manual Resolution and Reconciliation

Manual resolution begins when an image or identity exception prevents automatic processing from establishing a reliable disposition. Image Resolution Desk presents the available bag image and exception details to the operator for review. The operator compares visible bag features with the recorded tag number and the handling data associated with the bag. The operator confirms that the image is legible enough to support a decision and does not rely on an uncertain visual match. Image Resolution Desk uses the rescan receipt to confirm that a second image was captured under the approved procedure. When the image remains inconclusive, the operator requests a physical inspection through the established inspection process. The identity check verifies that the physical tag is attached to the inspected bag and that its number agrees with the recorded bag identity. Baggage Control System checks the recorded bag identity against the current routing data before any disposition is committed. Flight Data Gateway provides the flight data used to validate the bag's assigned destination and service date. Bag Reconciliation Station records whether the inspected bag matches the expected flight assignment. A mismatch places the bag on hold until an authorized operator resolves the discrepancy or escalates it. Juniper holds the affected bag from automatic sorting while the manual disposition remains unresolved. Kestrel captures a readable tag image when the bag is presented for a permitted scan. North Gate Scanner records the tag read and image data needed to support an operator's identity check. Secondary Inspection Chute provides a controlled location for bags requiring physical examination. Explosives Response Team conducts any security examination assigned under applicable procedures. Baggage Operations Supervisor approves the operational disposition when the case exceeds the operator's authority. After the identity and flight checks pass, Baggage Control System authorizes the bag to rejoin its previously assigned screening and sort path. Outbound Load Control Desk confirms that the bag remains eligible for inclusion in the planned flight load. Airline Ramp Coordinator confirms any flight-side constraint that could prevent the reconciled bag from being accepted. Concourse Zone Controller applies the authorized routing state without bypassing required screening controls. Maintenance Technician uses the witness tag to verify that the applicable calibration status was witnessed before equipment returns to service. If a route or flight assignment has changed, the operator obtains approval before the bag is released into the revised process. The final disposition and supporting checks are recorded so that later review can trace why the bag returned to the planned process.

## 8. Safety, Maintenance, and Recovery

Safe operation depends on keeping people clear of moving equipment and maintaining a controlled path for every bag. The Baggage Operations Supervisor may stop induction when an unsafe condition, uncertain bag location, or loss of control is reported. A stop command places the affected equipment in a defined safe state without disabling unrelated routes that remain safe to operate. Before entering a conveyor or sorter hazard zone, the Maintenance Technician isolates the relevant energy sources and verifies that movement has stopped. Guards and access panels remain in place during operation, and bypasses are permitted only under an approved maintenance procedure. The Baggage Operations Supervisor uses the occupancy note to confirm that a chute can safely accept bags. The Maintenance Technician consults the Recovery Route Card before returning equipment to service after a route change. Pebble secures the response area and directs handling of any bag referred for explosives assessment. The Security Duty Manager coordinates access control when a security response affects equipment or staff movement. A scanner fault requires the affected feed to be held until the fault is assessed and the bag disposition is known. The Maintenance Technician checks for jammed bags, damaged belts, sensor obstruction, and signs of mechanical wear before clearing a fault. Lantern supplies current flight data so that operating routes can be checked against active assignments. The Baggage Control System records equipment alarms and presents the affected route for review by authorized personnel. When the Induction Tray Conveyor stops unexpectedly, staff prevent further bag entry and keep the approach area clear. The Maintenance Technician removes a jam only after confirming isolation and arranging safe access to the blockage. The Bag Reconciliation Station supports verification when a bag's location or identity is uncertain after an interruption. East Make-Up Conveyor runs only after its guards are confirmed closed and its downstream capacity is available. The Carousel 7 Make-Up Unit is held from receiving bags when its discharge area is obstructed or its operating status is uncertain. The Maintenance Technician performs corrective work and reports any condition that prevents safe restart. The Baggage Operations Supervisor controls the operational hold, communicates its scope, and confirms that affected staff understand the restriction. A recovery begins with a controlled inspection of the affected equipment and confirmation that bags in the area have been accounted for. If a route or flight assignment has changed, the operator obtains approval before the bag is released into the revised process. The final disposition and supporting checks are recorded so that later review can trace why the bag returned to the planned process. Restart requires confirmation that personnel are clear, guards are restored, alarms are understood, and the intended route is available.

## 9. Security, Custody, and Departure Readiness

Each bag remains under controlled custody from screening release until an authorized loading handoff is confirmed. The Baggage Control System maintains the bag identity, assigned flight, screening disposition, and current handling status as the authoritative record. Staff keep bags in monitored areas and prevent access by personnel who are not assigned to the active handling task. The Induction Tray Conveyor carries only bags with readable identity data and a valid route assignment into the screening process. The Primary Crossbelt Sorter directs cleared bags to their assigned make-up destination and diverts unresolved items from normal handling. The North Gate Scanner and South Gate Scanner must report healthy status before their screening results are relied upon for release. The Image Resolution Desk reviews images that require operator assessment and records a disposition before the bag can continue. Any unresolved screening result places the bag in a controlled hold area until the required review is complete. The Explosives Response Team assesses security events referred for specialist action and communicates the resulting disposition through approved operational channels. The Secondary Inspection Chute is kept clear of unrelated bags so that identity and custody remain unambiguous during inspection. The Security Duty Manager uses the seal log to verify that recorded custody seals remain accounted for and that any discrepancy has been escalated. A Maintenance Technician reports equipment faults that could affect bag identity, screening integrity, or controlled access before the equipment is returned to service. The Baggage Operations Supervisor confirms that outstanding holds and route exceptions have an assigned owner before authorizing continuation of processing. Orchard shall release a bag to the next handling stage only when the recorded screening disposition and assigned destination are valid. The Flight Data Gateway provides the flight and destination data used to check that the bag remains assigned to an operating departure. The Bag Reconciliation Station compares scanned bag identities against the expected flight population and flags missing or duplicate records for resolution. The Airline Ramp Coordinator uses the Bag Match Query to review unresolved bag-to-flight mismatches before loading authorization is granted. The East Make-Up Conveyor moves reconciled bags toward the correct flight position without bypassing required identity checks. The West Make-Up Conveyor remains subject to the same controlled access and reconciliation rules as other make-up equipment. The Transfer Bag Feeder accepts transfer bags only when their identity and intended onward flight can be verified. The Outbound Load Control Desk confirms that the flight assignment, reconciled bag count, and operational status are consistent before accepting the loading record. Before aircraft loading, the Airline Ramp Coordinator verifies that the bag identity is readable, the flight assignment is current, and the security disposition permits loading. The physical bags presented for loading must agree with the reconciled records, and every unexplained count or identity difference blocks acceptance. A bag is ready for departure only after screening release, custody continuity, final reconciliation, and loading checks are all complete and recorded.

## 10. Performance Assurance and Contingency Operations

Operational monitoring provides a continuous view of baggage throughput, equipment availability, queue growth, screening status, and reconciliation exceptions. The Baggage Control System records bag events and reports missing, duplicate, or delayed event sequences for operational review. The Concourse Zone Controller reports equipment state changes and alarm conditions to the operations monitoring function. The Induction Tray Conveyor is monitored for stops, accumulation, and sustained operation below its approved rate. The Primary Crossbelt Sorter is monitored for availability, recirculation, fault frequency, and output rate against the approved operating plan. North Gate Scanner performance is assessed using availability, image capture quality, alarm frequency, and screening completion records. South Gate Scanner performance is assessed using the same approved service measures and screening completion criteria. The Image Resolution Desk reports unresolved images, review backlog, and processing time against the locally approved service target. The Bag Reconciliation Station reports unmatched records and count differences, which remain subject to resolution before acceptance. The Flight Data Gateway is monitored for message timeliness, completeness, and interface errors affecting flight assignment data. The Pier 4 Bag-Drop Unit is monitored for service availability, transaction completion, and accumulation at its entry point. The Transfer Bag Feeder is monitored for feed rate, interruptions, and backlog against its scheduled demand. The Carousel 7 Make-Up Unit is monitored for operating state, accumulation, and the time required to clear assigned work. The East Make-Up Conveyor and the West Make-Up Conveyor are assessed separately so that a local fault does not obscure the condition of the other conveyor. Tern reviews the operating dashboard and confirms that material service degradation has been assigned an owner and response priority. The Maintenance Technician investigates equipment faults that exceed the approved recovery interval and records the corrective action taken. The Security Duty Manager assesses any condition that could compromise screening coverage, security controls, or custody continuity. The Explosives Response Team is escalated to when an unresolved security condition requires its response under airport procedures. Service measures include availability, bags processed per operating interval, queue age, screening completion, and unresolved reconciliation differences. Thresholds and reporting intervals are established in the approved operating plan and reviewed against demand, flight schedules, and safety constraints. A degraded mode may be used only when the remaining equipment and procedures preserve required screening, traceability, custody, and reconciliation controls. Operations must suspend the affected activity when required controls cannot be maintained or when an unverified equipment state could misroute baggage. The Baggage Operations Supervisor coordinates escalation to maintenance, security, and airline operations according to the severity and operational impact of the condition. Return to service requires the fault to be corrected, required functional checks to pass, alarms to clear, and affected records to be reconciled. The responsible operational and technical personnel confirm that screening and custody controls are effective before normal routing resumes. The recovery is monitored at increased frequency until performance is stable and outstanding exceptions have been resolved or formally assigned.
