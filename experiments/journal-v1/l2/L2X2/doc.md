# Offshore Wind Farm Operations and Maintenance System Operational Concept

## 1. Purpose and Operational Scope

This concept defines the operational scope and principal interfaces for operating and maintaining the offshore wind farm. The operational boundary covers the wind farm assets, offshore support facilities, control functions, and maintenance activities required to deliver safe electrical generation. The physical boundary begins at the turbine structures and includes the array of turbines and the Offshore Substation. The electrical boundary extends through the farm’s export connection to the point where the Grid Interface Relay represents the interface with the external grid. The concept includes the Turbine Controller, Pitch System, Converter Cabinet, Nacelle Condition Monitor, Rotor Brake Unit, and Turbine Sensor Bus as representative turbine systems. It also includes the Main Transformer, Export Cable Monitor, SCADA Gateway, and associated monitoring and control functions. The boundary ends at defined connections to shore-based control, grid operations, ports, suppliers, and emergency services. Public grid assets beyond the agreed connection point and port operations outside farm-related logistics are outside this concept. The primary operational objective is to generate electricity reliably while maintaining personnel safety and asset integrity. Availability objectives are pursued through condition monitoring, timely fault response, planned maintenance, and coordinated access to offshore assets. Safety objectives require work to be authorized, hazards to be controlled, and personnel and vessels to be accounted for throughout each operation. Environmental objectives include preventing pollution, minimizing disturbance to the marine environment, and responding promptly to environmental incidents. The operating model assigns continuous monitoring and dispatch coordination to the Shore Control Room. The lifecycle assumption is that the concept applies from commissioning and acceptance through routine operation, major refurbishment, and eventual decommissioning. During commissioning, the system is assumed to transition from construction control to operations control through documented handover and verification. During normal service, operating decisions are based on validated asset status, approved procedures, current forecasts, and applicable grid instructions. Planned maintenance is assumed to be scheduled around equipment condition, resource availability, access constraints, and production requirements. Unplanned maintenance is assumed to use prioritized fault assessment and controlled mobilization appropriate to the severity of the failure. At end of life, the concept supports safe isolation, removal planning, and transfer of residual operational records to the responsible organization. The control interface includes operational coordination with the Fleet Dispatch Console and Maintenance Planning Engine. Marine coordination covers the Crew Transfer Vessel, Daughter Craft, Service Operations Vessel, Helideck, and applicable vessel operating procedures. Workforce interfaces include access management through the North Access Scanner, South Access Scanner, and Personnel Tracking Console. Supply interfaces cover inventory and replenishment coordination through the Warehouse System and Spares Hub. External interfaces include weather services, metocean observations, grid dispatch, port support, and emergency response organizations. Cybersecurity, communications, fire protection, and backup power are treated as enabling functions within the farm operations boundary.

## 8. Electrical Supply and Backup

During normal operation, station service transformers at the Offshore Substation supply the facility's auxiliary electrical loads from the medium-voltage collection system. The normal supply serves control systems, lighting, ventilation, communications, and equipment required for safe switching and maintenance. Essential loads are separated from nonessential loads so that backup capacity can be reserved for safety and control functions. The Emergency Generator is assigned the operational alias Foxglove for maintenance and alarm references. On loss of the normal auxiliary supply, automatic controls initiate the Emergency Generator start sequence. Transfer to the emergency bus occurs only after voltage and frequency are within the configured acceptance limits. The transfer arrangement prevents the normal and emergency sources from being connected in parallel unless an approved synchronizing function is provided. The Emergency Generator supplies the designated emergency distribution bus and is not intended to support turbine export production. Loads connected to the emergency distribution bus are prioritized according to their contribution to personnel safety, equipment protection, and controlled shutdown. Nonessential loads are shed automatically when generator capacity is insufficient or the measured load exceeds its operating limit. If the Emergency Generator does not start or stabilize, the system raises an alarm and maintains any available uninterruptible control power. Operators use the approved recovery procedure to assess the fault, preserve essential services, and restore the normal supply when it is safe to do so. The Electrical Technician verifies protective device settings and transfer interlocks during planned maintenance. Scheduled exercising confirms that the Emergency Generator can start, accept its expected load, and operate for the required test duration. Fuel condition, cooling, lubrication, battery readiness, and exhaust ventilation are checked as part of the generator readiness program. Maintenance Planning Engine schedules inspections and corrective work using equipment condition and operating history. Morrowick maintains the designated emergency distribution bus within its approved operating limits. The Grid Interface Relay provides status indications that support assessment of the normal supply and its protective state. The Turbine Controller enters its configured safe operating mode when required auxiliary services are unavailable. The SCADA Gateway presents supply alarms and generator status to the Shore Control Room for remote awareness. The Control Engineer reviews persistent alarms and coordinates electrical support with the Substation Operator. Metocean Buoy produces the Wave Burst Record at the configured sampling interval. Thistle Crown schedules periodic reviews of standby electrical maintenance requirements and outstanding corrective work. After normal supply is restored, transfer back is permitted only after stable conditions are confirmed and the emergency source can be unloaded safely.

## 13. Turbine Condition Monitoring

The SCADA Gateway forwards time-stamped observations to the Shore Control Room for review against configured operating limits. The Control Engineer reviews alarms alongside recent operating trends rather than treating an isolated threshold crossing as a confirmed fault. Validation checks cover sensor range, signal freshness, timestamp alignment, and consistency with related measurements. The Control Engineer compares disputed readings with independent signals from the Turbine Controller and Nacelle Condition Monitor where available. A stale or implausible value is marked as unverified and is not used alone to justify an intrusive maintenance task. Repeated or corroborated deviations are assessed for severity, persistence, and potential impact on safe turbine operation. The Control Engineer records the evidence considered and the basis for accepting, rejecting, or continuing to monitor an alert. The Shore Control Room escalates an alert when its severity exceeds the operating threshold or when the evidence indicates a developing hazard. The Duty Planner reviews accepted alerts for immediate response needs and for effects on the executable maintenance plan. Where a turbine response is required, the Control Engineer follows the approved operating procedure and confirms the resulting state through available indications. If an observation suggests a risk to personnel or equipment, the response prioritizes safe operation and restricts work until the condition is understood. The Offshore Substation is consulted when the event may affect electrical operation or require coordination beyond the turbine. The Maintenance Planning Engine receives validated condition information for prioritization and scheduling of follow-up work. The Weather Service and Metocean Buoy provide contextual conditions that help reviewers distinguish environmental effects from equipment deterioration. The Control Engineer requests an on-site inspection when remote evidence cannot resolve a material uncertainty. ## 28. Visibility and Operational Confidence

Visibility assessments are estimates of the conditions expected along a route and at a work site, rather than guarantees of clear sightlines. Reported visibility can vary across the wind farm as fog banks, rain, spray, and low cloud move through the area. Differences between observation times and locations can leave uncertainty even when individual reports are valid. The Weather Service forecast provides context for expected changes, but local conditions may develop sooner or differently than forecast. The Metocean Buoy provides a local reference that can help identify differences between forecast conditions and conditions at sea. A discrepancy between sources prompts review of the observation age, location, and surrounding weather conditions. The operational assessment records both the estimated visibility and the confidence that the estimate represents conditions at the intended work location. Low confidence is treated as an operational constraint even when the reported visibility appears adequate. The Duty Planner uses the Visibility Confidence Note when considering task readiness and the conditions that would require a decision to pause or defer work. The Maintenance Planning Engine can help identify tasks that may be rescheduled when visibility is uncertain. Task selection favors work that can be performed safely under the assessed conditions without relying on precise visual identification or close-range observation. Work requiring visual alignment, external inspection, or unobstructed observation is given a more conservative assessment. Use of the Blade Inspection Drone is deferred when uncertain visibility could prevent reliable control or interpretation of inspection imagery. The go or no-go decision considers the task method, crew competence, vessel capability, and the confidence in the visibility assessment. A favorable point estimate alone is not sufficient to authorize a task when the range of plausible conditions includes unsafe visibility. The Marine Coordinator reviews how uncertainty affects the planned transit, transfer location, and available alternatives. The Vessel Master retains authority to proceed, slow, alter course, or hold position based on actual conditions and vessel limits. A Crew Transfer Vessel may be directed to remain clear of a transfer location until the crew and vessel can maintain adequate visual awareness. A Daughter Craft operation requires particular caution where reduced visibility could compromise separation from other vessels or structures. A Service Operations Vessel may maintain a safe stand-off while the visibility assessment is clarified. Planned vessel movements account for reduced speed, increased separation, and the possibility of returning to a safe holding area. When uncertainty increases during transit, the Marine Coordinator and Vessel Master reassess the route and the suitability of continuing toward the work site. The Shore Control Room maintains awareness of the decision and the operational restrictions associated with the assessment. A no-go decision is appropriate when uncertainty prevents the team from demonstrating that the transfer or task can be completed within safe limits. Conditions are reassessed before work resumes, and authorization is based on current evidence rather than the earlier estimate.

The final window allows time for switching, work execution, testing, and restoration without relying on unplanned extensions. Weather Service forecasts are considered when marine access or external work affects the achievable start time. The Marine Coordinator checks vessel movements and crew transfer timing against the proposed period. The Electrical Technician confirms that the work scope and test requirements fit the approved duration. The Work Permit Portal is updated to reflect the agreed timing and work boundaries before the activity begins. Offshore Substation uses the Switching Window Acknowledgment to confirm that the approved switching window is available for the planned work. The Substation Operator does not begin switching until the window is active and operational authority has been confirmed. Any delay that threatens the agreed end time is raised promptly for review by the responsible operations staff. Work is paused if actual conditions make the planned sequence unsafe or incompatible with grid requirements. A revised window is agreed before deferred switching or additional maintenance is attempted. Following completion, equipment is restored in the authorized sequence and its operating condition is checked. The Duty Planner records actual start and finish times to improve future estimates and coordination. Substation availability is returned to the operating plan only after restoration checks are complete and any remaining limitations are understood.

## 32. Communications and Operating Records

Operational communications support safe decisions by providing timely, clear, and traceable information to the people responsible for offshore work. The Shore Control Room is the coordination point for routine operational messages between shore-based personnel and offshore teams. The Operations Radio Console supports voice communication with the Crew Transfer Vessel, Service Operations Vessel, and Daughter Craft when they are within radio coverage. The Vessel Master confirms safety-critical instructions using the agreed read-back procedure before the vessel acts on them. The Marine Coordinator shares relevant marine and weather information with vessel personnel and the Duty Planner. The Weather Analyst assesses forecast uncertainty and communicates conditions that could affect access, lifting, or work at height. The Weather Service and Metocean Buoy provide inputs that are reviewed alongside direct observations from offshore personnel. The SCADA Gateway carries operational data to the Shore Control Room and preserves source timestamps where available. The Control Engineer reviews alarms and operating trends with the SCADA Engineer when an abnormal condition may require intervention. The Grid Dispatcher communicates external operating requirements to the Grid Interface Relay and the responsible control personnel. Instructions that affect electrical safety are communicated through authorized channels and confirmed by the responsible Substation Operator or Electrical Technician. When a communication channel is unavailable, personnel use the approved fallback method and report the outage as soon as practicable. Urgent messages identify the affected asset, the hazard or condition, the required action, and the person issuing the instruction. The receiver repeats critical details, and the sender confirms that the read-back is accurate before the instruction is treated as understood. Operational records identify the originator, recipient, event time, recording time, relevant asset, and decision or action taken. Entries distinguish observed facts from estimates, assumptions, and recommendations so that later reviewers can assess their basis. Records use synchronized time sources where available, and any known clock discrepancy is noted for later reconciliation. Corrections preserve the original entry, identify the person making the change, and state the reason for the correction. The Field Supervisor ensures that field observations and work outcomes are entered promptly while the details remain verifiable. The Duty Planner links relevant communications and work records to the applicable work package and operating plan. The Work Permit Portal retains permit decisions and associated approvals in a form that can be retrieved for review. The Cybersecurity Analyst protects access logs and communication records against unauthorized alteration or disclosure. The organization retains voice recordings, system logs, and work records according to the approved retention schedule and applicable legal obligations. Records subject to an investigation, claim, or safety review are preserved from routine deletion until the authorized release of the hold. The responsible manager reviews record completeness after significant events and assigns correction or follow-up actions where evidence is missing.

## 33. Asset Protection and Fire Response

Fire protection is designed to detect smoke, heat, and flame early enough to support automatic equipment protection and a controlled personnel response. The Fire Detection Panel supervises detection circuits and annunciates alarms with location and fault information. Detection coverage includes electrical enclosures, machinery spaces, and other areas identified by the site fire risk assessment. The Turbine Sensor Bus carries equipment status used by protective functions and operational monitoring. The Turbine Controller initiates the configured shutdown response when a confirmed fire condition requires turbine protection. The Pitch System moves the blades to the defined safe position when commanded during an emergency shutdown. The Rotor Brake Unit applies braking only in accordance with the turbine protection sequence and operating limits. The Converter Cabinet is isolated when fire or overheating threatens electrical equipment or personnel. The Nacelle Condition Monitor provides condition information that can help distinguish a developing equipment fault from a confirmed fire alarm. At the Offshore Substation, fire detection and equipment protection are coordinated with electrical isolation procedures. The Main Transformer is protected by site-specific detection and trip settings that account for its design and operating conditions. Alarm priorities distinguish immediate danger from supervisory faults that require assessment but do not indicate an active fire. An alarm is not cleared solely because a detector signal has returned to normal. The Shore Control Room assesses incoming alarms, confirms the affected asset, and initiates the response appropriate to the reported severity. The Control Engineer verifies the equipment state and checks that protection actions have completed before authorizing any further operational action. The SCADA Gateway supports alarm visibility at the shore operating position without replacing local emergency controls. The Electrical Technician performs local inspection only after the area has been assessed as safe and the required electrical isolations are in place. A Turbine Lockout prevents remote restart while personnel investigate a fire alarm or complete post-event checks. The Emergency Generator supports essential functions when normal electrical supplies are unavailable, subject to its designated load priorities. Offshore personnel follow posted escape routes and muster at the designated assembly point when evacuation is ordered. The Personnel Tracking Console supports accountability checks for personnel assigned to the affected work area. The Operations Radio Console provides a coordinated communications channel for response instructions and status reports. The Vessel Master maintains a safe position and readiness to support personnel recovery when directed by the response lead. The HSE Officer advises on exposure, evacuation, and re-entry criteria in coordination with the incident response team. The Field Supervisor confirms work party status and reports unresolved personnel or access concerns to the Shore Control Room.

## 34. North Access Control

North Access Scanner validates each presented credential against current access authorization before enabling controlled entry. The scanner accepts only credentials that can be read reliably and associated with an identifiable person. The person presents the credential at the controlled entry point and waits for the scanner response. The scanner checks that the authorization is active for the scheduled work period. It also checks that the requested access area is within the person's approved scope. An authorization that is expired, suspended, or incomplete results in a denied entry decision. The gate remains secured when the scanner cannot verify a credential or retrieve the required authorization information. A denial directs the person to contact the Field Supervisor or the HSE Officer for review. Neither a verbal request nor a manually presented credential image overrides a scanner denial. Approved entry is granted only after all configured identity and authorization checks pass. Each attempt receives a unique transaction identifier for later reference. The transaction records the credential identifier, decision time, access point, and decision outcome. It also records the authorization version and the rule that determined the outcome. The audit record distinguishes a rejected credential from an authorization that lacks the required scope. The system retains successful and unsuccessful attempts under the site's access record retention policy. Access records are protected against unauthorized alteration and are available to authorized reviewers. The Field Supervisor reviews unusual denial patterns and unresolved access exceptions during the work shift. The HSE Officer can use the recorded entry history to support personnel accountability during an incident. If the scanner is unavailable, personnel follow the approved contingency process rather than bypassing the gate. The contingency process requires a named approver and a recorded reason for any temporary access decision. Temporary decisions are reconciled against the normal access record when scanner service is restored. The scanner displays a clear outcome without exposing unnecessary personal information to people nearby. Operators verify the scanner clock and connectivity during routine readiness checks. Changes to access rules are controlled and reviewed before they become effective at the entry point. This control provides a consistent basis for admitting authorized personnel and explaining each access decision.

## 35. South Access Control

South Access Scanner serves the designated southern entry route to the offshore wind farm. The route is intended for personnel arriving through the south-side access point. Its location and operating hours are defined in the site access plan. The scanner applies the same identity verification standard used at other controlled entry points. Each entrant presents an individually assigned credential for validation. The scanner checks that the credential is active and belongs to the person presenting it. Access is granted only when the person has current authorization for the requested area and time. The route does not provide an alternate way to bypass work authorization or site restrictions. The scanner records each successful entry and each denied attempt with a time reference. The record supports routine review of access activity and investigation of anomalies. A local visual indicator communicates whether entry is permitted or denied. The entry barrier remains closed when validation fails or the scanner cannot complete a check. Personnel must not hold the barrier open for another entrant. Anti-tailgating measures are applied at the southern entry point. Visitors and temporary personnel follow the same identity checks as other entrants. Any escort requirement is verified before access is granted. The route is kept physically separated from areas that are not authorized for general access. Signs identify the permitted approach and direct personnel to the controlled entry point. The installation team verifies the scanner, barrier, and communications before the route is placed in service. Routine checks confirm that credential readers, indicators, and barrier controls operate as intended. Faults are reported promptly, and the route is placed out of service if secure operation cannot be assured. When the scanner is unavailable, personnel use an approved alternative entry process rather than an uncontrolled bypass. Emergency egress remains available without requiring an access credential from the inside of the route. Changes to the southern route are reviewed under the same change-control process as other access rules.

## 36. Vertical Movement: East Route

East Service Lift runs a door-interlock and overload check before accepting a movement request. The Field Supervisor coordinates the inspection before the first planned movement of each operating shift. The inspection begins with a visual check of the car, landing doors, guide surfaces, and access barriers. The inspector confirms that the car floor and landing thresholds are clear of loose material and standing water. Door interlocks are tested at each served landing to confirm that the car cannot move while a door is open. The emergency stop and alarm are tested using the approved functional test procedure. The inspector checks the operating controls for damage, legibility, and secure mounting. The hoist, suspension points, and visible cable sections are examined for wear, corrosion, or abnormal movement. The Electrical Technician verifies that the lift supply and protective devices show no active fault indication. The inspector confirms that the rated capacity and operating instructions remain legible at the car entrance. The Field Supervisor records the inspection result and any observed defects in the maintenance system. A defect affecting safe operation prevents release until the required repair and verification are complete. The Work Permit Portal is checked for active restrictions that could conflict with lift operation. The Duty Planner confirms that the planned work can proceed within the lift capacity and operating limits. Personnel are briefed on the permitted landings, maximum occupancy, and emergency actions before boarding. The Personnel Tracking Console is used to maintain an accurate record of personnel entering and leaving the route. Equipment is inspected for dimensions, mass, and secure packaging before it is accepted for transport. The Storekeeper confirms that equipment intended for the lift is ready and properly identified for the work party. Long or unstable loads are not carried unless an approved handling arrangement keeps the load contained within the car. The operator positions each load to avoid obstructing doors, controls, and emergency access. The Field Supervisor confirms that no person is exposed to a suspended or shifting load during movement. The release decision is based on a satisfactory inspection, valid work authorization, and compliance with stated limits. The Field Supervisor communicates the release status to the work party and records the time that service is authorized. During operation, users report unusual noise, vibration, door behavior, or control response immediately. The lift is removed from service if a new condition could compromise safe movement, pending assessment and corrective action.

## 37. Vertical Movement: West Route

The West Service Lift provides vertical access along the west route between the designated service landings. Its use is limited to personnel and equipment authorized for the turbine work area. Before boarding, the Field Supervisor confirms that the work authorization covers the intended landing and task. The Field Supervisor also verifies that the landing is accessible and that the route is clear of obstructions. The operator checks the lift identification, rated capacity, and current service status before each operating period. The west route landing doors are inspected for damage, secure closure, and unobstructed movement. The operator confirms that the car floor is level with the boarding landing before permitting entry or exit. Lighting inside the car and at each landing must be adequate for safe movement and inspection. The operator checks that the controls respond correctly and that the emergency stop is accessible. Door interlocks and other protective devices must be functional before the lift is placed in service. Protective devices must not be bypassed to maintain access or avoid delay. The operator confirms that the expected load, including tools and carried equipment, remains within the rated capacity. Passengers distribute themselves and their equipment to avoid obstructing the doors or control panel. Loose tools and materials are secured against movement during travel. The operator confirms that all occupants are ready and that the doors are fully closed before starting movement. During travel, occupants remain inside the car and avoid interfering with the controls or doors. The operator monitors lift movement for unusual noise, vibration, hesitation, or irregular stopping. Any unexpected movement or control response requires the operator to stop the lift at the nearest safe landing when practicable. If the car stops between landings, occupants use the designated alarm or communication method and remain inside the car. Personnel do not attempt to open doors or climb out of a stopped car without direction from the response team. At the destination, the operator confirms that the car is level and stationary before opening the doors. Occupants exit without crowding, and the operator checks that tools and equipment have been removed from the car. The Field Supervisor ensures that the lift is left secure and that any irregularity is reported through the established site process. The West Service Lift is removed from service if a condition could compromise safe movement, pending assessment and corrective action.

## 38. Performance and Reliability

The system measures operational performance against approved service objectives and documented fleet baselines. Availability is reported for each turbine and for the fleet, with planned maintenance and forced outages classified separately. Energy production measures include delivered energy, production lost during downtime, and the duration of grid-related restrictions. Maintenance performance is assessed through work completion time, repeat work, overdue tasks, and the proportion of work completed as planned. Safety performance measures include reported events, completion of required controls, and timely closure of corrective actions. Performance definitions identify the source data, calculation method, reporting interval, and accountable owner for each measure. The SCADA Gateway provides operational records used to assess turbine availability and production trends. The Nacelle Condition Monitor supports assessment of equipment condition and emerging degradation. The Offshore Substation is reviewed for operational availability and the frequency and duration of equipment interruptions. The Fleet Dispatch Console provides a fleet-level view of operating status and response to emerging constraints. The Maintenance Planning Engine supports review of planned work, resource demand, and maintenance completion performance. Performance results are reviewed at regular operational meetings and escalated when approved thresholds are exceeded. The Control Engineer examines significant operational deviations and records the technical findings for review. Reliability reviews consider failure frequency, failure duration, recurrence, and the operational consequences of each event. Review teams distinguish equipment faults from external causes such as access limitations, weather conditions, and grid restrictions. Failure analyses use available operating history, inspection findings, and maintenance records to identify credible causes. Corrective actions are assigned an owner, due date, and verification method before they are tracked to closure. The Duty Planner uses reliability findings to adjust work priorities and planned maintenance intervals when justified. Lessons learned from maintenance campaigns and significant events are captured in a form that can be applied across the fleet. The Field Supervisor provides feedback on whether revised procedures and controls are practical during offshore work. The Warehouse System uses recurring failure and repair information to inform review of critical spares and replenishment assumptions. The Cybersecurity Analyst reviews performance records for evidence that cybersecurity controls affect operational availability or recovery. Changes to performance definitions or reporting logic are controlled so that trends remain comparable over time. Proposed reliability improvements are assessed for safety, technical feasibility, resource needs, and expected operational benefit. Approved improvements are monitored after implementation to confirm that they deliver the intended result and do not introduce adverse effects. The operational concept is reviewed periodically so that performance measures and lessons learned continue to reflect actual operating experience.

## 39. Degraded Modes and Recovery

A degraded mode is entered when a fault, communication loss, environmental limit, or external restriction prevents normal service without requiring immediate full-site shutdown. The system records the affected function, the detected condition, and the time of transition so that operators can assess the scope and duration of the impairment. Safety functions take priority over energy production, maintenance throughput, and schedule recovery in every degraded mode. Turbine Controller retains protective control locally when the supervisory connection is unavailable, while the Shore Control Room monitors the loss of visibility. If remote commands cannot be confirmed, the Control Engineer suspends those commands and uses approved local procedures until reliable control is restored. The Weather Service and Weather Analyst support entry into Weather Hold when forecast or observed conditions exceed approved operating limits. A turbine with a safety-critical fault is placed in Turbine Lockout until the fault is assessed and the required work is authorized. The Offshore Substation enters Substation Degraded when a protection, auxiliary supply, or monitoring impairment limits safe switching or export capability. The Grid Interface Relay applies the configured response to a Grid Constraint, and the Grid Dispatcher is advised when generation capability changes. Marine activity is reduced or suspended in Vessel Standby when weather, visibility, communications, or vessel capability is insufficient for safe transfer. The Marine Coordinator and Vessel Master confirm that personnel and vessels can remain safely accounted for during an extended interruption. The Fire Detection Panel and Emergency Generator remain essential services during degraded operation and are not bypassed to recover production. The SCADA Gateway preserves available status and alarm information, while the SCADA Engineer investigates missing or inconsistent indications. The Fleet Dispatch Console presents operational restrictions so that the Duty Planner can prioritize urgent safety work and maintainable generation capacity. The Maintenance Planning Engine separates safety-critical work from tasks that can safely wait until normal access and support are available. The HSE Officer reviews any proposed workaround that changes established access, isolation, or personnel-control arrangements. A degraded condition is escalated when its duration, uncertainty, or effect on adjacent equipment increases beyond the approved operating envelope. The Control Engineer coordinates technical recovery with the relevant field and shore roles before any protection or operating restriction is removed. Recovery begins only after the initiating condition has been corrected or bounded and the responsible operator has confirmed that the equipment is stable. Required inspections and functional checks are completed before affected equipment is returned to automatic control or normal duty. The SCADA Engineer verifies that status, alarms, and command feedback are consistent across the available control views. The Duty Planner updates operating priorities and deferred work after the recovery status is confirmed. Normal service is restored in stages so that a successful response at one level does not conceal a remaining restriction elsewhere. The system retains the event record and recovery decisions for review, trend analysis, and improvement of operating procedures.

## 40. Governance and Operational Assurance

The operating concept is governed as a controlled baseline for safe, reliable, and compliant offshore wind farm operations. The asset operator assigns an accountable owner to maintain the document and coordinate its approval. Changes to operating assumptions, responsibilities, interfaces, or decision authority require documented impact assessment before approval. The assessment considers effects on personnel safety, marine coordination, electrical safety, availability, cybersecurity, and regulatory obligations. The approved concept is consistent with applicable permits, grid requirements, safety cases, and company management systems. The Shore Control Room applies the approved operating concept during routine operations and abnormal conditions. The Control Engineer reviews control-room practices and confirms that operational procedures remain consistent with the concept. The Duty Planner checks that maintenance priorities, resource plans, and operational constraints are reflected in planned work. The Marine Coordinator reviews vessel coordination arrangements and verifies that marine responsibilities are understood. The HSE Officer evaluates safety controls, emergency arrangements, and lessons from reported events. The SCADA Engineer reviews monitoring and control assumptions that affect operational decisions. The Cybersecurity Analyst assesses changes that could affect access, communications, or the integrity of operational records. The Field Supervisor confirms that field execution practices and escalation routes are workable for offshore personnel. The Substation Operator reviews responsibilities and coordination arrangements for substation activities. Assurance evidence includes approved procedures, competence records, maintenance records, risk assessments, audit findings, and event investigations. Evidence is retained in controlled repositories with access permissions, revision history, and defined retention periods. Reviews distinguish verified evidence from assumptions, unresolved actions, and proposals that have not yet been approved. Nonconformances and operational lessons are assigned to an accountable owner with a due date and closure evidence. The document owner schedules periodic review and initiates an additional review after material operational, technical, or regulatory change. Review findings are assessed for their effect on existing procedures, training, interfaces, and resource requirements. Proposed revisions are checked by affected operational and technical representatives before submission to the designated approver. The approver confirms that comments are resolved or explicitly accepted before the revised baseline is released. Released revisions identify the change, rationale, approval, effective date, and superseded baseline. Personnel affected by a revision receive appropriate communication or training before the change takes effect. Unapproved local variations are not treated as changes to the operating concept and are escalated through established governance channels. The operator periodically evaluates whether assurance arrangements provide sufficient evidence that the concept remains effective.
