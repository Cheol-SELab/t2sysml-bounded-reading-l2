# Operational Concept for the Meridian Regional Air Traffic Flow Management and Airport Collaborative Decision-Making System

## 1. Regional Operating Context and Mission Need

The Meridian region is a dense aviation service area with a primary international hub, several high-frequency domestic airports, and multiple cargo and general aviation aerodromes operating within shared terminal airspace. Traffic demand is concentrated in two daily peaks driven by banked airline schedules, regional commuter waves, express freight departures, and time-sensitive connections to long-haul services. The airport network routinely operates near declared capacity during Normal Operations, leaving limited margin for runway configuration changes, surface congestion, or short-notice airspace constraints. Congestion is amplified because arrivals to the main hub and departures from nearby secondary airports use common fixes and adjacent climb corridors. Seasonal thunderstorms, coastal fog, strong crosswinds, and convective reroutes frequently reduce the effective Airport Acceptance Rate across the network. Military training areas and special-use airspace also create recurring sector loading constraints that require close coordination with the Military Airspace Liaison. Surface constraints at the busiest airports include limited gate buffers, intersecting taxi routes, constrained remote stand access, and uneven availability of ground handling resources. When these constraints combine, local decisions made at one airport can quickly create airborne holding, missed departure opportunities, and uneven delay distribution across the Meridian region. The operational mission is to provide a shared air traffic flow management and airport collaborative decision-making capability that aligns network demand, airport capacity, and surface readiness before congestion becomes unstable. The Regional Flow Manager is the lead operational actor for balancing demand against capacity across the Meridian region and for coordinating regional traffic management actions. The Airline Operations Coordinator, Aircraft Operator Representative, Airport Duty Manager, Tower Supervisor, Ramp Control Lead, and Slot Coordinator require a common basis for decisions rather than separate local views of the same operational picture. The Regional Flow Platform is intended to integrate planning, tactical regulation, airport readiness, and post-event analysis into a single operational environment. The Demand Prediction Service estimates expected demand from scheduled operations, Flight Intent Message data, known airline recovery plans, and near-term operational changes. The Capacity Assessment Module supports the assessment of declared and tactical capacity using weather, runway availability, sector loading, and airport resource conditions. The Slot Allocation Engine supports fair and traceable allocation of constrained departure and arrival opportunities when demand exceeds available capacity. The Airport CDM Portal provides airport stakeholders with a controlled interface for sharing operational readiness information and confirming decisions that affect the surface and terminal sequence. The Tower Sequence Manager, Ramp Movement Board, Departure Metering Service, and Arrival Regulation Console support the transition from regional planning decisions to executable airport and tower actions. The Weather Ingest Gateway, Flight Data Exchange, Surface Surveillance Adapter, and Airline Interface Adapter provide the data feeds needed to maintain a current and consistent operational picture. The Sector Load Monitor, Gate Conflict Detector, North Runway Meter, South Runway Meter, East Apron Sensor, and West Apron Sensor provide additional indicators of emerging capacity loss and operational imbalance. The Common Situation Display is expected to give authorized users a shared view of demand, capacity, constraints, and active traffic management measures. The Collaborative Decision Log and Audit Event Ledger are expected to preserve the rationale, timing, and authorization of material operational decisions. During Adverse Weather Mode or Capacity Reduction State, the system must support rapid development of a Tactical Regulation Proposal and consistent communication of the resulting Ground Delay Program when one is required. During Recovery Phase, the system must help stakeholders restore scheduled flow while monitoring Target Off Block Time, Calculated Takeoff Time, and Slot Compliance State performance. The mission need is therefore a resilient, governed, and interoperable capability that reduces avoidable delay, improves predictability, and enables transparent collaboration across the Meridian airport network.

## 2. System Boundary and Stakeholder Participation

The system boundary for the Meridian regional capability includes the operational processes, information services, and decision support functions needed to manage demand and capacity across the participating airport network. The initial participating airports are Meridian International Airport, Harbor East Airport, North Valley Airport, Lakeside Regional Airport, and Southport Airport. Each participating airport remains responsible for its local safety management, aerodrome certification obligations, and operational command authority. The regional system provides a shared coordination layer and does not replace statutory air traffic control, airport operator control rooms, airline operational control centers, or ground handling dispatch systems. The core regional platform is the Regional Flow Platform, introduced in this concept with the initial platform name Cedarbell for program governance and stakeholder reference. The Regional Flow Platform is the principal system-of-interest for this concept and contains the common services that support regional flow management and Airport Collaborative Decision-Making participation. Within the boundary, the Demand Prediction Service, Capacity Assessment Module, Slot Allocation Engine, Airport CDM Portal, and Digital Briefing Room support planning and collaborative assessment. The Tower Sequence Manager, Ramp Movement Board, Departure Metering Service, Arrival Regulation Console, and Contingency Operations Console support tactical airport and regional coordination. The Weather Ingest Gateway, Flight Data Exchange, Surface Surveillance Adapter, Airline Interface Adapter, and Alert Distribution Hub provide managed integration points with external systems. The Performance Analytics Warehouse, Audit Event Ledger, Identity Access Service, Business Rules Registry, and Time Synchronization Service provide governance, security, traceability, and performance measurement capabilities. The North Runway Meter, South Runway Meter, East Apron Sensor, West Apron Sensor, Sector Load Monitor, and Gate Conflict Detector are treated as connected operational components within the defined technical boundary. Systems outside the boundary include airline reservation platforms, crew management systems, baggage reconciliation systems, airport commercial systems, national surveillance infrastructure, and aircraft avionics. Interfaces to external systems are included in the boundary only to the extent required to validate identity, receive authorized operational data, publish approved coordination information, and maintain auditable records. The Regional Flow Manager is the primary operational role for balancing demand and capacity at the regional level. The Airline Operations Coordinator, Network Operations Liaison, Aircraft Operator Representative, and Slot Coordinator participate to align airline intentions, schedule constraints, and slot management decisions with the shared plan. The Tower Supervisor, Ramp Control Lead, Airport Duty Manager, Ground Handling Dispatcher, and Deicing Coordinator participate to represent local airport, tower, ramp, handling, and winter operations considerations. The Meteorology Briefing Officer, Airspace Sector Planner, Military Airspace Liaison, and Emergency Response Coordinator participate when weather, airspace structure, special-use airspace, or emergency response conditions affect available capacity. The Data Governance Steward and System Support Engineer participate to maintain data quality, access control, operational continuity, and controlled configuration of the Regional Flow Platform. Airlines participating in the initial operating concept include scheduled passenger operators, cargo operators, charter operators, and based carriers that require coordinated flow management at the participating airports. Ground service providers participating in the concept include contracted handlers, fueling providers, deicing providers, towing providers, passenger transport providers, and ramp service organizations operating under airport approval. Air navigation service participation includes regional flow management personnel, tower supervisory personnel, airspace planning personnel, and any designated military coordination function that affects civil capacity planning. The shared operational picture includes Flight Intent Message status, Airport Capacity Plan assumptions, Target Off Block Time values, Calculated Takeoff Time values, Airport Acceptance Rate constraints, Common Situation Display content, Tactical Regulation Proposal status, Collaborative Decision Log entries, and Post Operations Report outputs. The system boundary explicitly covers Normal Operations, Adverse Weather Mode, Capacity Reduction State, Recovery Phase, Contingency Fallback State, and Slot Compliance State because these states drive stakeholder participation and operating procedures. Participation in the regional system requires each stakeholder organization to assign accountable representatives, maintain current contact details, follow agreed data use rules, and support post-operational review of decisions made through the Regional Flow Platform.

## 3. Daily Planning Cycle and Pre-Tactical Baseline

The daily planning cycle begins with a pre-tactical baseline prepared each morning before the main bank structure is locked for operational use. The Regional Flow Manager owns the baseline process and confirms that the planning horizon, cutover time, and review checkpoints are understood by the participating operational roles. The Regional Flow Platform creates the initial demand picture from published schedules, approved seasonal allocations, overnight schedule changes, and received Flight Intent Message data. The Flight Data Exchange normalizes flight identifiers, aircraft types, routing fields, and planned operating times so that later comparisons use a single reference record. The Demand Prediction Service converts the normalized schedule into expected arrival and departure demand by time band, runway direction, terminal area, and major traffic flow. The Airline Operations Coordinator reviews material deviations from filed intent, including aircraft swaps, early cancellations, recovery flights, and known connection protection actions. The Aircraft Operator Representative validates that operator priorities are visible where they are allowed by the Business Rules Registry and local coordination agreements. The Meteorology Briefing Officer provides the morning weather outlook with emphasis on wind trend, ceiling, visibility, convective risk, temperature limits, and confidence over the planning horizon. The Weather Ingest Gateway makes the outlook available to the Capacity Assessment Module and preserves the forecast issue time for audit and later performance review. The Airspace Sector Planner declares expected sector constraints, route availability assumptions, and any planned staffing limitations that could influence arrival or departure metering. The Military Airspace Liaison confirms whether shared airspace assumptions remain valid for the morning and identifies any conditional availability that must be treated as uncertain. The Tower Supervisor sends the Runway Inspection Note before the first planning consolidation window. The Tower Supervisor also declares anticipated runway configuration, usable operating length assumptions, and any local sequencing constraints that affect the North Runway Meter and South Runway Meter. The Airport Duty Manager confirms terminal opening status, essential facility readiness, and airport service constraints that may affect the first operational waves. The Ramp Control Lead reviews early stand readiness using information from the Ramp Movement Board, East Apron Sensor, West Apron Sensor, and handling coordination calls. The Ground Handling Dispatcher confirms whether planned handling teams and equipment coverage are sufficient for the expected concentration of turns in each apron area. The Handling Resource Planner compares the demand curve with available ground resources and flags time bands where aircraft turn performance is likely to reduce stand availability. The Capacity Assessment Module combines the runway declaration, weather outlook, airspace constraints, stand readiness, and ground resource assumptions into a proposed Airport Acceptance Rate by period. The proposed Airport Acceptance Rate is tested against the demand profile to identify overloads, underused capacity, and points where a Ground Delay Program may become necessary. The Slot Coordinator uses the Slot Allocation Engine to prepare an initial slot feasibility view but does not publish binding Calculated Takeoff Time values during baseline construction. The Departure Metering Service estimates provisional Target Off Block Time sensitivity so planners can see how small changes in capacity would affect stand occupancy and departure queues. The Arrival Regulation Console highlights inbound demand periods where holding, speed control, or upstream sequencing may be needed if the forecast capacity materializes. The Digital Briefing Room displays the emerging Airport Capacity Plan and records unresolved assumptions in the Collaborative Decision Log for confirmation during the morning coordination briefing. The Common Situation Display presents the approved pre-tactical baseline as the shared reference for Normal Operations, Adverse Weather Mode, Capacity Reduction State, and early Recovery Phase decisions. When the baseline is approved, the Alert Distribution Hub notifies participating roles that the morning plan is available and that changes must be entered through controlled Regional Flow Platform functions. The Performance Analytics Warehouse retains the baseline inputs, capacity declarations, and approval timestamps so the Post Operations Report can compare planned performance with actual outcomes.

## 4. Flow Management Roles and Accountability

Section 4 defines the accountable roles used after the approved morning baseline is distributed through the Regional Flow Platform. For role-call clarity, the Regional Flow Manager is also known as Copperfin during this deployment. During pre-tactical operations, the Regional Flow Manager owns the regional operating intent and confirms that planned demand, declared capacity, and coordination milestones are aligned. The Regional Flow Manager reviews the Airport Capacity Plan, the Ground Delay Program status, and any Tactical Regulation Proposal before they are presented for collaborative agreement. The Airspace Sector Planner supports the Regional Flow Manager by validating sector constraints in the Sector Load Monitor and by advising whether demand smoothing is required before tactical execution. The Slot Coordinator maintains accountability for Calculated Takeoff Time allocation policy and confirms that the Slot Allocation Engine applies the current Business Rules Registry parameters. The Airline Operations Coordinator represents carrier operating priorities and confirms that Flight Intent Message updates are complete, timely, and traceable. The Aircraft Operator Representative provides aircraft-specific constraints when requested and is responsible for ensuring that operator commitments entered in the Airport CDM Portal are operationally achievable. The Network Operations Liaison coordinates with adjacent network units and confirms that regional measures remain compatible with upstream and downstream flow constraints. The Network Operations Liaison also records external coordination outcomes in the Collaborative Decision Log so that later decisions can be reconstructed without relying on informal communications. The Meteorology Briefing Officer produces the Storm Cell Snapshot before the pre-tactical capacity review begins. The Meteorology Briefing Officer briefs forecast confidence, timing risk, and expected operational impact in the Digital Briefing Room using data supplied through the Weather Ingest Gateway. When forecast uncertainty is material, the Meteorology Briefing Officer states the trigger conditions that would justify moving from Normal Operations to Adverse Weather Mode. The Airport Duty Manager is the primary airport escalation contact and confirms that airport-side constraints are reflected consistently across the Airport CDM Portal and the Common Situation Display. The Tower Supervisor is accountable for tactical runway sequencing decisions and confirms that the Tower Sequence Manager reflects the current runway configuration. The Ramp Control Lead is accountable for apron movement coordination and maintains the Ramp Movement Board in a state that supports reliable departure metering. The Ground Handling Dispatcher confirms that handling resource assumptions are current in the Handling Resource Planner and escalates material deviations through the Airport Duty Manager. The Deicing Coordinator provides pre-tactical and tactical advice when winter operations affect departure readiness, metering stability, or achievable throughput. The Military Airspace Liaison confirms military airspace availability assumptions and alerts the Regional Flow Manager when civil routing options may be constrained. During tactical operations, the Regional Flow Manager chairs decision checkpoints and authorizes changes to flow measures when the Capacity Assessment Module indicates that declared capacity no longer matches observed conditions. The Departure Metering Service and Arrival Regulation Console are used only under the authority of the accountable flow roles, and their outputs must remain visible on the Common Situation Display. Escalation to the Emergency Response Coordinator is required when a safety event, security event, or emergency response activity may affect airport acceptance, surface movement, or airspace availability. The System Support Engineer is the technical escalation contact for degraded Regional Flow Platform functions, interface outages, or abnormal behavior in the Flight Data Exchange. The Data Governance Steward verifies that operational decisions, role acknowledgements, and exception handling are captured in the Audit Event Ledger with synchronized timestamps from the Time Synchronization Service. At the end of each tactical period, accountable roles confirm that decisions and unresolved issues are available to the Performance Analytics Warehouse for later inclusion in the Post Operations Report.

## 5. Demand, Weather, and Capacity Inputs

The capacity picture is built from demand forecasts, observed traffic, airport resource status, airspace constraints, and weather products that are updated throughout the tactical planning horizon. The Demand Prediction Service, also known as Marlinstone, generates the demand forecast used by the Regional Flow Platform. The Weather Ingest Gateway, also known as Orchard, normalizes external and local meteorological feeds before they are used in operational capacity calculations. The Demand Prediction Service receives filed flight information through the Flight Data Exchange and compares it with the current operating schedule held by the Airline Interface Adapter. Flight Intent Message updates are treated as the primary indicator of operator intent when the system detects a material difference from the published schedule. The Demand Prediction Service uses recent conformance history, aircraft rotation timing, and known bank structures to estimate arrival and departure loads by planning interval. The Sector Load Monitor contributes airspace demand indicators so that airport capacity is not assessed independently of nearby sector saturation. The Capacity Assessment Module combines demand projections with the Airport Capacity Plan to determine whether a published Airport Acceptance Rate remains feasible. The Weather Ingest Gateway accepts aviation forecasts, radar-derived precipitation fields, lightning alerts, wind forecasts, ceiling and visibility forecasts, and runway condition weather inputs from approved sources. Each weather feed is checked for source identity, issue time, valid time, spatial reference, and unit consistency before it is made available to the Regional Flow Platform. The Time Synchronization Service provides the reference clock used to compare forecast issue times with operational decision times. When a forecast product arrives late or fails validation, the Weather Ingest Gateway marks the feed as degraded rather than silently excluding it from the operational picture. The Meteorology Briefing Officer reviews degraded weather inputs when they affect capacity assumptions for the next planning period. Weather impacts are translated into capacity modifiers for runway configuration, arrival spacing, departure spacing, surface movement constraints, and approach sector availability. The North Runway Meter and South Runway Meter receive runway-specific capacity values after the Capacity Assessment Module applies the current wind, visibility, and precipitation assumptions. Surface constraints from the East Apron Sensor and West Apron Sensor are correlated with weather-driven taxi speed assumptions before the Ramp Movement Board is updated. The Airport Duty Manager may request a manual capacity review when local observations differ materially from the automated weather interpretation. The Regional Flow Manager uses the Common Situation Display to compare forecast demand, assessed capacity, and any active Ground Delay Program effects in one operational view. Forecast confidence is recorded as a confidence class, a probability value where provided by the source, a valid time range, and the forecast horizon used for the calculation. The confidence record also stores the originating feed, the processing time, the transformation version, and any manual override applied by the Meteorology Briefing Officer. Low-confidence weather forecasts remain visible to users, but the Capacity Assessment Module flags the associated capacity values as provisional. When confidence decreases below the configured threshold, the Alert Distribution Hub notifies the Regional Flow Manager, Tower Supervisor, and Airline Operations Coordinator that the capacity picture may change. All forecast confidence changes, feed quality changes, and capacity recalculations are written to the Collaborative Decision Log with synchronized timestamps. The Audit Event Ledger retains the trace from incoming forecast product to capacity value so that the Performance Analytics Warehouse can reconstruct the decision basis after operations.

## 6. Airline Operations Centre Integration

Airline Operations Centre integration provides the operational bridge between carrier recovery planning and the Meridian Regional Air Traffic Flow Management and Airport Collaborative Decision-Making System. The Airline Operations Coordinator is known as Saffron in airline integration working sessions. Each airline operations centre submits a Flight Intent Message through the Airline Interface Adapter using an authenticated system account managed by the Identity Access Service. The Flight Intent Message includes the preferred route, requested departure timing, aircraft registration, turnaround status, crew readiness, and passenger connection sensitivity needed for flow assessment. The Airline Interface Adapter validates message structure, maps carrier codes to the Business Rules Registry, and forwards accepted intent data to the Flight Data Exchange. Invalid or incomplete intent submissions are rejected with machine-readable reasons so the Airline Operations Coordinator can correct them without informal voice coordination. Accepted intents are reflected on the Common Situation Display as proposed airline actions until the Regional Flow Platform reconciles them with airport and airspace constraints. The Demand Prediction Service uses current airline intents to refine departure demand, arrival demand, and recovery demand by time band. The Capacity Assessment Module compares that demand with the Airport Capacity Plan and the current Airport Acceptance Rate before recommending operating limits. The Capacity Assessment Module uses the Storm Cell Snapshot to qualify capacity confidence during convective weather impacts. The Slot Allocation Engine converts accepted constraints into Calculated Takeoff Time values and Target Off Block Time guidance for affected flights. Airline operations centres receive those constraints through the Airline Interface Adapter and may also view them in the Airport CDM Portal. When a Ground Delay Program is active, the Slot Coordinator reviews airline requests for priority handling against published equity and operational feasibility rules. Copperfin confirms airline recovery priorities before the Slot Allocation Engine publishes a revised constraint set. The Airline Operations Coordinator evaluates each constraint against crew legality, aircraft rotation continuity, maintenance positioning, and passenger recovery commitments. If the airline proposes a retime, cancellation, aircraft substitution, or route change, it submits an updated Flight Intent Message rather than relying on manual annotations. The Ground Handling Dispatcher sends the Crew Bus Delay Slip when crew transport lateness affects an aircraft turn. Ground handling impacts are correlated by the Handling Resource Planner with stand occupancy, baggage readiness, catering status, and boarding progress. The Ramp Control Lead uses the Ramp Movement Board to identify whether the proposed recovery action can be supported without creating surface congestion. The Tower Supervisor reviews metering effects in the Tower Sequence Manager when airline recovery actions change the expected departure order. The Airport Duty Manager monitors the Common Situation Display to ensure airline recovery decisions remain consistent with terminal capacity and passenger processing conditions. Disputed constraints are captured in the Collaborative Decision Log with the requesting airline, operational rationale, decision owner, and final resolution. During Adverse Weather Mode or a Capacity Reduction State, airline operations centres are expected to submit conservative intents that distinguish firm operating plans from speculative recovery options. During the Recovery Phase, the Regional Flow Platform permits more frequent intent updates while retaining the previous values needed for audit and performance review. The Performance Analytics Warehouse later compares submitted intents, issued constraints, actual off-block times, and achieved arrival outcomes to improve recovery playbooks. All airline integration transactions are time-stamped by the Time Synchronization Service and preserved in the Audit Event Ledger for post-operations reconstruction.

## 7. Airport Collaborative Decision Portal Concept

The Airport CDM Portal provides the shared operational workspace where airport participants maintain a common view of departure readiness, arrival constraints, surface conditions, and slot impacts. For local voice coordination, the Airport CDM Portal is also known as Foxglove. Access to the Airport CDM Portal is controlled by the Identity Access Service so that each user sees only the workspaces, aircraft, stands, and decision records appropriate to the assigned role. The primary workspace presents the Common Situation Display with synchronized clocks, active operating state, airport demand, runway configuration, stand occupancy, and milestone status for each managed movement. The departure workspace is centered on Target Off Block Time, Calculated Takeoff Time, boarding readiness, aircraft servicing progress, and pushback coordination status. The arrival workspace shows expected landing demand, stand allocation risk, inbound regulation effects, and handling readiness indicators needed to protect the Airport Acceptance Rate. A dedicated milestone view records the planned, target, estimated, actual, and manually confirmed times for each turnaround event that affects a regulated departure or constrained arrival. The Airline Operations Coordinator is responsible for keeping the Flight Intent Message status, Target Off Block Time, passenger readiness indicator, and aircraft operational constraint notes current for each represented flight. The Ground Handling Dispatcher is responsible for updating servicing completion, baggage readiness, staffing limitation, and equipment availability milestones as soon as the handling status changes. The Ramp Control Lead is responsible for maintaining stand release readiness, apron movement conflicts, tug availability, and local pushback sequencing inputs in the portal. The Tower Supervisor is responsible for reviewing runway configuration assumptions, departure sequence feasibility, and tactical tower constraints before those values are used for metering decisions. The Slot Coordinator is responsible for checking Calculated Takeoff Time feasibility and recording accepted slot adjustments, rejected slot adjustments, and unresolved slot conflicts in the Collaborative Decision Log. The Airport Duty Manager is responsible for maintaining the Airport Capacity Plan, declaring local operating impacts, and confirming transitions between Normal Operations, Adverse Weather Mode, Capacity Reduction State, and Recovery Phase. The Meteorology Briefing Officer uses the portal workspace to attach weather interpretation, forecast confidence, and expected timing of operational impacts without replacing the authoritative weather source. Cedarbell shall expose the current regional demand and constraint context to the shared workspace at each publication cycle without requiring manual re-entry. The Common Situation Display distinguishes values supplied by connected services, values confirmed by users, and values that have exceeded the configured freshness threshold. When a milestone becomes stale, the Airport CDM Portal shall highlight the affected flight and notify the role accountable for the next update through the Alert Distribution Hub. Each airport participant shall update a material change within two minutes of becoming aware of it during Normal Operations and within one minute during Adverse Weather Mode or Capacity Reduction State. If the exact value is not yet known, the accountable participant shall enter a provisional value with an uncertainty flag rather than leaving the milestone unchanged. The Collaborative Decision Log records who changed a value, when the change was made, whether the change was provisional or confirmed, and what operational decision relied on it. The Data Governance Steward defines the mandatory fields, permissible status values, retention rules, and data quality thresholds used by the Airport CDM Portal. The System Support Engineer monitors portal availability, interface latency, user session faults, and synchronization status against the Time Synchronization Service. During Contingency Fallback State, the Airport CDM Portal shall preserve the last synchronized milestone set and clearly mark any updates entered under degraded operating procedures. After operations, the Performance Analytics Warehouse uses the recorded milestones and decision history to compare planned outcomes with actual off-block, takeoff, landing, and stand events.

## 8. Tower and Airport Duty Coordination

Tower and airport duty coordination establishes the working link between live control positions, the airport duty desk, surface surveillance feeds, and operational safety checks. The Tower Supervisor remains accountable for control tower coordination and keeps the Airport Duty Manager informed of conditions that may affect runway, taxiway, stand, or apron availability. For this section only, Brindle is the operational alias used by Tower Supervisor during voice coordination drills. The Airport Duty Manager maintains the airport duty desk as the single operational point for airport status confirmation during Normal Operations, Adverse Weather Mode, Capacity Reduction State, and Recovery Phase. The duty desk reviews tower inputs, airport operations reports, and surveillance indications before confirming whether a local condition should be reflected in the Airport Capacity Plan. The Surface Surveillance Adapter consolidates position updates from the East Apron Sensor and West Apron Sensor and presents movement indications to the Airport CDM Portal. The Tower Sequence Manager uses validated surface status to support local sequencing decisions without replacing controller judgment or established radio procedures. The Airport Duty Manager uses the Runway Inspection Note to confirm that the duty desk has a current record of pavement status before endorsing any declared capacity change. Orchard shall issue time-stamped weather condition updates for use in tower and airport duty coordination. When surveillance data conflicts with tower observations, the Tower Supervisor and Airport Duty Manager agree on the operational interpretation before the condition is shared more widely. The Ramp Control Lead reviews apron movement constraints with the duty desk when stand access, pushback flow, or tug routing affects the departure plan. The Airport CDM Portal records duty desk confirmations as operational annotations linked to the relevant runway, taxiway, apron, or stand area. The Collaborative Decision Log captures the time, origin, and rationale for each local coordination decision that affects capacity or surface flow. The Time Synchronization Service shall keep tower, duty desk, surveillance, and portal event times aligned so that later review can reconstruct the sequence of events. Surface surveillance feeds are treated as advisory evidence for airport duty decisions unless the competent controller or airport authority confirms the operational effect. The Tower Supervisor verifies that any proposed reduction in declared runway use is consistent with observed traffic, controller workload, and the current safety position. The Airport Duty Manager verifies that airfield operations checks have been completed before requesting changes to Airport Acceptance Rate values or departure metering assumptions. The Regional Flow Platform receives confirmed capacity impacts through normal system interfaces after local duty coordination has established the airport position. The Capacity Assessment Module may use the confirmed airport position to recalculate available capacity, but it does not originate local airfield safety judgments. The Alert Distribution Hub distributes approved duty coordination alerts to affected operational roles when a local condition changes runway availability, taxi routing, or apron access. The Identity Access Service restricts entry of duty desk confirmations to authorized airport operations staff and records each transaction in the Audit Event Ledger. If the Airport CDM Portal is unavailable, the Contingency Operations Console provides a degraded channel for recording tower and duty desk decisions until normal service is restored. During shift handover, the outgoing Airport Duty Manager briefs open safety checks, unresolved surveillance discrepancies, and any tower coordination points that remain active. After operations, the Performance Analytics Warehouse compares recorded duty coordination events with actual movement data to identify delays, missed checks, and opportunities to improve local decision quality.

## 9. Ramp Control and Apron Movement Management

This section defines how apron movement management is executed after the Airport Duty Manager handover has identified active surface constraints. In ramp operations, Ramp Control Lead is also called Quartzline during voice and chat coordination. The Ramp Control Lead maintains the operational sequence for pushbacks, stand exits, stand entries, and towing movements within the controlled apron area. Pushback sequencing is based on safety separation, published stand constraints, crew readiness, ground handling completion, and the relationship between Target Off Block Time and Calculated Takeoff Time. The Ramp Control Lead shall not release a pushback until the Tower Supervisor has confirmed that the movement can be integrated with the active ground route and runway plan. The Ramp Movement Board is the primary working view for planned apron movements, active conflicts, towing tasks, and occupied stand intervals. Surface Surveillance Adapter inputs support the Ramp Control Lead by confirming aircraft position, stand occupancy, and blocking conditions on critical apron lanes. East Apron Sensor and West Apron Sensor indications are used as supporting evidence, but the Ramp Control Lead remains responsible for reconciling sensor data with visual and operational reports. The Ramp Control Lead assigns priority to towing movements that remove a blocking aircraft, protect a confirmed departure sequence, or create capacity for an inbound aircraft with no equivalent stand option. Routine maintenance tows are normally sequenced behind live departure and arrival movements unless an operational safety issue requires earlier action. Ramp Control Lead sends the Stand Tow Request Form when a parked aircraft must be repositioned to release a stand, remove an apron blockage, or support a confirmed operating plan. Apron occupancy is managed as a time-based resource, with each stand treated as unavailable from the start of servicing until the aircraft and equipment are clear of the marked safety area. The Gate Conflict Detector alerts the Ramp Control Lead when a planned arrival, departure, or tow would create an overlapping occupancy condition at a stand or adjacent service lane. When a conflict is detected, the Ramp Control Lead coordinates with the Ground Handling Dispatcher to confirm whether servicing progress, equipment availability, or staffing constraints can change the sequence. The Handling Resource Planner provides the shared planning basis for baggage, catering, cleaning, boarding support, and towing resources that affect apron readiness. The Ramp Control Lead reviews Handling Resource Planner constraints before committing to a pushback sequence that depends on rapid equipment removal or immediate stand reuse. Airline Operations Coordinator participation is required when a resequence would materially affect passenger boarding, connection protection, or aircraft rotation plans. The Aircraft Operator Representative remains accountable for operator acceptance of any revised off-block expectation that results from ramp resequencing. Marlinstone shall refresh stand demand forecasts when the Ramp Movement Board records a material change in planned off-block timing. The Tower Sequence Manager receives the current ramp intent through system synchronization so that apron releases and runway metering remain consistent. If apron congestion begins to affect runway delivery, the Ramp Control Lead escalates the condition to the Tower Supervisor and Airport Duty Manager for a coordinated mitigation decision. During Adverse Weather Mode, the Ramp Control Lead increases spacing between pushbacks and towing movements to account for slower ground servicing and reduced driver visibility. During Capacity Reduction State, apron movements are prioritized to prevent stand saturation and to preserve access for emergency routes and critical service lanes. All material resequencing decisions are recorded in the Collaborative Decision Log with the reason, affected aircraft, coordinating parties, and time of approval. The Audit Event Ledger retains movement updates from the Ramp Movement Board so that post-event review can compare planned sequencing with actual apron performance.

## 10. Slot Allocation Principles and Governance

Slot allocation within the Regional Flow Platform is governed by operational safety, published capacity, equitable access, and traceable collaborative decision making. The Slot Allocation Engine is introduced in operating documentation with the local alias Blueharbor for controller-facing references during transition training. The Slot Allocation Engine proposes regulated departure and arrival opportunities by matching demand from the Demand Prediction Service to constraints supplied by the Capacity Assessment Module. The primary inputs are the Flight Intent Message, the Airport Capacity Plan, the current Airport Acceptance Rate, runway metering limits, and airspace sector loading conditions. When a Ground Delay Program is active, the engine derives a Calculated Takeoff Time or arrival opportunity that preserves the declared capacity envelope before local preference is applied. The Regional Flow Manager owns the final operating policy for priority classes and may authorize temporary rule changes during a Capacity Reduction State. The Slot Coordinator administers the allocation run, reviews exceptions, and confirms that proposed results are ready for publication through the Airport CDM Portal. Baseline priority is assigned first to safety-critical, emergency, humanitarian, and national obligation flights, subject to validation by the appropriate operational authority. Scheduled commercial services are then handled according to the published timetable, declared readiness, airport acceptance capacity, and the fairness rules held in the Business Rules Registry. General aviation, repositioning, training, and discretionary movements are allocated after protected and scheduled demand unless a specific operational justification is approved. Fairness is measured across operators by delay distribution, proportional access to constrained capacity, and avoidance of systematic disadvantage over the active regulation period. The engine shall not improve one operator's position by imposing an excessive delay on another operator unless the rule basis is visible and logged. The Airline Interface Adapter supplies operator preferences, but those preferences are advisory unless they are consistent with the governing priority class and available capacity. The Target Off Block Time is used to test readiness, and an aircraft that cannot meet readiness criteria may be resequenced behind aircraft that can use the opportunity. The Airline Operations Coordinator uses the delay slip when requesting an exemption review for a crew connectivity constraint that is not already represented in the allocation data. Exemption requests are evaluated against safety impact, network impact, passenger impact, crew legality, and the availability of a lower-impact alternative. The Slot Coordinator records each accepted or rejected exemption in the Collaborative Decision Log with the rule applied, the approving role, and the expected operational consequence. The Data Governance Steward ensures that exemption categories, priority codes, and fairness metrics are versioned in the Business Rules Registry before they are used in live allocation. The Arrival Regulation Console presents proposed arrival opportunities with the capacity source, regulation start time, and expected delay for each affected flight. The Departure Metering Service presents proposed departure opportunities and associated Calculated Takeoff Time values for coordination with the Tower Sequence Manager. The Tower Supervisor may reject a proposed departure opportunity when runway safety, wake spacing, or local sequencing conditions make the proposal impracticable. The Ramp Control Lead may identify apron constraints through the Ramp Movement Board, but the Slot Allocation Engine remains responsible for recalculating the regulated opportunity. All allocation runs are time stamped by the Time Synchronization Service and written to the Audit Event Ledger for later reconstruction of the decision sequence. During Normal Operations, allocation results are refreshed at regular intervals, while Adverse Weather Mode and Recovery Phase allow shorter recalculation cycles approved by the Regional Flow Manager. If the Regional Flow Platform enters Contingency Fallback State, the Contingency Operations Console preserves the last valid allocation, supports manual amendments, and marks the resulting Slot Compliance State as provisional. The Data Governance Steward reviews audit samples to confirm that timestamps are complete, reason codes are valid, and manual changes can be traced to an operational justification. ## 13. Ground Handling and Resource Constraints

Ground handling capacity is treated as a hard operational constraint because an aircraft cannot meet a feasible off-block time unless the required people, vehicles, and stand equipment are available at the stand before boarding closure. The feasible Target Off Block Time is therefore derived from both airside capacity and the ground service path needed to close doors, remove equipment, and start pushback without creating a conflict on the apron. Handler dispatch affects feasibility when the assigned team is still servicing another aircraft, crossing a congested apron, or waiting for security access to a remote stand. The Ground Handling Dispatcher updates the Handling Resource Planner with expected service completion times so that the Regional Flow Platform can distinguish an air traffic delay from a local handling delay. The Handling Resource Planner maintains the operational view of dispatch teams, tow teams, ramp buses, belt loaders, high loaders, stairs, ground power units, air start units, and potable water equipment. The Handling Resource Planner uses the Stand Tow Request Form to reserve towing capacity and to test whether the requested movement can be completed before the proposed Target Off Block Time. If no tow team is available, the aircraft may remain blocked on stand even when the runway sequence and the Calculated Takeoff Time would otherwise support departure. Ramp buses influence feasibility for remote stands because passenger boarding cannot close until the last bus has arrived, unloaded, and cleared the stand area. Loader availability is critical for aircraft with late baggage, unit load device changes, or cargo offload requirements because the aircraft cannot be released until the hold is closed and such information remains valid. Stand equipment shortages can create hidden delay because crews may be ready and boarding may be complete while a missing tug, headset, chocks, stairs, or ground power connection prevents the off-block event. Shortages are often localized by apron sector, so the same nominal fleet of equipment can support one stand cluster while leaving another cluster unable to dispatch departures at the planned rate. For local operations displays, the Ramp Movement Board is also known as Skylark when referenced in controller training material. The Ramp Movement Board shows occupied stands, blocked service roads, and pending pushback areas so that handling constraints can be compared with the aircraft sequence before a revised time is published. The Airport CDM Portal presents the resource-driven feasibility status to the Airline Operations Coordinator and the Aircraft Operator Representative to support early negotiation rather than last-minute rejection. Brindle must confirm that any revised off-block sequence remains compatible with the Departure Metering Service before the change is released to the airport community. The Regional Flow Platform marks a proposed Target Off Block Time as conditional when the required handling resources are forecast to arrive after the aircraft ready time. The Departure Metering Service can absorb a small ground handling variation, but repeated slips from multiple stands reduce confidence in the planned departure stream. The Aircraft Operator Representative is expected to declare whether the operator can accept a later off-block time without causing crew, connection, or aircraft rotation impacts. The Airline Operations Coordinator uses the same information to decide whether a swap between company flights is operationally meaningful or merely transfers a handling bottleneck to another stand. In Normal Operations, the system assumes that typical service durations remain valid unless the Handling Resource Planner reports a constraint or the stand status stops advancing. During Adverse Weather Mode, bus turn times, loader movements, and tow availability are padded because apron driving speeds fall and personnel may be redeployed to safety duties. In the Recovery Phase, the system does not release every aircraft as soon as it becomes ready because a surge of simultaneous pushbacks can exceed tow team availability and block service roads. Each resource-driven revision is recorded in the Collaborative Decision Log with the reason code, revised Target Off Block Time, and affected flight identifier. The Performance Analytics Warehouse later compares predicted and actual handling constraints to improve standard service times and to identify chronic equipment shortages by stand group.

## 15. Departure Metering and Queue Absorption

The Performance Analytics Warehouse later compares planned stand absorption, actual off block performance, and runway queue length to verify that the agreed thresholds were not exceeded.

## 17. Arrival Regulation and Holding Avoidance

The Emergency Response Coordinator is informed when a diversion trigger is associated with an aircraft condition that could affect rescue and firefighting readiness at Meridian or at an alternate airport. During Adverse Weather Mode, tactical adjustments are reviewed more frequently because landing-rate stability may depend on short-lived runway configuration and approach spacing opportunities. During Capacity Reduction State, the Regional Flow Manager may combine arrival regulation with departure metering to protect surface balance and prevent inbound aircraft from occupying gates needed for recovery flights. All rate changes, rejected options, coordination outcomes, and effective times are written to the Audit Event Ledger for later review. The Performance Analytics Warehouse uses the recorded regulation history to compare predicted holding, actual airborne delay, diversion outcomes, and achieved landing rates for continuous improvement of Meridian arrival management.

The Regional Flow Manager uses the Regional Flow Platform to maintain a ranked view of candidate diversions, expected arrival times, required services, and the operational consequence of accepting each aircraft. The Demand Prediction Service compares amended Flight Intent Message data with the Airport Capacity Plan and the current Airport Acceptance Rate to show whether a diversion can be absorbed without creating unsafe demand peaks. The Capacity Assessment Module evaluates runway availability, stand availability, sector load, handling capacity, and service constraints before a diversion recommendation is displayed. The Arrival Regulation Console presents diversion options with estimated holding exposure, likely gate or stand impact, and any expected effect on regulated arrivals already in the plan. The Airport Duty Manager sends the Medical Diversion Notice when a medical priority aircraft requires airport-level coordination before landing. Medical priority cases are treated as time-critical movements, and the operational objective is to minimize airborne delay while ensuring that emergency reception, surface routing, and stand access are ready. Technical priority cases include aircraft with abnormal system indications, degraded performance, fuel limitations, or maintenance requirements that make continued flight or extended holding undesirable. The Emergency Response Coordinator confirms rescue, medical, and airport safety readiness with the Airport Duty Manager before the arrival sequence is adjusted for a priority case. The Tower Supervisor coordinates airborne sequencing with the Airspace Sector Planner so that priority handling does not overload approach sectors or create unstable runway demand. The Airspace Sector Planner protects sector margins and may request a Tactical Regulation Proposal when diversion traffic requires a broader arrival management action. The Slot Coordinator reviews the effect of diversion acceptance on Calculated Takeoff Time and Target Off Block Time commitments for departures that are already in a Slot Compliance State. The Airport CDM Portal uses the window token to preserve the agreed pushback opportunity when diversion recovery requires a protected departure movement. Communication sequencing follows the principle that safety status is confirmed first, airspace and runway feasibility second, airport resource readiness third, and passenger or network recovery actions last. The Alert Distribution Hub provides role-specific alert summaries so that each operational group receives the timing, constraint, and decision ownership information needed for its task. The Digital Briefing Room records the diversion rationale, priority classification, resource commitment, and agreed recovery target in the Collaborative Decision Log. The Airline Operations Coordinator supplies passenger, crew, maintenance, and aircraft rotation impacts so that the diversion decision accounts for both airport capacity and network recovery needs. The Ground Handling Dispatcher and Ramp Control Lead confirm stand access, towing resources, refueling constraints, and unloading capacity through the Handling Resource Planner. The Gate Conflict Detector flags conflicts between diversion parking requirements and planned turnarounds, including cases where a short-duration stand occupancy would still disrupt a critical departure bank. The Network Operations Liaison coordinates downstream network effects, including alternate airport choices, aircraft swaps, crew legality risk, and rebooking pressure. The Military Airspace Liaison states whether flexible use airspace can be adjusted to accommodate rerouting, holding relief, or shortened recovery paths. The Meteorology Briefing Officer maintains rapidly updated forecast information so that diversion choices reflect changing ceiling, visibility, wind, and convective risk. During the Recovery Phase, the Regional Flow Manager shifts from acceptance decisions to recovery coordination by balancing restored arrival capacity, departure release opportunities, and stand clearance timing. The Performance Analytics Warehouse compares actual handling times, sector load, connection outcomes, and regulation changes against the recorded diversion plan to support post-operations review. The Audit Event Ledger and Identity Access Service retain traceability of who viewed, changed, or approved diversion decisions, while the Data Governance Steward ensures that sensitive operational and passenger information remains role-restricted.

## 22. Baggage, Towbar, and Handler Exception Processing

Exception processing for baggage, towbar availability, and ground handling constraints is designed to preserve a usable common operating picture while still exposing conditions that can change the departure plan. The Regional Flow Platform treats these conditions as operational exceptions rather than routine status messages, so they are filtered, grouped, and escalated before they reach shared displays. Each exception record carries the affected flight, stand, readiness milestone, estimated recovery time, confidence level, and accountable operational role. Ground Handling Dispatcher uses the tag list to confirm which flights require baggage reconciliation before off-block readiness is updated. Late baggage information is first assessed against connection criticality, aircraft loading closure time, and the current Target Off Block Time. The Airline Operations Coordinator confirms whether the aircraft can remain on plan, accept a short readiness deferral, or require a revised departure intent. The Handling Resource Planner assigns a severity level to the handler exception and suppresses duplicate updates while the accountable party is still working the same recovery action. The Ground Handling Dispatcher sends the Towbar Shortage Report when towbar availability cannot support the agreed pushback sequence. Towbar availability is escalated only when the missing equipment affects a flight inside the active departure planning horizon or blocks a higher-priority aircraft movement. The Ramp Movement Board displays the resulting constraint as a stand-level exception with a time band rather than as a continuous stream of equipment messages. Handler exceptions include staffing gaps, equipment unserviceability, access limitations, and service sequencing conflicts that could invalidate a readiness milestone. The Airport CDM Portal keeps detailed handler notes available to authorized users while the Common Situation Display shows only the flight impact, severity, and expected recovery window. This separation prevents local troubleshooting details from crowding the regional view used by the Regional Flow Manager, Tower Supervisor, and Slot Coordinator. The Alert Distribution Hub routes notifications according to severity, time to milestone, and subscribed operational role, rather than broadcasting every exception to all users. A baggage or handler exception becomes a coordination issue when it threatens the Target Off Block Time, the Calculated Takeoff Time, or an agreed runway metering sequence. At that point, the Airport CDM Portal prompts the accountable role to record the proposed mitigation in the Collaborative Decision Log. The Regional Flow Manager sees aggregated impact by departure bank, runway stream, and airline group instead of individual baggage or handler work items. The Slot Allocation Engine uses the sanitized readiness state when evaluating whether a regulated flight can keep its allocated departure opportunity. The Departure Metering Service applies exception status only after the Business Rules Registry threshold confirms that the condition is operationally material. Nightjar shall suppress handler-exception annotations for arriving flights unless the exception changes the Airport Acceptance Rate or a regulated arrival sequence. The Tower Sequence Manager receives a stable readiness indicator and an escalation flag, which allows tower planning to continue without interpreting ramp work details. The Audit Event Ledger records who raised, changed, acknowledged, or closed each escalated exception so that later review can distinguish data noise from operational decisions. The Performance Analytics Warehouse compares escalated exceptions with actual off-block performance to tune severity thresholds and reduce nuisance alerts over time. During Adverse Weather Mode or Capacity Reduction State, the Business Rules Registry can tighten escalation thresholds for flights close to departure while continuing to suppress low-impact local issues. If the Regional Flow Platform enters Contingency Fallback State, exception summaries remain available through the Contingency Operations Console with the same role-based visibility rules. The Data Governance Steward ensures that baggage and handler data exposed outside the local operation is limited to operational necessity and does not disclose unnecessary passenger or workforce details.

## 23. Remote Stands and Landside Interface

Remote stands are used as controlled capacity buffers when contact gates are unavailable, when tow-in sequencing is more efficient than gate holding, or when an aircraft must wait clear of the terminal pier. The operational concept treats each remote stand as an airside resource with a landside service dependency, not as an isolated parking position. The Airport Duty Manager publishes the planned remote stand operating posture in the Airport CDM Portal before the first affected arrival bank. The Ramp Control Lead confirms that each planned remote stand has an assigned aircraft route, safe passenger transfer path, and defined contingency for blocked apron movement. The Airline Operations Coordinator uses remote stands to protect connection banks only when the resulting passenger transfer time is compatible with the planned departure sequence. Handling Resource Planner produces the Remote Stand Shuttle Count at regular operating intervals to represent available passenger transfer capacity for remote stand activity. Shuttle availability is modeled as a finite resource that includes vehicles, drivers, loading positions, terminal curb space, and accessible boarding support. Passenger bus constraints include maximum load, door cycle time, stand access routing, security segregation, mobility assistance requirements, and the time needed to clear passengers from the aircraft vicinity. The Ground Handling Dispatcher verifies that bus assignments do not conflict with baggage, catering, fueling, or crew transport movements on the same apron corridor. The Ramp Movement Board displays remote stand occupancy, expected towing actions, and bus service readiness in a shared view for airside coordination. The Tower Sequence Manager does not advance an aircraft toward an active departure sequence unless the remote stand release time supports the expected taxi plan. The Departure Metering Service incorporates remote stand constraints when estimating whether a Target Off Block Time remains achievable. If a bus shortage or terminal loading delay is detected, the Airline Operations Coordinator may request revision of the Target Off Block Time before the delay propagates to runway demand. The Regional Flow Manager considers repeated remote stand transfer delays as an input to tactical capacity assessment during peak arrival and departure banks. Ramp Control Lead uses the Towbar Shortage Report to determine whether remote stand aircraft can be repositioned without creating secondary apron congestion. The Capacity Assessment Module reflects persistent remote stand service limits as a reduction in practical gate throughput rather than only as a ramp movement issue. The Gate Conflict Detector identifies cases where an aircraft assigned to a contact gate would cause less overall delay than a nominally available remote stand with inadequate passenger transfer capacity. When remote stand use increases, the Airport CDM Portal records the operational reason, affected flights, expected passenger transfer duration, and responsible control position. The Common Situation Display presents remote stand status in time order so that airline, ramp, tower, and airport roles share the same expectation for aircraft readiness. Landside service limits are linked to airside timing through estimated boarding completion, bus return availability, crew access, and terminal processing constraints. A remote stand departure is not considered ready for push or tow until passengers, bags, crew, and required ground services are aligned with the planned aircraft movement. During Adverse Weather Mode, the Airport Duty Manager may reduce planned remote stand use because bus loading, walking routes, and accessible transfer times become less predictable. During Recovery Phase, remote stands may be used to absorb late arrivals while preserving contact gates for flights with tight turnarounds or high connection sensitivity. The Performance Analytics Warehouse retains remote stand timing measures so that planners can compare scheduled assumptions with actual passenger transfer and apron release performance.

## 24. Border, Customs, and Apron Sensor Inputs

This section extends the remote stand performance concept by describing how border, customs, and apron sensing inputs become constraints in the daily operating plan. Border release status is represented as a readiness attribute on each affected flight record in the Regional Flow Platform, separate from aircraft readiness and airline commercial readiness. The Airport Duty Manager produces the Customs Hold Release Note when border processing confirms that a held arrival may proceed to normal turnaround handling. Customs hold indicators are represented as operational constraints that prevent premature stand release, passenger transfer assumptions, or departure readiness claims. A flight under a customs hold may remain physically parked and technically serviceable while still being unavailable for normal passenger, baggage, or crew movement decisions. When border release status is unknown, the Demand Prediction Service treats the turnaround milestone as uncertain rather than assuming that the planned connection interval is valid. The Airport CDM Portal displays border and customs status in a controlled view so that only authorized operational users see the clearance condition needed for coordination. The Regional Flow Manager uses aggregated border delay exposure to decide whether an arrival regulation should be adjusted or whether delay absorption should remain local to the airport. The Airline Operations Coordinator uses customs hold visibility to adjust connection protection, passenger messaging assumptions, and expected aircraft turnaround timing. The Slot Allocation Engine treats an unresolved customs hold as a reason to avoid assigning an aggressive departure sequence position to the affected flight. The Arrival Regulation Console uses the Medical Diversion Notice to protect arrival priority, stand assignment timing, and downstream border coordination in the active arrival plan. Customs status changes are time stamped before they are used in sequencing so that operational decisions are based on the latest authoritative clearance state. The East Apron Sensor reports stand occupancy transitions, pushback lane blockage durations, and sensor confidence values at a synchronized event time. West Apron Sensor observations are used to verify whether remote stand areas and service roads are actually available for the traffic pattern shown in the plan. The Surface Surveillance Adapter normalizes apron sensing data into movement, occupancy, and blockage events that can be correlated with flight milestones. The Ramp Movement Board presents apron sensor events as operational evidence for stand release, taxi lane availability, and vehicle conflict assessment. The Gate Conflict Detector uses border status and apron occupancy evidence to distinguish a true gate conflict from a temporary clearance delay. The Tower Sequence Manager considers confirmed apron blockage when determining whether a departure can be included in the next practical movement sequence. The Capacity Assessment Module applies persistent apron congestion as a local capacity factor when estimating achievable departure throughput during Normal Operations and Adverse Weather Mode. The Time Synchronization Service is required because customs status events and apron sensor events must align with Calculated Takeoff Time and Target Off Block Time decisions. The Business Rules Registry defines which border and customs states block sequencing, which states require human confirmation, and which states are informational only. The Data Governance Steward maintains the access rules and retention rules for border and customs data because the data can affect both operational outcomes and passenger privacy obligations. The Alert Distribution Hub issues targeted alerts when a customs hold conflicts with an assigned stand plan or when apron sensing contradicts a declared off-block milestone. The Collaborative Decision Log records the operational reason when border release status, customs hold status, or apron sensing data changes a sequencing or stand allocation decision. The Performance Analytics Warehouse compares planned clearance assumptions with actual border release timing and apron availability to improve future demand prediction and resource planning.

## 25. Sector Load and En-Route Constraint Management

The airport-level constraint process starts by translating en-route demand indicators into rates, time windows, and advisories that can be understood by local operations teams. Each Sector Load Monitor supplies time-bucketed counts, predicted occupancy, and overload flags for the sectors that feed or constrain Meridian arrivals and departures. The Regional Flow Platform aligns these sector values with scheduled movements from Flight Data Exchange and predicted readiness from Demand Prediction Service. When a sector is predicted to exceed its planning threshold, Capacity Assessment Module evaluates whether the affected traffic is dominated by departures, arrivals, or mixed flows. For departure-dominated overload, the conversion result is normally an airport departure rate, a constrained departure fix allocation, or a proposed spacing value at the runway release point. For arrival-dominated overload, the result is expressed as an adjusted Airport Acceptance Rate or a metered arrival stream for Arrival Regulation Console. Mixed-flow constraints are converted into paired measures so that departure metering does not protect one sector by creating an unmanaged arrival queue. The Regional Flow Manager uses the load sheet to confirm whether a departure fix constraint should be represented as an airport departure measure. Departure fix saturation is calculated by comparing planned departures by fix against the usable fix capacity declared in Airport Capacity Plan. Capacity Assessment Module applies Business Rules Registry thresholds to distinguish a short tactical peak from sustained saturation requiring a Tactical Regulation Proposal. A short peak normally becomes a sequence advisory in Tower Sequence Manager or a limited departure metering adjustment in Departure Metering Service. Sustained saturation is converted into revised Calculated Takeoff Time targets and, when necessary, adjusted Target Off Block Time values published through Airport CDM Portal. The Slot Allocation Engine preserves equity by distributing delay across operators according to the active Ground Delay Program logic and local priority rules. The Airline Operations Coordinator reviews the airport-level result to determine whether flight swaps, cancellations, or recovery plans are needed inside the airline schedule. The Tower Supervisor uses the same result to understand whether a runway queue is being held for surface capacity, runway capacity, or downstream airspace capacity. The Surface Surveillance Adapter produces the Runway Lighting Outage Card when monitored runway lighting status affects a declared operating configuration. En-route constraints received from network coordination are normalized into constraint records with an affected route, start time, end time, expected capacity, and confidence value. The Airspace Sector Planner validates the operational meaning of each constraint before it is allowed to influence airport rates. The Military Airspace Liaison confirms whether conditional route availability changes the effective capacity before the constraint is converted to a local measure. Weather-related en-route limitations are correlated with information from Weather Ingest Gateway so that airport measures reflect both sector capacity and route availability. In Normal Operations, the conversion may only produce monitoring alerts because sector demand remains below the action threshold. In Adverse Weather Mode or Capacity Reduction State, the same inputs are allowed to produce binding metering values, revised rates, and additional coordination tasks. All converted measures are displayed on Common Situation Display with the source constraint, effective interval, affected runway or fix, and expected delay impact. Alert Distribution Hub sends notifications to Regional Flow Manager, Tower Supervisor, Ramp Control Lead, Slot Coordinator, and Network Operations Liaison when a measure changes operational behavior. Collaborative Decision Log records the constraint, chosen conversion rule, responsible actor, and time of approval for traceability. Performance Analytics Warehouse later compares predicted sector pressure, actual airborne delay, and airport departure compliance to tune Demand Prediction Service and Capacity Assessment Module.

## 26. Crew Legality and Operator Recovery Choices

Crew legality is represented in the Regional Flow Platform as operational constraints rather than as detailed employment records. The Airline Interface Adapter accepts legality indicators that describe whether a proposed departure, arrival, or aircraft swap remains feasible within declared crew duty limits. The model uses time thresholds, qualification flags, and pairing feasibility status, but it does not require names, pay rules, hotel arrangements, or internal crew roster details. Aircraft Operator Representative sends the Crew Duty Extension Log when an approved company process extends duty availability for a specific rotation. The Regional Flow Platform treats the received legality status as an operator assertion and does not independently calculate labor agreement compliance. Aircraft Operator Representative remains responsible for the correctness of crew legality inputs supplied through Airline Interface Adapter or Airport CDM Portal. Airline Operations Coordinator may mark a flight as non-recoverable by delay after a specified time, which prevents automated suggestions that would create an infeasible rotation. The same representation supports aircraft and crew combinations that are valid only if a Target Off Block Time is achieved within a limited tolerance. Slot Allocation Engine uses these constraints when evaluating whether a Calculated Takeoff Time should be offered, retained, exchanged, or released. Departure Metering Service receives only the feasibility result and timing boundary needed for sequencing decisions. Common Situation Display shows crew legality impacts as coded operational states, such as feasible, time critical, infeasible, or operator review required. These states are visible to authorized users without exposing the commercial reasoning behind an operator decision. Operator recovery choices are represented as ranked options that may include delay acceptance, aircraft substitution, flight cancellation, flight consolidation, repositioning, or slot exchange. The ranking is provided by Aircraft Operator Representative or Airline Operations Coordinator and is stored as a preference profile for the affected flight or rotation. Business Rules Registry defines which preference fields are mandatory, optional, or hidden from other participants. For example, an operator may disclose that a flight can accept a later Calculated Takeoff Time but withhold the passenger revenue, maintenance cost, or network protection reason. Slot Coordinator can evaluate the operational acceptability of a requested change without receiving unnecessary commercial information. Regional Flow Manager can see aggregate recovery pressure across operators, but individual preference details remain limited by role and purpose. Identity Access Service enforces these visibility rules for Airport CDM Portal, Digital Briefing Room, and application programming interfaces. Audit Event Ledger records who entered or changed a recovery choice, the effective time, the affected flight reference, and the reason category selected by the operator. Collaborative Decision Log records any agreed operational outcome, including whether the decision was constrained by crew legality or by an operator preference. Flight Data Exchange distributes only the approved operational result to downstream services that need to update schedules, metering, or airport resource plans. Performance Analytics Warehouse may analyze anonymized patterns of legality constraints and recovery selections to improve disruption playbooks. The design allows coordinated recovery during Normal Operations, Adverse Weather Mode, Capacity Reduction State, and Recovery Phase while preserving the operator's commercially sensitive decision logic.

## 27. Apron Cleanliness, Safety, and Readiness Checks

Apron cleanliness and stand readiness checks are treated as operational prerequisites before any surface movement release is accepted into the departure sequence. The Ramp Control Lead defines the sweep areas by stand, taxilane, service road interface, and equipment staging zone so that each check has a clear spatial boundary. Ground Handling Dispatcher assigns qualified personnel to perform apron sweeps during planned turnaround milestones and after any event that may introduce foreign object debris. Foreign object debris checks cover paved stand surfaces, jet bridge wheel paths, towbar storage locations, belt loader positions, fueling lanes, and adjacent drainage grates. East Apron Sensor and West Apron Sensor provide supporting surveillance cues, but visual confirmation remains required where sensor coverage is obstructed by parked aircraft or ground equipment. The Ramp Movement Board displays stand readiness status using time-stamped states that distinguish unchecked, under inspection, cleaned, blocked, and ready conditions. Ramp Control Lead sends the Apron Sweep Completion Note after the responsible sweep team confirms that the assigned apron area is clean, safe, and free of observed foreign object debris. Airport CDM Portal uses the shuttle count to confirm that remote-stand passenger transfer demand is consistent with stand readiness timing. The Airport CDM Portal records each stand readiness confirmation with the stand identifier, responsible organization, inspection time, readiness constraint, and confirming user identity. Identity Access Service restricts readiness confirmation privileges to approved ramp, handling, and airport operations roles to prevent unverified operational releases. Time Synchronization Service provides the common time reference for sweep records, readiness confirmations, and safety-related release decisions. When a sweep identifies debris, leaking fluid, damaged pavement, unsecured equipment, wildlife activity, or lighting damage, the stand remains unavailable until the hazard is removed or mitigated. Ground Handling Dispatcher updates the Handling Resource Planner when personnel, equipment, or cleaning vehicles are reassigned to restore a contaminated stand. The Ramp Control Lead reviews unresolved hazards against active turnaround demand before allowing pushback planning to continue for the affected stand. Tower Supervisor does not use a stand readiness indication as clearance for aircraft movement, but the indication supports coordinated surface planning and prevents premature sequencing. Departure Metering Service suppresses release recommendations for flights whose assigned stand is not marked ready or whose access route is blocked by an unresolved apron condition. Gate Conflict Detector checks readiness status against aircraft allocation, towing activity, and adjacent stand occupancy to identify conflicts before ground movement begins. Airline Operations Coordinator may propose a revised Target Off Block Time when apron cleaning or stand restoration is expected to delay the planned turnaround. Slot Allocation Engine retains the Calculated Takeoff Time until the operational delay exceeds the tolerance defined in the Business Rules Registry. Airport Duty Manager can declare a localized Capacity Reduction State when repeated apron contamination or stand closures materially reduce usable gate capacity. During Adverse Weather Mode, sweep intervals may be shortened because wind, precipitation, or deicing residue can increase the probability of foreign object debris and surface contamination. Safety-related releases are timed so that the latest sweep confirmation remains valid through passenger boarding completion, equipment removal, door closure, and pushback coordination. If the readiness validity window expires before movement begins, the stand returns to an inspection-required state and a new check is performed before release. Audit Event Ledger stores readiness changes, user acknowledgements, hazard closures, and release decisions to support post-operation review and accountability. Performance Analytics Warehouse uses aggregated readiness and contamination timing data to identify recurring debris locations, inefficient sweep patterns, and opportunities for preventive maintenance.

## 28. Slot Swaps and Collaborative Substitution

The controlled slot swap process begins when an Aircraft Operator Representative identifies a flight that can exchange its assigned demand position without reducing declared capacity or creating an unfair priority change. The Airline Operations Coordinator enters the proposed substitution in the Airport CDM Portal with the flight identifiers, operational reason, readiness basis, and requested effective time. The Airport CDM Portal validates the submitting user through the Identity Access Service and blocks any proposal from a party without delegated operator authority. The Slot Allocation Engine evaluates the candidate swap against active Ground Delay Program constraints, Calculated Takeoff Time integrity, Target Off Block Time dependencies, and Airport Acceptance Rate commitments. The Business Rules Registry provides the governing parameters for precedence, equity, customs eligibility, minimum notification time, and prohibited trades. The Slot Coordinator reviews any exception raised by the Slot Allocation Engine and confirms whether the swap remains consistent with regional flow objectives. Slot Coordinator sends the Slot Swap Permission Email only after the governance checks have been completed and recorded. For airline-to-airline coordination, each Aircraft Operator Representative records concurrence in the Airport CDM Portal rather than relying on a verbal agreement. The Network Operations Liaison monitors aggregate displacement from approved substitutions to ensure that one carrier's recovery action does not destabilize the wider network plan. The Regional Flow Manager may suspend collaborative substitution during a Capacity Reduction State when scarce capacity must be protected for the published tactical plan. The Tower Supervisor retains authority to reject a swap if the resulting sequence would conflict with safe runway sequencing or local control workload. The Ramp Control Lead confirms that the revised sequence remains compatible with stand occupancy, pushback spacing, and tug availability shown on the Ramp Movement Board. Airport Duty Manager uses the Apron Sweep Completion Note to confirm that apron availability constraints do not invalidate an approved substitution. Airline Operations Coordinator uses the release note to confirm that a candidate substitution is eligible for customs release before requesting a slot swap. Customs release status is treated as a prerequisite condition for any substitution that would move an international departure into an earlier operating position. If customs status is incomplete, the Airport CDM Portal keeps the proposal pending and prevents the Slot Allocation Engine from publishing the revised slot assignment. The Collaborative Decision Log captures the proposed flights, the reason code, the user confirmations, the governance outcome, and the final decision time. The Audit Event Ledger records each approval, rejection, cancellation, and override so that later review can distinguish operational judgment from system enforcement. The Flight Data Exchange publishes revised Flight Intent Message content only after the approved substitution is final and time synchronized. The Common Situation Display presents the updated Calculated Takeoff Time and Target Off Block Time so that operational users share the same view of the accepted change. The Departure Metering Service recalculates metering advisories after approval and prevents the swap from creating a new demand peak at a departure fix. Disputes between operators are escalated to the Regional Flow Manager when the recorded concurrences conflict or when a party alleges that governance rules were bypassed. The System Support Engineer investigates technical failures in submission, validation, or publication without altering the operational decision unless authorized through the defined control process. The Post Operations Report includes swap volume, rejection reasons, approval latency, compliance with revised times, and any equity concerns identified during the operating period.

## 29. Lighting, Navaid, and Aerodrome Infrastructure Events

Aerodrome infrastructure events are treated as capacity inputs when they affect runway availability, taxi route usability, stand access, lighting performance, or supporting navigation services. The Regional Flow Platform records each event as an operational constraint with an effective time, expected duration, affected resource, confidence level, and approving authority. The Airport Duty Manager is responsible for ensuring that declared service reductions are entered promptly and that the operational description is consistent with local aerodrome control procedures. The Tower Supervisor uses the Runway Lighting Outage Card to confirm which runway lighting elements are unavailable before approving the operational status shown in the Tower Sequence Manager. When lighting performance limits night or low-visibility operations, the Capacity Assessment Module converts the limit into a runway configuration constraint rather than treating it as a general advisory. The Airport CDM Portal presents infrastructure events to authorized airport, airline, and flow management users as part of the shared operating picture. The Regional Flow Manager reviews the published constraint when it changes the usable runway rate, taxi-out predictability, or the feasibility of the active demand plan. The Capacity Assessment Module calculates the operational effect by comparing the affected resource with the current Airport Capacity Plan and the active weather state. If the event reduces arrival throughput, the Arrival Regulation Console applies the revised Airport Acceptance Rate after confirmation by the Regional Flow Manager. If the event reduces departure throughput, the Departure Metering Service updates metering rates and provides revised inputs to the Slot Allocation Engine. The Slot Allocation Engine uses the reduced capacity value to protect fairness while avoiding schedules that exceed the declared infrastructure capability. The Demand Prediction Service recalculates short-term demand against the constrained capacity so that delay and queue forecasts reflect the published limitation. The Tower Sequence Manager displays the accepted constraint to support runway sequencing decisions and to reduce conflicting tactical instructions. The Ramp Movement Board receives the same operational status so that apron movements can be coordinated with restricted taxi routes or stand access limitations. The Common Situation Display shows the affected resource, start time, review time, and current decision status using language that can be understood by operational staff. The Alert Distribution Hub sends notifications when the constraint is created, materially changed, suspended, or cancelled. The Business Rules Registry defines which infrastructure events require approval, which events may be published as advisory information, and which events automatically trigger a Capacity Reduction State. During Adverse Weather Mode, lighting and navigation service limits are assessed together with ceiling, visibility, wind, and runway surface conditions before capacity values are released. During a Capacity Reduction State, the Regional Flow Platform maintains the infrastructure event as a controlling assumption until an authorized actor closes or supersedes it. The Collaborative Decision Log records the time of publication, approving role, capacity effect, affected services, and any later amendment made during the operating period. The Audit Event Ledger preserves the technical record of submission, validation, publication, acknowledgement, and withdrawal for accountability and post-event review. The Data Governance Steward monitors data quality for infrastructure events, including duplicate reports, inconsistent effective times, and unclear service descriptions. The Performance Analytics Warehouse uses completed infrastructure events to measure delay attribution, capacity recovery time, and the accuracy of initial duration estimates. When normal capability is restored, the Airport Duty Manager confirms closure in the Airport CDM Portal and the Regional Flow Platform returns the affected resource to Normal Operations or Recovery Phase handling as appropriate.

## 30. Contingency Operations and Fallback Modes

Contingency operations cover any period in which the Regional Flow Platform cannot provide trusted automation for one or more airport, runway, apron, or airspace flow functions. A degraded operation is declared when data latency, service unavailability, or inconsistent operational outputs prevent users from relying on the normal decision support path without additional verification. The Airport Duty Manager is accountable for declaring the Contingency Fallback State after consultation with the Regional Flow Manager, Tower Supervisor, and System Support Engineer. The declaration identifies the affected functions, the operational area, the expected duration, and the authority that will approve restoration to Normal Operations or Recovery Phase. For voice coordination, the Contingency Operations Console is referred to as Silverpine during exercises and live fallback operation. The Contingency Operations Console provides a protected operating position with independent access to the Flight Data Exchange, Alert Distribution Hub, Audit Event Ledger, and Digital Briefing Room. If the Airport CDM Portal becomes unavailable, participants continue coordination through the Digital Briefing Room and record decisions in the Collaborative Decision Log when service is restored. If the Demand Prediction Service or Capacity Assessment Module is unreliable, the Regional Flow Manager applies the last verified Airport Capacity Plan and adjusts it by direct coordination with the Airspace Sector Planner and Meteorology Briefing Officer. If the Slot Allocation Engine is unavailable, the Slot Coordinator initiates manual slot procedures using a time-ordered control sheet maintained at the Contingency Operations Console. Manual slot procedures preserve the most recent valid Calculated Takeoff Time unless safety, capacity, curfew, or regulatory constraints require a new time. The Slot Coordinator assigns any revised Calculated Takeoff Time only after confirming runway capacity with the Tower Supervisor and surface feasibility with the Ramp Control Lead. The Airline Operations Coordinator and Aircraft Operator Representative acknowledge manual slot assignments through recorded voice readback or an agreed written channel. The Slot Coordinator uses the Crew Duty Extension Log to check whether manually issued slot times remain operationally achievable. During manual operation, the Tower Sequence Manager and Ramp Movement Board are treated as advisory displays unless their data quality has been confirmed by the System Support Engineer. The Departure Metering Service may remain in monitoring mode while manual slot control is active, but it must not automatically overwrite issued times. The Arrival Regulation Console may continue to display arrival demand, but the Regional Flow Manager approves any arrival regulation changes through direct coordination. Fallback entry criteria include loss of authentication through the Identity Access Service, loss of time alignment from the Time Synchronization Service, corrupted operational data, or a sustained mismatch between surveillance and flight data. Fallback entry also occurs when the Alert Distribution Hub cannot deliver mandatory operational advisories to the required participants within the agreed operational tolerance. The System Support Engineer classifies the technical condition, isolates failed components, and advises whether partial restoration is safe for operational use. The Data Governance Steward confirms which data sets may be trusted and marks any later synchronization from fallback records for reconciliation review. The Airport Duty Manager conducts fallback briefings at intervals appropriate to the traffic level and publishes the current operating basis through the Digital Briefing Room. Transition out of the Contingency Fallback State requires stable service performance, verified data consistency, successful user access, and agreement from the operational leads affected by the outage. Before returning automation to control, the Slot Coordinator reconciles the manual slot record with the Slot Allocation Engine and resolves any conflict in the Collaborative Decision Log. After restoration, the Performance Analytics Warehouse receives the outage timeline, manual intervention record, and recovery milestones for inclusion in the Post Operations Report.

## 31. Passenger Disruption Indicators and Service Recovery

The system treats passenger disruption information as a secondary planning signal that can refine, but not replace, capacity and safety constraints in traffic flow decisions. Its purpose in this area is to help the Regional Flow Manager understand where a small metering adjustment may reduce downstream disruption without degrading the Airport Capacity Plan. Airline Operations Coordinator sends the Passenger Misconnect Count. The Airline Interface Adapter validates the timeliness, flight association, and airline ownership of connection-risk data before it is made available for operational use. The Data Governance Steward defines the acceptable aggregation level so that the Regional Flow Platform does not expose passenger names, booking references, or protected itinerary details. The Demand Prediction Service combines connection-risk indicators with aircraft readiness, scheduled bank structure, and forecast arrival variability to estimate the sensitivity of selected departures. These estimates are advisory and do not create an entitlement for a flight to receive a favorable Calculated Takeoff Time. The Slot Allocation Engine continues to apply the approved sequencing policy, runway capacity, airspace constraints, and published Ground Delay Program parameters as controlling inputs. When several flights have similar operational priority, connection-risk information may be used as a tie breaker if the Business Rules Registry permits that use for the active operating state. The Departure Metering Service can therefore recommend a minor hold, release, or resequencing action only when the change remains inside the available runway and surface movement envelope. The Tower Supervisor retains authority over tactical runway sequencing and may reject any advisory that would impair safe separation, wake spacing, or local controller workload. The Ramp Control Lead uses the Ramp Movement Board to determine whether a proposed metering adjustment can be supported by stand availability, pushback feasibility, and taxi route status. The Airport Duty Manager uses the Airport CDM Portal to see whether airline recovery actions are concentrated in one bank or distributed across the operating day. Typical airline recovery actions include flight retiming, aircraft substitution, passenger protection on later services, and selective holding of departures already inside normal operational tolerance. The system records these actions as operational context rather than as passenger service tasks assigned to the airport flow management process. The Regional Flow Manager may consider a recovery action credible only when it is aligned with a valid Target Off Block Time, crew availability status, and confirmed aircraft readiness. If an airline indicates a recovery plan that depends on unrealistic turnaround timing, the Demand Prediction Service discounts the associated connection benefit. During Adverse Weather Mode, passenger disruption indicators are normally given less influence because the Capacity Assessment Module and Sector Load Monitor have reduced flexibility to absorb discretionary changes. During Recovery Phase, the same indicators can have greater practical value because metering margins may exist after the primary capacity constraint has begun to clear. The Arrival Regulation Console may show inbound connection pressure as context for arrival rate discussions, but it does not convert passenger handling needs into a separate arrival priority scheme. The Common Situation Display presents passenger disruption cues with clear labeling so that participants understand they are decision-support indicators, not commands. Every accepted use of connection-risk information in a metering decision is recorded in the Collaborative Decision Log with the responsible actor, rationale, and affected flight interval. The Performance Analytics Warehouse compares later delay outcomes with the logged rationale to determine whether the advisory use of passenger disruption data improved recovery performance. This approach keeps the primary system function focused on safe, efficient, and transparent traffic flow management while still allowing airline recovery information to inform proportionate metering choices.

## 32. Cybersecurity, Identity, and Access Control

Cybersecurity for the Regional Flow Platform is based on centrally managed identities, explicit authorization, and traceable operational actions. The Identity Access Service is the authoritative source for user accounts, service accounts, role memberships, authentication policies, and session lifecycle controls. Each named operational user shall authenticate with an individually assigned identity before accessing the Airport CDM Portal, Digital Briefing Room, or any operational console. Shared operational accounts are not permitted for routine use because they prevent reliable attribution during time-critical flow management decisions. The Regional Flow Manager, Tower Supervisor, Ramp Control Lead, Slot Coordinator, and Airline Operations Coordinator receive role profiles that reflect their normal operational responsibilities. Role profiles shall grant the minimum access needed to view, propose, approve, or administer operational data within the Regional Flow Platform. The Regional Flow Manager may authorize regional traffic management measures, while the Slot Coordinator may administer slot allocation functions within boundaries defined by the Business Rules Registry. The Tower Supervisor may operate the Tower Sequence Manager and view runway metering information, but may not modify airline recovery priorities or user entitlement records. The Ramp Control Lead may update ramp movement status through the Ramp Movement Board, but may not change airport acceptance parameters or sector capacity assumptions. The Airline Operations Coordinator and Aircraft Operator Representative may view airline-relevant planning data and submit coordination inputs without receiving administrative access to system configuration. The System Support Engineer may maintain infrastructure components and investigate technical faults, but operational approval privileges shall remain separated from technical administration privileges. The Data Governance Steward may manage data retention rules, reference data ownership, and audit review processes without being able to issue operational traffic flow instructions. Operational segregation shall prevent a single user from creating, approving, and technically concealing a material change to a Ground Delay Program, Airport Acceptance Rate, or Tactical Regulation Proposal. Where dual control is required, the Regional Flow Platform shall record the initiating user, the approving user, the affected operational object, and the effective time of the decision. Access to the Performance Analytics Warehouse shall be restricted to authorized analytical roles and shall not allow retrospective alteration of operational records held in the Audit Event Ledger. The Audit Event Ledger shall capture successful and failed authentication attempts, role changes, privilege elevations, operational approvals, configuration changes, data exports, and emergency access activations. All audit records shall use time derived from the Time Synchronization Service so that investigations can reconstruct event order across the Airport CDM Portal, Slot Allocation Engine, and Alert Distribution Hub. Audit records shall be protected against ordinary user modification and retained for a period agreed by the airport operator, regional authority, and participating aircraft operators. Security monitoring shall alert the System Support Engineer and Data Governance Steward when unusual access patterns indicate possible credential compromise or inappropriate privilege use. The Identity Access Service shall support rapid suspension of accounts when a user changes role, leaves an organization, or becomes subject to an operational integrity concern. Temporary access shall have a defined sponsor, purpose, role scope, and expiry time, and shall be reviewed after the operational need has ended. Silverpine shall accept emergency access activations only after the Identity Access Service records the authorizing incident reference and expiry time. Emergency access may be granted to the Emergency Response Coordinator, Airport Duty Manager, or System Support Engineer when life safety, airport continuity, or critical system recovery requires immediate action. Use of emergency access shall generate prominent audit events and shall require post-event review by the Data Governance Steward and the responsible operational authority. During Contingency Fallback State, access control shall continue to enforce role separation even when some integrations are unavailable and manual coordination becomes necessary. When normal connectivity is restored, the Regional Flow Platform shall reconcile contingency actions with the Collaborative Decision Log so that accountability is preserved across the full incident timeline.

## 33. Audit, Compliance, and Slot Performance Review

After connectivity is restored, audit processing shall treat reconciled contingency actions as part of the same operational record as actions taken during Normal Operations. The Regional Flow Platform shall record slot compliance events in the Audit Event Ledger using the operating date, flight identifier, airport, runway direction, and applicable regulation reference. Each compliance assessment shall compare the Calculated Takeoff Time, the Target Off Block Time, actual off-block time, and actual takeoff time against the tolerance defined in the Business Rules Registry. The Slot Compliance State shall indicate whether a flight was compliant, early, late, exempted, substituted, cancelled, or pending review at the end of the traffic period. The Audit Event Ledger shall preserve the time source applied by the Time Synchronization Service so that later review can distinguish operational delay from data latency. User decisions entered through the Airport CDM Portal shall be linked to the authenticated role provided by the Identity Access Service. The Collaborative Decision Log shall retain the stated reason, decision time, affected flight, and visible operational context for each material change to a slot or sequence. The Airline Operations Coordinator uses the permission email to justify an approved slot substitution during post-operations review. Substitution records shall show the original flight, replacement flight, assigned Calculated Takeoff Time, acceptance status, and any subsequent cancellation or reversion. When an Aircraft Operator Representative proposes a substitution, the audit trail shall capture whether the change preserved the constraints of the active Ground Delay Program. The Slot Coordinator shall review substitution activity after operations to confirm that priority rules were applied consistently across participating operators. Late operational changes shall be distinguished from planned updates by comparing the change timestamp with the freeze horizon held in the Business Rules Registry. Changes made inside the freeze horizon shall require a reason code and shall be visible to the Regional Flow Manager during post-operations review. The Tower Sequence Manager and Ramp Movement Board shall provide actual movement milestones that support independent verification of slot performance. Surface movement evidence from the Surface Surveillance Adapter shall be retained long enough to support dispute resolution and regulatory audit requests. Capacity changes made through the Capacity Assessment Module shall be correlated with the active Airport Capacity Plan and Airport Acceptance Rate used at the time of allocation. The Performance Analytics Warehouse shall aggregate compliance outcomes by carrier, route group, regulation, airport operating configuration, and time band. Aggregated metrics shall support review of systemic lateness without exposing commercially sensitive detail beyond authorized roles. The Data Governance Steward shall verify that audit fields required for slot performance reporting remain complete, immutable, and traceable to approved data definitions. The System Support Engineer shall investigate technical gaps when audit sequences show missing events, duplicate updates, or inconsistent timestamps. The Regional Flow Manager shall use the Post Operations Report to identify whether noncompliance resulted from operator choice, airport constraint, airspace constraint, weather impact, or system issue. The Airport Duty Manager shall review late airport-driven changes that affected multiple flights and shall record whether the operational justification was valid. Findings from the review shall feed adjustments to tolerance values, reason code definitions, and training material, subject to change control. All audit outputs shall be retained according to the approved records schedule and shall remain searchable for compliance investigations, performance meetings, and lessons-learned activity.

## 34. Data Governance and Information Quality

Data governance for the Regional Flow Platform shall assign accountable owners to each operational data domain and shall make those assignments visible in the Airport CDM Portal. The Data Governance Steward shall maintain the data ownership register and shall coordinate updates through the Business Rules Registry. The Regional Flow Manager shall own flow management policy data, including Ground Delay Program parameters and Tactical Regulation Proposal status values. The Slot Coordinator shall own slot allocation reference data, including Calculated Takeoff Time tolerances and Slot Compliance State definitions. The Airport Duty Manager shall own airport capacity declarations, including Airport Capacity Plan records and Airport Acceptance Rate values. The Tower Supervisor shall own tower sequence status data maintained for the Tower Sequence Manager. The Ramp Control Lead shall own ramp movement status data maintained for the Ramp Movement Board. The Meteorology Briefing Officer shall own operational weather classification inputs accepted by the Weather Ingest Gateway. The Airline Operations Coordinator shall be accountable for airline-supplied schedule intent, readiness status, and Target Off Block Time updates submitted through approved interfaces. Each owned data element shall have a defined business meaning, allowed value range, update authority, retention class, and escalation contact. Freshness indicators shall be displayed on the Common Situation Display wherever stale data could affect sequencing, metering, regulation, or resource allocation decisions. The freshness indicator shall include the time of last accepted update, the maximum permitted age, and a visual state that distinguishes current, aging, stale, and unavailable data. The Time Synchronization Service shall provide the common time basis used for freshness calculations across the Regional Flow Platform. The Demand Prediction Service, Capacity Assessment Module, Slot Allocation Engine, and Departure Metering Service shall reject data that lacks a trusted timestamp. Validation rules shall include schema checks, mandatory field checks, code set checks, temporal sequence checks, and operational plausibility checks. The Flight Data Exchange shall validate Flight Intent Message content before the data is made available for operational use. The Surface Surveillance Adapter shall apply position quality checks and shall mark uncertain observations before they are used by surface decision functions. The Airline Interface Adapter shall apply carrier-specific format checks while preserving the common operational meaning required by the Regional Flow Platform. The Handling Resource Planner shall validate resource quantities, task timing, and allocation identifiers against active operational reference data. The Business Rules Registry shall hold the current validation thresholds, allowable reason codes, and exception rules approved for Normal Operations, Adverse Weather Mode, Capacity Reduction State, Recovery Phase, and Contingency Fallback State. When a discrepancy is detected, the affected component shall mark the data as disputed and shall preserve the prior accepted value until an authorized correction is available. The Data Governance Steward shall coordinate discrepancy resolution with the responsible operational owner and shall record the disposition in the Audit Event Ledger. Urgent discrepancies that could change a metering, arrival regulation, or slot allocation decision shall be raised through the Alert Distribution Hub and reviewed in the Digital Briefing Room. The System Support Engineer shall investigate technical causes of missing, delayed, duplicated, or transformed records and shall provide evidence needed for stewardship decisions. The Performance Analytics Warehouse shall retain data quality measures, including validation failure rates, freshness breaches, manual corrections, and recurring ownership conflicts. Stewardship reviews shall use those measures to update training, adjust validation rules, and improve the reliability of information used in operational decision making.

## 35. Performance Measurement and Regional Benefits

Performance measurement for the Meridian Regional Air Traffic Flow Management and Airport Collaborative Decision-Making System shall convert operational events into repeatable indicators that can be reviewed after each operating day. The Performance Analytics Warehouse shall calculate delay absorption as the portion of expected airborne or terminal-area delay that is converted into controlled ground delay before departure. Delay absorption shall be measured by comparing the unconstrained demand predicted by the Demand Prediction Service with the regulated demand produced by the Slot Allocation Engine and the realized movement times captured through the Flight Data Exchange. The principal delay absorption indicator shall report minutes absorbed at origin, minutes remaining in the arrival stream, and minutes transferred to surface queues within the region. The Regional Flow Manager shall use this indicator to verify that a Ground Delay Program reduced airborne holding without creating excessive departure congestion at constrained airports. Queue length performance shall be measured for runway departure queues, arrival metering queues, apron movement queues, and regulation waiting lists. The Tower Sequence Manager shall provide runway sequence counts, while the Ramp Movement Board shall provide surface queue counts based on movement status and surveillance confirmation. The North Runway Meter, South Runway Meter, East Apron Sensor, and West Apron Sensor shall support independent checks of queue length where surveillance coverage is available. Queue metrics shall include maximum queue length, average queue length, time above threshold, and recovery time after a constraint is lifted. Slot adherence shall be measured as the difference between actual movement times and the assigned Calculated Takeoff Time or Target Off Block Time. The Slot Coordinator shall review slot adherence by operator, airport, time band, and regulation identifier to detect recurring causes of noncompliance. A flight shall be counted within tolerance when the actual takeoff or off-block event occurs inside the operating window defined in the Business Rules Registry. The Slot Compliance State shall be used to identify periods when adherence monitoring has operational priority over advisory optimization. The Departure Metering Service uses the Passenger Misconnect Count to weight departure sequencing decisions when connection protection is an approved local objective. Cancellation avoidance shall be estimated by comparing actual cancellations with a baseline scenario derived from demand, capacity, weather, and historical disruption outcomes. The Capacity Assessment Module shall record the available Airport Acceptance Rate used in each baseline so that avoided cancellation estimates remain traceable. The Airline Operations Coordinator and Aircraft Operator Representative shall be able to challenge cancellation avoidance results through the Collaborative Decision Log when local business decisions affected the outcome. Emissions reduction shall be estimated from reduced airborne holding, reduced taxi-out time, avoided engine running in queues, and smoother departure metering. The Performance Analytics Warehouse shall apply approved fuel burn and emissions factors by aircraft category, operating phase, and delay location. Emissions metrics shall distinguish between actual reductions and delay transfers that only move fuel burn from one phase of flight to another. The Meteorology Briefing Officer shall support interpretation of emissions results by identifying periods when weather routing or convective avoidance dominated fuel performance. Stakeholder benefit tracking shall allocate measured outcomes to aircraft operators, airport operations, air navigation service functions, ground handling activities, and passengers where the source data supports that attribution. The Airport Duty Manager shall receive benefit summaries that show changes in stand occupancy, surface congestion, recovery duration, and passenger-facing disruption indicators. The Data Governance Steward shall ensure that published performance measures identify data sources, calculation versions, confidence limits, and known exclusions. The Post Operations Report shall present regional benefits alongside operational trade-offs so that decision makers can see whether delay, queue, slot, cancellation, and emissions objectives were balanced in practice.

## 36. Training, Exercises, and Human Factors

Training for the Meridian Regional Air Traffic Flow Management and Airport Collaborative Decision-Making System shall be role based and shall cover normal coordination, constrained operations, recovery actions, and degraded system use. The Regional Flow Manager shall receive advanced instruction on interpreting demand and capacity information, leading collaborative decisions, and authorizing operational measures in the Regional Flow Platform. Air traffic controllers shall be trained to understand how flow measures, sequencing changes, and calculated times affect sector workload, runway demand, and controller tactical flexibility. The Tower Supervisor shall receive practical training on the Tower Sequence Manager, Departure Metering Service, Arrival Regulation Console, North Runway Meter, and South Runway Meter. Ramp personnel shall be trained under the direction of the Ramp Control Lead to use the Ramp Movement Board without substituting system indications for required visual checks, radio coordination, and local safety procedures. The Airline Operations Coordinator and Aircraft Operator Representative shall be trained to submit, review, and update Flight Intent Message data through approved interfaces and to assess the operational effect of Calculated Takeoff Time and Target Off Block Time changes. Ground handling personnel shall be trained by the Ground Handling Dispatcher on the Handling Resource Planner and on procedures for confirming that resource constraints are represented accurately before they influence collaborative decisions. The Slot Coordinator shall receive training on the Slot Allocation Engine, Airport CDM Portal, and applicable Business Rules Registry entries so that slot actions are applied consistently and transparently. The Network Operations Liaison and Airspace Sector Planner shall be trained to coordinate regional constraints, sector load conditions, and network measures using the Sector Load Monitor and Common Situation Display. The Airport Duty Manager shall be trained to recognize when an Airport Capacity Plan requires revision and when the operational state should move from Normal Operations to Adverse Weather Mode or Capacity Reduction State. The Meteorology Briefing Officer shall be trained to explain weather uncertainty in operational terms and to verify that the Weather Ingest Gateway supports the assumptions used during briefings. The Emergency Response Coordinator shall participate in recurrent training so that emergency priorities are integrated into flow management decisions without obscuring command authority or safety responsibilities. The Deicing Coordinator shall receive seasonal refresher training on how deicing capacity assumptions are represented in demand and departure metering tools. The Data Governance Steward shall train operational users on data quality responsibilities, privacy expectations, and the correct interpretation of audit records in the Audit Event Ledger. The System Support Engineer shall train superusers on service status checks, access fault escalation, and safe use of the Contingency Operations Console during partial outages. Initial training shall include classroom instruction, supervised workstation practice, scenario walkthroughs in the Digital Briefing Room, and observed operational shadowing before independent system use is authorized. Recurrent training shall be required at least annually and after major changes to the Regional Flow Platform, Demand Prediction Service, Capacity Assessment Module, or Flight Data Exchange. Simulation exercises shall use realistic traffic samples, weather constraints, surface congestion, airline schedule pressure, handling resource shortages, and recovery objectives to test coordinated decision making. Each exercise shall include participants from tower, ramp, airline operations, handling, airport operations, network coordination, meteorology, data governance, and system support functions when those functions are relevant to the scenario. Exercises shall cover transitions among Normal Operations, Adverse Weather Mode, Capacity Reduction State, Recovery Phase, Contingency Fallback State, and Slot Compliance State. Simulation events shall require users to compare automated recommendations with local knowledge, document significant decisions in the Collaborative Decision Log, and explain departures from system proposals. Human factors safeguards shall include workload monitoring, clear alert prioritization, consistent terminology, confirmation steps for high impact actions, and protection against silent mode changes. Workstation layouts shall support rapid comparison between the Common Situation Display, Airport CDM Portal, Tower Sequence Manager, Ramp Movement Board, and relevant advisory messages without forcing users to copy critical values manually. Training assessments shall verify that users can identify stale data, time synchronization problems, duplicate flights, conflicting constraints, and inappropriate automation reliance before such issues affect live operations. Post-exercise reviews shall use evidence from the Performance Analytics Warehouse and Audit Event Ledger to improve procedures, refine training materials, and confirm that human performance concerns are assigned to accountable owners.

## 37. Implementation Phasing and Airport Onboarding

Deployment of the Regional Flow Platform shall proceed through phased increments that limit operational exposure while progressively adding airports, interfaces, and decision support functions. The first increment shall establish the core operational baseline for the Regional Flow Manager, Network Operations Liaison, Data Governance Steward, and System Support Engineer in a controlled production environment. This baseline shall include the Identity Access Service, Time Synchronization Service, Audit Event Ledger, Performance Analytics Warehouse, and Business Rules Registry before any airport is permitted to exchange operational data. Pilot airport selection shall favor locations with stable operational leadership, representative traffic complexity, available surface surveillance data, and demonstrated willingness to participate in joint procedure development. The initial pilot shall include one constrained primary airport and one less constrained regional airport so that both high-demand and routine operating patterns are exercised. Each candidate airport shall provide evidence that the Airport Duty Manager, Tower Supervisor, Ramp Control Lead, Airline Operations Coordinator, Ground Handling Dispatcher, and Aircraft Operator Representative have named accountable implementation contacts. Readiness Gate 1 shall confirm that governance, security approvals, user role definitions, operating hours, and support escalation paths are documented and accepted by local stakeholders. Readiness Gate 2 shall confirm that the Flight Data Exchange, Airline Interface Adapter, Surface Surveillance Adapter, and Weather Ingest Gateway can be connected without disrupting existing airport operations. Readiness Gate 3 shall confirm that local procedures have been mapped to the Airport CDM Portal, Tower Sequence Manager, Ramp Movement Board, Departure Metering Service, and Arrival Regulation Console. Readiness Gate 4 shall confirm that training, shadow operations, contingency procedures, and operational acceptance criteria have been completed before live collaborative decisions are made through the Regional Flow Platform. Interface certification shall be performed separately from operational acceptance so that technical conformance defects do not become unresolved procedural issues. Each interface shall be tested for message structure, time stamping, identity attribution, replay handling, duplicate detection, outage recovery, and rejection behavior under degraded network conditions. The Demand Prediction Service, Capacity Assessment Module, Slot Allocation Engine, Gate Conflict Detector, Sector Load Monitor, North Runway Meter, and South Runway Meter shall be certified against agreed reference scenarios before they are used for operational recommendations. Certification results shall be recorded in the Collaborative Decision Log when they affect operational procedures and in the Audit Event Ledger when they affect access, configuration, or transaction traceability. Airport onboarding shall start with data discovery workshops that identify local tools, manual workarounds, unofficial spreadsheets, and locally maintained constraint registers. The migration plan shall classify each local tool as retired, retained for read-only reference, integrated through an approved interface, or replaced by a Regional Flow Platform function. No local tool shall be retired until the responsible operational owner verifies that its essential decisions, timing assumptions, and audit needs are supported in the target process. During shadow operations, local tools shall remain the authoritative source while the Regional Flow Platform produces parallel outputs for comparison by operational users. The cutover decision shall be based on measured agreement between local practice and Regional Flow Platform outputs, user confidence, defect closure, and demonstrated recovery from simulated failures. After cutover, local users shall enter operational decisions through the Airport CDM Portal and shall avoid reintroducing uncontrolled parallel records that undermine common situational awareness. The Contingency Operations Console shall be available at each onboarded airport before reliance on the Regional Flow Platform is declared for Normal Operations, Adverse Weather Mode, Capacity Reduction State, Recovery Phase, or Contingency Fallback State. Expansion beyond the pilot airports shall occur in waves that group airports by interface maturity, traffic dependency, stakeholder readiness, and available support capacity. Each onboarding wave shall include a stabilization period in which the System Support Engineer and Data Governance Steward monitor defect trends, data quality, access changes, and user adoption. Full regional deployment shall be declared only after the Regional Flow Manager confirms that operational benefits, safety constraints, cyber controls, and stakeholder obligations remain stable across multiple airports and traffic conditions.

## 38. Operational Service Management

Operational service management for the Regional Flow Platform shall begin when a deployed airport is accepted into steady state support after the regional stabilization period. The service model shall distinguish continuous operational support from planned engineering support so that safety related traffic flow functions remain protected at all times. The System Support Engineer shall provide round the clock on call coverage for production incidents affecting the Regional Flow Platform, Airport CDM Portal, Slot Allocation Engine, Departure Metering Service, Arrival Regulation Console, and Alert Distribution Hub. Standard service desk coverage for access requests, reporting questions, configuration inquiries, and minor usability issues shall be available during normal regional administrative hours on business days. During published peak traffic periods, Adverse Weather Mode, Capacity Reduction State, and Recovery Phase, the on call support posture shall be raised so that the System Support Engineer can join the Digital Briefing Room without delay. The Regional Flow Manager, Tower Supervisor, Ramp Control Lead, Airport Duty Manager, and Network Operations Liaison shall retain operational command authority during support activity. The System Support Engineer shall advise operational supervisors on technical status, known workarounds, data integrity concerns, and recovery options, but shall not issue traffic management instructions. Incidents shall be classified by operational effect, with the highest priority assigned to loss of common situational awareness, loss of slot computation, corruption of operational timing data, or failure of alert distribution during active traffic management. A critical incident shall be acknowledged within fifteen minutes and shall have continuous technical ownership until service is restored or an agreed operational workaround is in place. A high priority incident that degrades a major function without stopping tactical coordination shall be acknowledged within thirty minutes and shall be reviewed with the affected operational supervisor at least hourly. Medium and low priority incidents shall be handled through the service queue, with target updates provided at intervals appropriate to their operational impact and planned resolution path. The System Support Engineer shall maintain an incident record that captures the initial symptom, affected components, operational state, timeline, mitigation actions, data corrections, and supervisor decisions. Incident records shall reference the Audit Event Ledger where user actions, configuration changes, interface events, or automated decisions are relevant to the investigation. The Collaborative Decision Log shall be used to preserve the operational context of major incidents when traffic management decisions are made while a technical degradation is present. If the Regional Flow Platform cannot support safe collaborative operation, the Regional Flow Manager may direct transition to Contingency Fallback State using approved local procedures. The System Support Engineer shall verify that fallback tools, exported operational data, and restoration checkpoints are available before advising that normal technical service can resume. Post incident review shall be required for critical incidents, repeated high priority incidents, security related events, and failures that occur during capacity constrained operations. The review shall identify technical root cause, operational contributing factors, effectiveness of workarounds, user communication quality, and corrective actions assigned to named owners. Planned change windows shall normally be scheduled during low demand night periods and shall be coordinated with the Regional Flow Manager, Airport Duty Manager, Tower Supervisor, and affected Aircraft Operator Representative groups before approval. Changes that modify the Business Rules Registry, Identity Access Service, Time Synchronization Service, Flight Data Exchange, or operational metering behavior shall require additional validation in a representative test environment before production release. Emergency changes may be applied outside the standard window when needed to restore service, protect cyber security, or prevent material operational harm, but they shall be documented and retrospectively approved. Service level targets shall include monthly availability for critical production functions, incident response time, restoration time, data feed timeliness, and successful completion of scheduled operational reports. Availability targets shall exclude approved maintenance windows, customer network outages outside the managed boundary, and periods when operational supervisors have directed use of contingency procedures for nontechnical reasons. Performance against service targets shall be reviewed with the Regional Flow Manager and Data Governance Steward so that service quality, operational benefit, data quality, and user confidence are managed as a single operating obligation.

## 39. Future Enhancements and Regional Expansion

Future enhancement of the Regional Flow Platform should be treated as an incremental extension of the operational service rather than as a replacement of the current concept. The first expansion path is the exchange of cross-border flow constraints, demand forecasts, and agreed measures with adjacent regional systems under defined governance arrangements. This capability would allow the Regional Flow Manager and Network Operations Liaison to see upstream demand pressure earlier and to negotiate measures before local capacity is saturated. Cross-border exchange should use standardized message structures where practical, but the Business Rules Registry should retain the local parameters needed for Meridian operations. The Flight Data Exchange should be enhanced to accept richer intent data, including improved route options, expected cruise profiles, and updated departure readiness information. The Demand Prediction Service should use this data to create more stable demand pictures across longer planning horizons while still preserving tactical responsiveness. Trajectory prediction should also make greater use of weather features, sector configuration, runway direction, and surface movement status when estimating flight progress. The Weather Ingest Gateway and Sector Load Monitor should provide the common inputs needed to connect airspace capacity and airport capacity in the same prediction cycle. The Capacity Assessment Module should evolve from a mainly rules-based assessment into a service that can compare planned capacity, observed throughput, and predicted constraint persistence. Any use of learning models should be introduced with explainability requirements so that operational users can understand why a capacity or delay estimate has changed. Advanced turnaround prediction should combine aircraft state, handling milestones, stand occupancy, crew readiness, passenger processing, and ramp congestion signals. The Airport CDM Portal, Airline Interface Adapter, Handling Resource Planner, and Ramp Movement Board should provide the main operational inputs for this capability. Predicted changes to Target Off Block Time should be presented with confidence levels rather than as unconditional system corrections. The Slot Allocation Engine should use improved turnaround predictions to protect feasible Calculated Takeoff Time assignments and to reduce avoidable re-sequencing. The Tower Sequence Manager and Departure Metering Service should receive only the information needed for tactical sequencing, with detailed airline operational data controlled by policy. Machine-assisted recovery options should support the Recovery Phase by proposing coordinated actions that balance runway throughput, sector loading, stand availability, and airline priorities. These options should be generated by the Contingency Operations Console and reviewed through the Common Situation Display before any operational decision is applied. The system should not automatically impose a Ground Delay Program, change an Airport Acceptance Rate, or alter a tactical measure without authorized human confirmation. Recovery recommendations should include the operational rationale, expected benefit, key assumptions, and possible secondary effects on connected flights and surface movements. The Collaborative Decision Log and Audit Event Ledger should record each proposed option, the user response, and the final decision so that performance can be reviewed after operations. Regional expansion should include a repeatable onboarding pattern for new airports, airspace sectors, airlines, and handling organizations. The Identity Access Service, Time Synchronization Service, and Performance Analytics Warehouse should be scaled early because they form shared foundations for a wider regional deployment. Data governance should remain under the Data Governance Steward, with clear rules for retention, consent, quality thresholds, and permitted operational use. Future releases should be validated in shadow mode before operational activation so that the Regional Flow Manager, Airline Operations Coordinator, Tower Supervisor, and Airport Duty Manager can compare recommendations with actual outcomes.

## 40. Summary of Operating Commitments

Sustained regional ATFM and A-CDM operation requires each participating organization to treat the operating picture as a shared operational asset rather than a local advisory product. The Regional Flow Manager remains accountable for regional demand and capacity balancing and has the decision right to activate, amend, or terminate regional flow measures. The Airport Duty Manager remains accountable for airport operating capability and has the decision right to confirm capacity assumptions used for airport-level coordination. The Tower Supervisor retains authority for air traffic control safety decisions and runway sequencing, with automation used only to support judgment and workload management. The Slot Coordinator owns slot fairness, slot compliance monitoring, and exception handling under the approved business rules. The Airline Operations Coordinator and Aircraft Operator Representative are committed to providing operationally realistic airline intentions and to honoring agreed times unless a safety, security, or serviceability issue prevents compliance. The Ramp Control Lead and Ground Handling Dispatcher are committed to maintaining accurate surface and handling status so that departure metering is based on feasible ground operations. The Meteorology Briefing Officer, Airspace Sector Planner, and Military Airspace Liaison are committed to timely disclosure of weather, sector, and airspace constraints that may affect regional capacity. The Emergency Response Coordinator has the decision right to require protective operating measures when emergency conditions affect airport or regional flow priorities. The Deicing Coordinator has the decision right to confirm deicing constraints that affect achievable departure throughput during winter operations. The Data Governance Steward is accountable for data quality rules, retention policy, access controls, and permitted operational use of shared data. The System Support Engineer is accountable for technical availability, fault response, configuration control, and coordinated restoration of the operational services. The Regional Flow Platform is the primary operational environment for the common regional plan, while the Airport CDM Portal is the primary airport coordination environment. The Demand Prediction Service, Capacity Assessment Module, and Sector Load Monitor provide planning evidence, but accountable human authorities remain responsible for operational decisions. The Common Situation Display is the shared baseline for time-critical coordination and should be kept consistent with the Regional Flow Platform and Airport CDM Portal. The Collaborative Decision Log is the authoritative record of significant decisions, objections, approvals, and operational rationale during coordinated operations. Calculated Takeoff Time and Target Off Block Time commitments should be treated as executable operating constraints unless they are revised through the agreed coordination process. Ground Delay Program decisions, Tactical Regulation Proposal review, and Airport Acceptance Rate changes should be visible to affected participants before they are applied whenever operational time permits. Information-sharing duties include timely maintenance of the Flight Intent Message, Airport Capacity Plan, and other agreed planning data needed for demand and capacity balancing. The Flight Data Exchange, Airline Interface Adapter, Surface Surveillance Adapter, and Weather Ingest Gateway must be operated with monitored data quality thresholds and clear fallback procedures. The Slot Allocation Engine, Departure Metering Service, Arrival Regulation Console, Tower Sequence Manager, and Ramp Movement Board must use the approved rules recorded in the Business Rules Registry. The Identity Access Service and Audit Event Ledger must support traceability, least-privilege access, and after-action accountability for all operationally significant changes. The Performance Analytics Warehouse and Post Operations Report should be used to measure compliance, delay outcomes, capacity use, and the effectiveness of collaborative decisions. Governance should favor transparent decision making, timely challenge by affected parties, documented resolution of disputes, and continuous improvement through training, exercises, and post-operations review.
