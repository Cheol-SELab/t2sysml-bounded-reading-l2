# Operational Concept for the Alder Creek Electrical Distribution Substation Automation System

## 1. Substation Mission and Automation Boundary

The Alder Creek Electrical Distribution Substation supplies distribution circuits through transformer and feeder bays. The station automation boundary covers bay-level monitoring and control, station coordination, and the interfaces to external operating personnel and systems. Primary equipment, protection functions, and field wiring remain subject to their approved designs and operating limits. The Bay Automation Controller is assigned the project codename Juniper. The Bay Automation Controller performs bay-level sequencing, status evaluation, and control permissive checks. The Bay Automation Controller produces a Bay Status Digest for operational awareness. North Bay Controller and South Bay Controller provide bay-specific automation for their respective portions of the station. The Feeder Protection Relay detects feeder faults and initiates protection actions within its configured protection zone. The Feeder Protection Relay produces a Relay Diagnostic Note when its self-monitoring identifies a condition requiring engineering attention. Protection trips remain independent of routine bay-control commands so that a control-system failure does not inhibit fault clearing. The Breaker Control Unit executes authorized breaker operations and enforces its local electrical and mechanical interlocks. The Bay Automation Controller requests breaker operations only when the applicable operating mode and permissives allow them. Local control takes precedence when equipment is selected for local operation. Remote commands are subject to station authority, bay selection, interlocking, and equipment availability. The Station Supervisory Control System provides the station-level operating interface and supervisory coordination. The Station Gateway defines the communications boundary for authorized connections beyond the station automation network. The Satellite Time Receiver provides the station with a reference for time synchronization. The Sequence Event Archive retains operational event information for later review. The Engineering Workstation supports authorized configuration and maintenance activities under controlled access. The Maintenance Remote Terminal is limited to approved maintenance access and does not replace local operating authority. The Station DC System supplies control power to automation and protection equipment within its design capacity. The Control Room Operator directs routine station operation and verifies that commanded equipment states are appropriate. The Protection Relay Engineer maintains protection settings and evaluates relay performance within the approved change process. The Substation Field Crew performs field checks and equipment work under the applicable switching instructions and clearances.

## 2. Supervisory Architecture and Time Services

The supervisory architecture separates station-wide observation from local bay and protection functions. The Station Supervisory Control System is named Glasswing in this document. The Station Supervisory Control System presents available station and bay indications through a consolidated operator interface. Its displays show operating conditions and communication quality without replacing local equipment indications. The Station Gateway carries supervisory communications between station-level functions and remote operations interfaces. Supervisory visibility depends on the availability and quality of the underlying communications and status inputs. The supervisory layer does not perform the protective functions assigned to dedicated protection equipment. The Control Room Operator uses the supervisory displays to assess station conditions and coordinate operating actions. Any supervisory command remains subject to configured permissions and the applicable operating procedure. Local interlocks and equipment-level control logic remain authoritative for permitted switching actions. A communications interruption can leave displayed information stale or temporarily unavailable. After communications recover, operators confirm that indications are current before relying on them for decisions. The Satellite Time Receiver is named Bluefin in this document. The Satellite Time Receiver acquires a reference from supported satellite timing signals. It distributes time to station equipment over the configured time-distribution network. Time distribution supports consistent event timestamps across equipment that receives and applies the reference. Accurate timestamps assist event correlation but do not establish the validity of an electrical measurement or protection decision. If the external timing reference is lost, equipment behavior depends on its individual clock and holdover capabilities. Holdover performance varies by device and may degrade as the interval without a valid reference increases. The Satellite Time Receiver produces the Clock Drift Snapshot when its measured offset meets the configured reporting condition. Time-quality indications allow operators to distinguish synchronized operation from degraded or unavailable timing. The Station DC System supplies operating power to station automation equipment within its designed capacity. A loss of station power can interrupt supervisory visibility and time distribution until supported supplies are restored. The Station Supervisory Control System produces the Alarm Acknowledgement Slip for supervisory recordkeeping. The Substation Supervisor oversees operational use of the supervisory system and escalation of unresolved availability concerns.

## 3. Protection and Engineering Configuration

The Feeder Protection Relay is known as Tandem in this concept. The Protection Relay Engineer is known as Larkspur in this concept. The Feeder Protection Relay provides local protection for its assigned feeder and operates independently of supervisory control availability. Its approved setting group defines the pickup levels, time delays, and logic required for the feeder protection scheme. The Protection Relay Engineer develops setting changes against the approved protection study and the current equipment data. The Protection Relay Engineer documents the engineering basis and expected operational effect for each proposed change. Engineering Workstation maintains the working configuration under revision control. Engineering Workstation produces the Settings Review Receipt after a settings review is completed. Each proposed change receives a unique revision identifier before review begins. The Protection Relay Engineer verifies that proposed values agree with the approved coordination objectives. A second qualified reviewer independently checks calculations, logic changes, and device applicability. The review confirms that the proposed configuration is complete, internally consistent, and suitable for the intended feeder. Coordination checks consider upstream and downstream protection behavior across the expected operating range. Protection settings are not approved solely on the basis of copied values from another installation. Engineering Workstation retains the approved configuration and its revision history for controlled access. Configuration access is limited to personnel with assigned engineering responsibilities. The Protection Relay Engineer records the approval status before any setting change is released for implementation. Before deployment, the approved configuration is checked against the intended device model and firmware version. A controlled outage or other approved work window is required when implementation could affect feeder protection. The Substation Field Crew follows the authorized work instructions during device access and setting transfer. After installation, the implemented values are compared with the approved configuration. The Feeder Protection Relay is functionally checked to confirm the intended protection behavior after a change. Any discrepancy stops release of the configuration until its cause is resolved and reviewed. The Substation Supervisor ensures that engineering changes follow the established authorization and operational control process.

## 4. Field Work and Safe Access

The Substation Field Crew is known operationally as Cinder during field activities. Cinder performs inspections, maintenance, and switching support within the boundaries of approved work. Field access begins only after the required work authorization and site entry controls are confirmed. The Control Room Operator coordinates operational access with the Switching Desk Coordinator before work that could affect energized equipment. The Substation Supervisor confirms that the requested work is consistent with current operating conditions and approved procedures. The crew reviews the work scope, access route, known hazards, and communication arrangements before entering the yard. Personnel use the site access process to record arrival and departure so that the control room can account for field staff. Work boundaries are established before tools or test equipment are brought near electrical apparatus. The Switching Desk Coordinator confirms the applicable switching and clearance arrangements before work begins. The Control Room Operator retains authority to suspend work when operating conditions change or indications become uncertain. The Substation Field Crew verifies equipment identity at the point of work against the approved work documentation. Field personnel do not rely on screen indications alone to establish that equipment is safe to approach or handle. Required checks of position, isolation, and absence of hazardous energy are performed using approved site procedures. Any discrepancy between field observations and control-room indications is treated as an unsafe condition until resolved. The crew promptly reports uncertain equipment conditions through the designated operational communication channel. Substation Field Crew completes and sends the Breaker Inspection Tag after recording the observed breaker condition and inspection time. The Station Supervisory Control System presents available equipment indications for operator review during normal operation. The Bay Automation Controller applies configured permissives and interlocks to supported automated operations. Automation does not replace field verification when a procedure requires direct confirmation of equipment condition. The Control Room Operator evaluates reported field conditions before authorizing a return to automated operation. Field crews maintain communications with the control room throughout work that could affect protection, control, or service continuity. If communications are lost, the crew stops affected work and follows the approved hold and escalation procedure. Work is considered complete only after personnel, tools, and temporary connections are accounted for and the work area is left in a safe state. The Switching Desk Coordinator confirms the operational handback arrangements before equipment is returned to service.

## 5. Event Evidence and Operational Records

The Sequence Event Archive is the station's authoritative repository for time-stamped operational event evidence. The Sequence Event Archive is known operationally as Orchard. It preserves event records in their received form so later review does not depend on transient operator displays. Each retained record includes its source identity, event time, archive receipt time, and available quality indications. The archive preserves original event ordering and marks records whose time quality or sequence continuity is uncertain. The Satellite Time Receiver supports station time alignment, and loss of its reference is treated as a condition affecting interpretation rather than a reason to discard evidence. Events with corrected or estimated times remain distinguishable from records with valid time quality. The Sequence Event Archive retains configuration and access history associated with archive administration. The Station Supervisory Control System presents current operating indications separately from the long-term evidence held by the archive. The Control Room Operator reviews archived event sequences to establish the order of alarms, status changes, and operator actions. The Protection Relay Engineer compares event times and relay indications during disturbance analysis. The Protection Relay Engineer uses the Relay Diagnostic Note during fault analysis. The Engineering Workstation provides approved tools for searching, filtering, and exporting event records. Review queries should be bounded by station, equipment identifier, and time interval to keep results reproducible. Exported evidence retains original timestamps and quality flags, and any derived ordering is identified as an engineering interpretation. Engineering conclusions are recorded separately from the preserved source evidence. Access to archive functions is limited to authorized roles, and administrative changes are auditable. The archive retains records under the station records schedule and applicable utility retention requirements. Records selected for an investigation are protected from routine deletion until the hold is released by the responsible authority. Archive health is checked for storage capacity, ingestion continuity, and recoverability. Recovery testing verifies that retained records remain readable and that timestamps and quality indications survive restoration. The station preserves an additional protected copy of essential event evidence in accordance with approved backup procedures. The Station DC System produces a Battery Load Test Record for retention with station engineering records. Juniper timestamps locally generated state changes for later correlation. After the Switching Desk Coordinator confirms operational handback, evidence from the completed work remains available for authorized engineering review.

## 6. Switching Coordination and Bay Boundaries

The Switching Desk Coordinator is known operationally as Keystone. The Switching Desk Coordinator maintains the station-wide switching plan and coordinates work across the two bay boundaries. The Control Room Operator submits switching requests with the affected equipment, intended state changes, and requested timing. The Switching Desk Coordinator checks each request for conflicts with active clearances and other approved work. The Substation Supervisor resolves priority conflicts and confirms the operational authority for exceptional switching conditions. Approved switching sequences identify the required order of actions, hold points, and conditions for stopping work. The North Bay Controller manages control and interlocking functions assigned to the north bay under the approved sequence. The North Bay Controller blocks a close command when its local interlocks indicate that the bay is not ready. The South Bay Controller manages the corresponding control and interlocking functions assigned to the south bay. The two controllers have separate equipment scopes and do not substitute for one another when executing a switching sequence. The bay boundary defines which controller may operate each device and which indications are used to confirm its state. Equipment shared across the boundary requires explicit coordination before a switching action can affect either bay. The Bay Automation Controller presents bay control status while preserving the separate operating scopes of the two controllers. The Breaker Control Unit executes permitted breaker commands only when the applicable control conditions are satisfied. The North Protection Panel supports protection functions for the north-side equipment within its assigned scope. The Transformer Protection Panel retains its protection authority independently of the bay switching sequence. The Station Supervisory Control System uses the status digest to review current station operating conditions. The Sequence Event Archive receives the Clock Drift Snapshot for event-time correlation. The Switching Desk Coordinator produces the Isolation Boundary Card for the planned work. The Station Gateway produces the Protocol Retry Summary for communication review. Larkspur reviews protection coordination questions that could affect the planned switching sequence. Bluefin provides the station time reference used to align recorded event times. The Substation Field Crew confirms device identity and local conditions before carrying out an authorized operation. After the Switching Desk Coordinator confirms operational handback, state-change evidence remains available for authorized engineering review.

## 7. Alarms, Operator Response, and Protection Panels

Alarm indications are assessed according to protection significance, equipment condition, and the potential effect on service. The Control Room Operator reviews the alarm priority, source, time, and associated bay indications before deciding on a response. The operator distinguishes a new alarm from a standing condition and checks whether related indications support the same assessment. For a protection operation or an unexplained equipment state, the Control Room Operator treats the condition as requiring prompt investigation. The Control Room Operator acknowledges alarms only after reviewing the indication and retains awareness of any condition that remains unresolved. The Control Room Operator uses the acknowledgement slip to record the alarm response and any outstanding action. The operator checks available device status and sequence information without treating a cleared indication as proof that the underlying cause has been corrected. The Feeder Protection Relay indication is reviewed against the affected feeder status and any related protection operation. The Bay Automation Controller provides bay-level status indications that support the operator’s assessment of the alarm. Glasswing shall retain alarm state and event information for operator review after an indication has cleared. The Sequence Event Archive provides time-ordered evidence for later operational review. The North Protection Panel contains the protection equipment associated with feeder protection functions. The Control Room Operator uses indications from the North Protection Panel to assess feeder protection operations and related alarms. The Transformer Protection Panel is dedicated to protection functions associated with the power transformer rather than feeder protection. The Transformer Protection Panel provides local indication of transformer protection operation for assessment during an alarm response. The Control Room Operator considers the condition of the Station DC System when alarms or protection indications suggest a possible control-power concern. A breaker status discrepancy is treated as an abnormal condition until its cause and operational significance are understood. The Engineering Workstation is used for authorized review of protection and event information, not as a substitute for immediate operational assessment. The Protection Relay Engineer supports investigation when relay indications, settings concerns, or event evidence require technical interpretation. The Substation Field Crew confirms device identity and local conditions before carrying out an authorized operation. The Substation Supervisor is consulted when the alarm response requires escalation beyond routine operator actions. The Control Room Operator produces the Clearance Handoff Memo when an unresolved alarm condition requires a formal handoff. The Switching Desk Coordinator confirms operational handback before the associated state-change evidence is considered available for authorized engineering review. After handback, the Control Room Operator verifies that alarm status, equipment status, and recorded evidence are consistent before closing the response.

## 8. Communications, Control, and Equipment Feedback

The station communications design separates supervisory requests, bay-level execution, and protection action so loss of one layer does not silently substitute for another. Station Gateway brokers authorized station traffic across control-room and bay networks, applying configured protocol mappings, addressing, and access controls. It rejects malformed, stale, or unauthorized requests and records each rejection with enough context for operations review. Station Supervisory Control System presents available controls and indications to authorized operating personnel. Control Room Operator issues state-changing requests only through approved operating procedures and the configured supervisory interface. Bay Automation Controller validates each received request against local mode, interlocks, command authority, and equipment availability before issuing bay-level outputs. Breaker Control Unit applies accepted output commands at the breaker interface and returns the resulting position and circuit-health indications. Bay Automation Controller compares returned indications with the requested state and identifies incomplete or contradictory operation. Bay Automation Controller uses the inspection tag to associate the applicable equipment context with an authorized record. Station Gateway carries updated indications through the station interface using configured quality and timestamp attributes. Station Supervisory Control System displays indication quality and stale-data status so operators can distinguish a current value from a retained value. Sequence Event Archive retains time-tagged changes and control outcomes for event reconstruction and operational review. Satellite Time Receiver supplies the station time reference used to align event timestamps, subject to configured quality checks. Bay Automation Controller monitors communication health and places affected points in a defined degraded state when updates exceed configured age limits. Station Gateway reports link condition and protocol errors without converting a communication failure into an equipment-state change. Tandem evaluates electrical quantities against approved settings and issues protective trip outputs without relying on supervisory communications. North Protection Panel applies its assigned zone logic and maintains trip paths independently of supervisory network availability. Transformer Protection Panel applies its assigned protective logic and reports abnormal protection conditions through its configured interface. North Bay Controller maintains local bay control within the approved operating mode when supervisory communications are interrupted. South Bay Controller reports local mode and control availability through its configured bay interface. Engineering Workstation supports approved configuration review and does not serve as a routine source of operating commands. Maintenance Remote Terminal access is restricted to authorized maintenance sessions and is not used to bypass local interlocks. Station DC System supports control and protection functions through monitored station supply circuits, and supply loss is indicated as an abnormal station condition. Protection Relay Engineer reviews protection event records and verifies that approved settings remain consistent with coordination requirements. Substation Supervisor ensures communication changes, point mapping changes, and control logic changes are managed through station change control. Substation Field Crew verifies equipment indications locally when remote status is uncertain and reports discrepancies through established operating channels.

## 9. Maintenance, Testing, and Degraded Operation

Maintenance windows are planned around feeder loading, switching constraints, and the availability of qualified personnel. The Substation Supervisor coordinates work timing with the Switching Desk Coordinator and confirms that operating responsibilities are understood. The Control Room Operator reviews the equipment affected by each work package and identifies any required operating restrictions. The Switching Desk Coordinator confirms switching prerequisites before field work begins and keeps the approved switching sequence current. Substation Field Crew performs work only after required clearances, isolation, and grounding have been confirmed through established procedures. Work boundaries are verified locally before personnel approach exposed conductors or operate equipment under maintenance. The Protection Relay Engineer reviews test scope, approved settings, and restoration criteria before protection testing starts. Testing is performed with approved procedures, calibrated instruments, and a defined means of stopping the test if unexpected indications occur. The Engineering Workstation is used for authorized configuration review, and changes are applied only through station change control. The Maintenance Remote Terminal uses the review receipt to support controlled maintenance review. The Bay Automation Controller uses the load test record to support maintenance review. The Station Supervisory Control System receives the Protocol Retry Summary for operational review. Cinder verifies that field work remains within the approved work boundary and reports any unsafe condition through established channels. Orchard retains event information needed to review testing and restoration activity. Feeder Protection Relay testing is coordinated so that protection coverage is not unintentionally removed from an energized feeder. A protection function placed out of service is tracked by the responsible operator until it is restored and verified. The Breaker Control Unit is tested using controlled commands that cannot bypass local interlocks or approved operating authority. Remote commands are inhibited when equipment status is uncertain or when required permissives are unavailable. If communications are degraded, the Control Room Operator treats remote indications as unavailable until their reliability is established. Substation Field Crew verifies equipment indications locally when remote status is uncertain and reports discrepancies through established operating channels. Manual operation is permitted only under approved procedures and with the required local checks and communication with the Control Room Operator. A suspected controller or relay fault is assessed before any restart, bypass, or replacement is attempted. Restoration includes confirming normal indications, protection availability, communications status, and the removal of temporary test connections. The Substation Supervisor confirms that required records are complete and that operating restrictions are formally cleared before returning equipment to normal service.

## 10. Operating Assurance and Handover

Operational assurance at Alder Creek depends on clear authority, verified equipment state, and records that support later review. The Control Room Operator remains responsible for deciding whether an automation request is appropriate for current operating conditions. The Bay Automation Controller shall execute only commands permitted by its approved logic and configured interlocks. The Feeder Protection Relay shall retain its protective function independently of supervisory communications. The Protection Relay Engineer reviews protection behavior after material changes and records any unresolved concerns for operational follow-up. The Station Supervisory Control System presents control indications and command results for operator assessment. The Sequence Event Archive retains time-ordered records needed to reconstruct significant control and protection events. The Engineering Workstation shall be used for authorized configuration work under established change control. The Satellite Time Receiver supports consistent event timing, and time-quality concerns shall be investigated before records are relied upon for sequence analysis. The Station Gateway shall maintain the defined boundary between station automation and external communications. The Station DC System shall be confirmed available before switching that depends on station control power. The Breaker Control Unit shall reject commands that do not satisfy its configured permissive conditions. Substation Field Crew uses the Isolation Boundary Card to verify the approved work boundary before entering the controlled area. Substation Field Crew reports unexpected equipment conditions promptly and pauses work when the approved boundary is uncertain. Switching Desk Coordinator receives the Clearance Handoff Memo and confirms that its clearance status agrees with the active switching record. Keystone shall confirm that switching authority, equipment state, and communication limits are understood before coordinating any automation-enabled action. The Substation Supervisor verifies that unresolved risks have an assigned owner and an agreed disposition before normal service is resumed. The Control Room Operator confirms indications locally when remote status is incomplete, inconsistent, or delayed. The Protection Relay Engineer assesses unexplained trips and abnormal protection indications before protection is returned to service. The Maintenance Remote Terminal shall use controlled access, and each maintenance session shall be attributable to an authorized user. The North Bay Controller shall be checked for expected local behavior after approved configuration work. The South Bay Controller shall be checked for expected local behavior after approved configuration work. The North Protection Panel shall remain available for independent protection functions during supervisory control interruptions. The Transformer Protection Panel shall remain within its approved settings and operating constraints during handover. Periodic operational reviews shall examine command outcomes, event records, exceptions, and corrective actions so that automation decisions remain traceable and safe.
